Quit pouches

How to Quit Nicotine Pouches

Nicotine pouches are discreet, smoke-free and easy to use all day, which is exactly why they're hard to give up. Whether you use Zyn, Velo, On! or another brand, the plan is the same: understand your use, step down on purpose, and have a plan for cravings.

Why pouches are hard to quit

  • Constant use. Because no one can see them, it's easy to have a pouch in almost all day.
  • High strengths. Pouch strengths vary widely, and some deliver a lot of nicotine.
  • Habit loops. A pouch after meals, at your desk or during a workout quickly becomes automatic.

Step 1: Track your real use

For a few days, note how many pouches you use, the strength in milligrams (mg), and how long you keep each one in. Many people are surprised by the total. These numbers become the starting point for your taper.

Step 2: Taper strength and count

Stepping down gradually gives your body time to adjust. A simple four-week example:

WeekWhat to change
Week 1Keep your usual strength, cut the number of pouches by about a quarter. Add pouch-free windows, such as the first hour after waking.
Week 2Drop to the next lower strength and keep the lower count.
Week 3Halve your count again, or drop to the lowest strength. Shorten how long each pouch stays in.
Week 4Quit Day. Stop completely, with a plan for cravings in place.

This is an example, not a prescription. If you use a very high strength or a lot of pouches, a slower taper or nicotine replacement may suit you better. DayXero is being built to create a step-down schedule from your own numbers.

Step 3: Consider switching to licensed NRT

Nicotine gum and lozenges are used in a similar way to pouches, but they are licensed medicines with clear dosing and step-down instructions. Some people switch from pouches to NRT, then taper the NRT. A pharmacist can help you choose a strength.

Step 4: Or stop on a set Quit Day

Stopping all at once also works for many people, especially if your use is moderate. Set a Quit Day one to two weeks away, prepare a swap for each trigger (sugar-free gum, a mint, a drink of water), and remove every pouch from your bag, car and desk the night before.

Step 5: Plan for cravings and withdrawal

Pouch withdrawal is nicotine withdrawal: irritability, restlessness, poor focus, stronger appetite and cravings. It's usually strongest in the first few days and eases over two to four weeks. Each craving typically passes within a few minutes. See how to handle nicotine cravings and the withdrawal timeline.

Frequently asked questions

Are nicotine pouches safer than smoking?

Pouches don't involve smoke, so they avoid the tar and carbon monoxide of cigarettes. But nicotine is highly addictive, and the long-term health effects of pouches are still being studied. Being nicotine-free is the healthiest option.

How long does withdrawal from nicotine pouches last?

Symptoms usually peak in the first few days and ease over two to four weeks. Occasional cravings can continue for a few months but get weaker over time.

Can I quit Zyn cold turkey?

Yes. Many people stop all at once on a planned Quit Day. If you use high strengths or many pouches a day, tapering first or using nicotine gum or lozenges can make it more comfortable.

Is nicotine gum better than pouches for quitting?

Nicotine gum is a licensed stop-smoking medicine with set doses and instructions for stepping down, which makes it a useful tool for tapering off nicotine. Pouches aren't designed for that.
