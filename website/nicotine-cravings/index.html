Cravings

How to Handle Nicotine Cravings

A nicotine craving can feel like it will last forever, but it doesn't. Most cravings rise, peak and fade within a few minutes. The goal isn't to fight them with willpower. It's to get through those minutes without reaching for a cigarette, vape or pouch.

Cravings behave like waves

A craving builds, peaks and then falls away on its own, whether or not you give in. Every time you ride one out, the link between the trigger and nicotine gets a little weaker. Over the weeks after quitting, cravings come less often and feel less intense.

Urge surfing, step by step

Urge surfing is a mindfulness technique used in addiction treatment. Instead of fighting the craving, you watch it pass:

  1. Notice it. Say to yourself, "This is a craving. It will pass."
  2. Find it in your body. Tight chest, restless hands, a pull in your throat. Just notice where it is.
  3. Breathe slowly. Breathe in for about four seconds and out for about six. A longer out-breath helps you calm down.
  4. Ride the wave. Notice the craving getting stronger, peaking, and then starting to fade.
  5. Choose what's next. Once it's passed, carry on with what you were doing.

Quick techniques that work

  • Delay: tell yourself you'll wait ten minutes. The craving often passes first.
  • Move: a short walk or a few minutes of activity can reduce cravings.
  • Drink water or chew sugar-free gum to keep your mouth busy.
  • Change your surroundings: leave the room or step outside somewhere new.
  • Text someone who knows you're quitting.

A plan for your biggest triggers

TriggerTry this instead
Waking upGet up straight away, drink a glass of water and change your morning order.
CoffeeSwitch the place or the cup, or have tea for the first weeks.
After mealsGet up from the table, brush your teeth or go for a short walk.
StressThree slow breaths with a long out-breath, then urge surf.
AlcoholDrink less in the first weeks and plan what you'll say if offered one.
DrivingKeep gum or water in the car and change your playlist.

Fast-acting nicotine replacement

If you use nicotine replacement, gum, lozenges or mouth spray can take the edge off breakthrough cravings, especially combined with a patch. Follow the product instructions or ask a pharmacist.

If you give in

A slip doesn't mean you've failed. Note what set it off, plan for that trigger next time, and carry on. Thinking "I've ruined it" is what turns a slip into a full relapse, not the slip itself.

Frequently asked questions

How long do nicotine cravings last?

A single craving usually peaks and passes within a few minutes. Over the weeks after quitting, cravings become less frequent and less intense.

Do nicotine cravings ever go away completely?

For most people, yes. After a few months cravings become rare. Occasional ones can appear around old triggers, but they're much weaker and easier to let pass.

Does exercise help with nicotine cravings?

Yes. Even a short burst of activity, like a brisk walk, can reduce cravings and withdrawal symptoms.

What is urge surfing?

Urge surfing is a mindfulness technique where you notice a craving, feel where it is in your body and breathe through it as it rises and falls, instead of fighting it or giving in.
