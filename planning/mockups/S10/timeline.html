Health timeline

What changes in the body after stopping, counted from Quit Day, one timeline per product. Every item cites its source and opens it, and the wording is kept conservative ("risk drops", "usually", "for most people"). Where the evidence is thin, as for vaping and pouches over the long term, the timeline says so instead of guessing. Content is stored as data so it can be corrected without an app update. Example: Milan on Day 12, vapes and smokes.

Draft content and sources

Smoking (American Cancer Society, "Benefits of quitting smoking over time"): minutes, heart rate and blood pressure start to come down · a few days, carbon monoxide in the blood drops to normal · 2 weeks to 3 months, circulation and lung function improve · 1 to 12 months, coughing and shortness of breath ease · 1 to 2 years, heart attack risk drops sharply · 5 to 10 years, risk of mouth, throat and voice-box cancer is about halved and stroke risk falls · 10 years, lung cancer risk is about half that of someone who still smokes · 15 years, heart disease risk is close to that of a non-smoker.

All products (smokefree.gov; CDC): a few hours, withdrawal can start · first week, withdrawal is usually at its strongest · a few days, most nicotine and its by-product cotinine have left the body (cotinine's half-life is about 15–20 hours) · 1 to 2 weeks, mood changes from withdrawal usually ease · a few weeks, cravings come less often and feel weaker.

Vaping and pouches use the "all products" items plus a plain note: "Research on the long-term effects of vaping (or pouches) is still young, so this timeline sticks to what's known." Nothing about lungs or gums is claimed without a solid source.

Not medical advice. Each item's detail sheet ends with "Health information, not medical advice. Everyone's body is different."
