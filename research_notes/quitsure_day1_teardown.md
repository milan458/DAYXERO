# QuitSure Day 1: teardown

Source: three screen recordings of QuitSure Day 1, recorded 5 Nov 2026 (IMG_0028–0030). This is a reference for writing DayXero's prep days and Quit Day. **Don't copy their wording, images or numbers.** Take the structure and techniques, then write our own content.

## Shape of the day

QuitSure runs **6 days of prep while still smoking. The last cigarette is on Day 6, then 30 days of maintenance** (19 more sessions). It's the same model as ours: 7 prep days, then Quit Day.

Day 1 is called "A New Journey Begins". It's a list of **6 modules that unlock in order**. Each shows a time estimate and gets a green tick when done. Two **exercises** unlock after the modules.

| # | Module | Time | What it does |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | START HERE: Why Can't We Just Quit? | 5–7 min | You've tried before, and it's not your fault. You were trapped, you didn't choose this. It confronts denial with analogies to alcohol, heroin and gambling, then lists 5 "mind-tricks" (it hasn't harmed me / I don't smoke much / it has benefits / I'm in control / I can quit any time). A **control test**: imagine throwing every cigarette away right now and notice the panic. Ends with "2% vs 98%" and **"I commit to continue"**. |
| 2 | Is Smoking an Addiction? | 3–5 min | The rat-and-lever experiment: "every smoker became that rat". You've pressed the lever 200,000+ times, mostly on autopilot. A pack warning ("harder to quit than heroin or cocaine"). "It's not your fault, it's an engineered trap." Ends with "How to quit?" |
| 3 | The Path to Freedom | 8–10 min | 96% fail, like trying to escape a maze blindfolded. 4 stages: denial, understanding the trap, learning how to get out, training the mind to stay out. **"Don't quit today (yes, really)"**, which gives permission. Optional smoking tracker. "Your one job today." Why willpower runs out, with holding-your-breath and weights images. Every method (cold turkey, cutting down, switching to vaping) "leads to willpower". A first-person story of 30 failed attempts. Grim health detail. Ends with a recap and what's next. |

**Exercises after the modules**
- **Journal: "Why I want to quit".** List your 5 most important reasons. The answer is required, and they say they'll bring the reasons back later.
- **Video: "Quitting is enjoyable".** Affirmations over stock video ("All the situations in my life will be more enjoyable", "Most of my health issues will disappear"). **Watch it twice, at two separate times**, and mark it as watched (0/2). It's framed as rewiring the brain through repetition.
- The home screen has a **"Today, I smoked [0] − +"** counter and "Track your progress".

Total for Day 1: about **40–50 minutes of reading** plus the exercises.

## How it's built

- **One long scrolling article per module.** A thin segmented progress bar at the top, Back and ✕.
- **Colour-coded marker highlights**: yellow for key lines, red for danger, green for hope, blue for questions. Beige callout boxes hold the takeaway. A bold line closes each section.
- **AI-made comic pictures**, mostly two-panel contrasts: blind struggle vs clear understanding, willpower path vs QuitSure path, thinking about quitting vs panic.
- **The bottom button is held on "PLEASE WAIT…" on a timer** so you can't skip the reading. Then it becomes a commitment **in your own voice**: "I'm ready to see it", "Let's understand", "I'm listening", "I've lived this", "This makes sense", "I'm in", "Trusting the process", "This time is different!".
- **"How was this module?"** pops up after every module, with 5 faces from red to green.
- The voice talks straight to you, with rhetorical questions and pauses: "Take 15 seconds. Really think about it." Short one-line paragraphs, a lot of repetition.

## The persuasion toolkit (worth knowing)

1. **Takes the blame away:** "it's not your fault, it's a trap", "not weak, trapped".
2. **Confronts denial**, using other addictions to make it obvious.
3. **Names the mind-tricks** you tell yourself.
4. **A control test**: imagine quitting now, notice how strongly you react.
5. **Permission against expectation:** don't quit today.
6. **Willpower is the enemy**, so every past failure was the method's fault.
7. **Social proof and authority**: client counts, named testimonials, "WHO", "clinical study".
8. **Tells you what's coming** (disgust by Day 4, excitement by Day 6), so it comes true.
9. **Small commitments** on every button, plus saying an affirmation out loud.
10. **Repetition** (watch twice) framed as neuroplasticity.
11. **Answers objections** with an FAQ, a guarantee and a cost comparison.

## What DayXero should borrow

- **A button in the user's own voice** at the end of each step ("I've lived this", "I'm listening"). It's cheap and works well with our cards and quizzes.
- **Named steps with times and ticks**, opening in order. That's our 5-step day.
- **Day 1 is about seeing the trap, not quitting.** We already have "You don't have to quit today".
- **The mind-tricks as quiz material.** A myth-or-fact deck (mockup D) or a fill-in quiz suits them well.
- **The control test as an ACT exercise.** Imagine throwing the pack away and notice the urge without acting on it. Name it ("there's the panic") and let it pass. That's our step 4.
- **Tell them what to expect** each day, honestly.
- **A recap at the end of the day**, and a moment that marks the day as done.
- **The reasons journal**, which we already have. Bring the reasons back on Quit Day and during cravings.
- **Repetition**: "listen once now, once tonight" on the audio, instead of their twice-watched video.
- **A light rating after each step** gives us content feedback.

## What DayXero should do differently

- **Far less reading.** 40–50 minutes of scrolling text is the main weakness. Our 5–7 minute steps with audio, cards and quizzes fix it. Make the step interactive (flip, answer, hold) instead of blocking the button with a timer.
- **Warmer than confrontational.** "You are in denial" and "they're lying to themselves" can push people away. Keep it direct, but with ACT-style empathy: the urge is a feeling, not an order.
- **Source every claim.** Their "95% success", "86.4% no withdrawal" and "100,000+ clients" can't be checked. Our rule stands: quote `health-timeline.json`, cite the study, and invent nothing.
- **Support NRT.** QuitSure says don't combine methods or use NRT, but NRT is backed by strong evidence (Cochrane). Our line: understanding removes the wanting, and NRT can take the edge off the physical side for some people.
- **No paywall inside the lesson.** Our prep is free and the offer comes after the Day Xero session (decisions 15, 23, MON-01). Keep selling out of the lessons.
- **Keep it calm on health.** Graphic harm copy ("tar", "starving your tissues") breaks our "the higher the stakes, the quieter the screen" rule.
- **No promises of zero withdrawal.** Set honest expectations and point to SOS.
