## Key question 1 — Onboarding: steps, order, value vs. data, the baseline summary, drop-off, and 2025–26 benchmarks

### Takeaway
For a dual user (Sam) onboarding is 14 screens / 13 counted steps: breathe → welcome → products → one baseline per product → baseline summary (first value reveal) → triggers → name+reason → past attempts → NRT → Quit Day → plan builds → plan reveal → reminders. That is shorter than the Health & Fitness median of ~20 screens, and the mid-flow "baseline summary" reveal is the right pattern; the real problems are (a) four required question screens stacked after the reveal with no skip anywhere, (b) the baseline summary's "Good news" line contradicts the product's own prep-week promise and is an unsourced health claim, (c) a progress model that says "12 steps" in the bar and "Step 6 of 6" on the reveal, and (d) a permission screen that promises "two nudges… That's it" while the app ships four daily notifications on by default.

- Benchmark (vendor, not peer-reviewed, small n): Lazyweb's July 2026 scan reports Health & Fitness apps run a median 20.5 onboarding screens (n=26 flows), about 2× the all-category median of 11; across 24 Health & Fitness quiz apps, question screens average 6.2 (median 4.5, max 21); only ~16% of ~751 apps run a personalisation quiz at all. — [Lazyweb: How many onboarding screens do Health & Fitness apps have?](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/health-fitness-onboarding-length) and [Lazyweb: how many quiz questions](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/how-many-quiz-questions-health-fitness-apps) (the domain could not be fetched from this session; figures are from the search summary). I found no published completion-rate benchmark for onboarding (see Gaps).
- Benchmark (competitors): Finch's onboarding is listed at 34 steps with an "Unlock Pro" paywall partway through the recording — [Lazyweb flow listing](https://experiments.lazyweb.com/flow/finch/onboarding), [Screensdesign Finch showcase](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/finch-self-care-pet); a Pratt design critique says Finch adds "a series of extra onboarding questions" after naming the pet and argues meeting the pet first would lower load — [Pratt IXD critique](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app-2/). Quittr's "Unlock Pro" paywall first appears about 31 s into the onboarding video — [Screensdesign Quittr](https://screensdesign.com/apps/quittr-quit-porn-now/?vs=201992). I Am Sober is listed with a 20-step onboarding — [Screensdesign I Am Sober](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/i-am-sober).
- NN/g (70 participants, four iPhone apps): tutorial readers rated tasks harder (ease 4.92 vs 5.49 on 1–7) with no gain in success (91% vs 94%) or time; NN/g's conclusion is that front-loaded instruction can make an app seem more complicated than it is. — [NN/g, Mobile tutorials](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/)

## Key question 6 — Benchmarks: what Smoke Free, Kwit, Quittr, QuitNow, EasyQuit, Puff Count / Quit Vaping, I Am Sober, Finch and Headspace do that DayXero should copy or avoid

### Takeaway
Where evidence exists, DayXero already avoids the three biggest competitor failure modes (paywall inside onboarding, streak-reset buttons, scolding after a slip) and copies the one strongest pattern (Finch's short daily checklist with a growing character). The gaps are the home-screen clutter Finch is criticised for, and the absence of a visible, kind restart path that I Am Sober users praise.

### Cited Findings
- Finch: 34-step onboarding; mid-onboarding paywall; "the primary loop is incredibly strong, but the home screen can get busy"; a Pratt critique argues meeting the pet before extra questions would lower cognitive load; the loop is "checking off tasks energizes the pet, which grows and goes on daily adventures". — [Lazyweb flow](https://experiments.lazyweb.com/flow/finch/onboarding) (vendor), [Screensdesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/finch-self-care-pet), [Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/x0hd2ssr80y5n7gv0w967pg7hwd7tl), [Pratt IXD](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2025/09/design-critique-finch-ios-app-2/)
- Quittr: "Unlock Pro" paywall first appears about 31 s into the onboarding; subscription "required for full access"; 2M+ users claimed. — [Screensdesign Quittr](https://screensdesign.com/apps/quittr-quit-porn-now/?vs=201992), [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id6532588521)
- I Am Sober: 20-step onboarding; reviewers praise a one-click reset with low-visibility placement and that the app "doesn't scold" after a slip; "Log Urge" is the in-the-moment tool. — [Screensdesign](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/i-am-sober), [App Store CZ reviews](https://apps.apple.com/cz/app/i-am-sober/id672904239)
- Kwit: shake-to-get-a-craving-card; reviewers criticise the "smoke with awareness" craving option and the slip-vs-relapse distinction; premium "NOT cheap". — [Justuseapp Kwit reviews](https://justuseapp.com/en/app/525441365/kwit-quit-smoking-and-vaping/reviews), [Kwit App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/%EB%8B%B4%EB%B0%B0%EB%A5%BC-%EB%81%8A%EC%9C%BC%EC%8B%AD%EC%8B%9C%EC%98%A4-kwit/id525441365?l=ko)
- Smoke Free: daily "Missions" based on CBT, "partly designed to be motivational and partly… to address the fundamental things which are known to cause people to return to smoking"; Missions require the pro upgrade. — [Scribd Smoke Free missions appendix](https://www.scribd.com/document/559169428/Smoke-Free-missions), [PMC study table](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7937918/table/T4), [HotDoc practice page](https://practices.hotdoc.com.au/?p=5584)
- Quit Vaping (Kopp): reviewer frustration at having to "hit the 'break your streak' button after a relapse". — [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/quit-vaping/id1479615245)
- Puff Count: main complaint "an aggressive paywall requiring a recurring weekly subscription" (AI-generated page, 54 reviews); a founder case study says the paywall was later removed — conflicting, low confidence. — [Marlvel](https://marlvel.ai/apps/puff-count-quit-vaping-now), [Starter Story](https://www.starterstory.com/puff-count-breakdown)
- DayXero's counterparts: trial offered on Quit Day not in onboarding (`onb-reveal`, `offer`); "Nothing resets" (`log-slip`, `log-menu`, `checkin-form`); prep week free (`onb-reveal`, `offer` "Always free: your counter and Craving SOS"); prep session "Use with your eyes open" (Prep 3) is the mindful-use idea placed before Quit Day only. — [onboarding.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/onboarding.js), [sos.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/sos.js), [program.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/program.js), [paywall.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/paywall.js)

### Inferences (copy / avoid)
1. **Copy from Finch**: the short daily checklist and the character that visibly changes with consistency — already present as "Your day" + Xero's sky/stage. **Avoid** Finch's busy home (cut `today` to five sections) and its mid-onboarding paywall (already avoided).
2. **Copy from I Am Sober**: a kind, one-tap "start again" that lives near the slip flow rather than in Settings (`log-slip-saved` tough state → "Start again from today?" using the `settings-quit-day` alert copy). **Avoid** making it prominent enough to feel like a punishment button.
3. **Avoid Kwit's** "smoke with awareness" after Quit Day — DayXero does, by keeping mindful-use to Prep 3; keep that boundary explicit in the Prep 3 copy ("This is for prep week only").
4. **Avoid Smoke Free's** paywalled daily missions — DayXero's free prep week is the differentiator; make sure the `paywall` first-run screen is not wired to fire after onboarding.
5. **Avoid Quit Vaping's** streak-break button and Puff Count's weekly plan — both already avoided; keep annual/monthly/lifetime and the exact-date trial copy.
