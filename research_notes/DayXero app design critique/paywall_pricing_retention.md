## KQ1. Paywall design: placement, offer structure, copy, legal text, restore, close, EU, failed/denied states, Apple 3.1 risk, and whether SOS should be free forever

### Takeaway
The paywall is a soft, value-first design with unusually honest copy (exact charge date, "$0 due today", two-tap cancel, refund route), a working free tier (counter, Craving SOS, all seven prep days and the Day Xero session), and restore/close/failed/pending states that are all drawn. The three material problems are: (1) the Screen Lab and the Swift code disagree about *when the trial is sold* — the lab's "Paywall · first run" sells "Start my 3-day free trial" on Fri 30 Oct, but `PaywallModel.load()` strips every plan with a trial before Quit Day, so the real first-run paywall offers only Monthly $9.99 and Lifetime $79.99 with a note that the trial "opens on your Quit Day"; (2) no Privacy Policy link exists anywhere in the app (`AppLinks.privacy` is nil), which breaks Apple's stated subscription-screen requirement and 5.1.1(i); (3) a 3-day trial started on Quit Day converts to a $39.99 charge on day 3–4 of withdrawal, the worst-feeling moment of the whole programme, and 3-day trials are the lowest-converting length in RevenueCat's data. There is no EU/DMA-specific state and none is needed while the app stays Apple-IAP-only.

**Apple rules (read first-hand from developer.apple.com)**

- Required on the sign-up screen: "Subscription name and duration, and the content or services provided during the subscription period; Full renewal price, shown clearly and prominently, and localized in available currencies; A way for current subscribers to sign in or restore purchases. Please note that your app and App Store metadata must include links to your Terms of Use and Privacy Policy." Also: "the amount that will be billed must be the most prominent pricing element in the layout … an annual subscription should clearly display the total amount … a breakdown price … should be displayed in a subordinate position and size to the annual price." Free trials: "clearly indicate how long the free trial lasts and the price billed once the free trial is over." "Customers can redeem one introductory offer per subscription group." Win-back offers "automatically appear to eligible customers within your app." Family Sharing "can't be undone." — [Apple, Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)
- Guideline 3.1.2(a): "the subscription period must last at least seven days and be available across all of the user's devices … those offering subscriptions should allow a user to get what they've paid for without performing additional tasks"; 3.1.2(c): "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price"; 3.1.1: "you should make sure you have a restore mechanism for any restorable in-app purchases"; 3.1.1(a): "In all other storefronts, except for the United States storefront … apps and their metadata may not include buttons, external links, or other calls to action that direct customers to purchasing mechanisms other than in-app purchase" (entitlements exist for "specific regions"); 5.1.1(i): "All apps must include a link to their privacy policy in the App Store Connect metadata field and within the app in an easily accessible manner"; 5.1.1(ii): "Apps that collect user or usage data must secure user consent for the collection, even if such data is considered to be anonymous … Paid functionality must not be dependent on or require a user to grant access to this data"; 1.4.1: "Apps should remind users to check with a doctor in addition to using the app and before making medical decisions." — [Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Developer-forum reports of repeated 3.1.2 rejections over missing/unclear Terms and Privacy links on the subscription screen — [Apple Developer Forums thread 812231](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/812231); [Apple Support Communities](https://discussions.apple.com/thread/256229268)

**Benchmarks on placement, trial length, close affordance**

- RevenueCat (vendor) 2025 report via Airbridge: hard paywalls median Day-35 install-to-paid ≈12.1% vs ≈2.2% freemium, ~2× year-one LTV ($49.30 vs $24.24), but higher refund rates (5.8% vs 3.4%); a 2026 RevenueCat figure cited elsewhere is ~10.7% vs 2.1% — [Airbridge summary](https://www.airbridge.io/blog/hard-vs-soft-paywalls); [PricePush glossary](https://pricepush.app/glossary/hard-paywall). A Product Hunt post attributes "hard paywalls convert 5x better than freemium, but with significantly wider variance" to the 2026 report (unverified) — [Product Hunt](https://www.producthunt.com/p/general/to-hard-paywall-or-not-that-is-the-question?page=3).
- Adapty (vendor) 2026: soft paywalls convert ~50% better than hard from paywall view to payment, but hard paywalls produce ~21% higher one-year LTV — [The Social Outline summarising Adapty 2026](https://thesocialoutline.com/blog/hard-paywall-vs-freemium-paid-acquisition).
- RevenueCat (vendor): trials of 17–32 days convert at a 42.5% median vs 25.5% for trials under 4 days; its own A/B test found a 14-day trial drew more starts than 7-day but fewer conversions overall — [RevenueCat, 7-day trial article (snippet)](https://www.revenuecat.com/blog/growth/7-day-trial-subscription-app). 2025 report (secondary): trials of 4 days or less had a 26.8% median conversion; Health & Fitness median trial-to-paid 39.9%, top 10% 68.3% — [Athletech News](https://athletechnews.com/fitness-apps-monetizable-winner-take-all-or-most/); [Gainframe](https://gainframe.app/blog/trial-conversion-21-to-44/). 2026 report: share of trials under 4 days rose from 42.1% (2025) to 46.5% (2026) — [RevenueCat 2026 (snippet)](https://www.revenuecat.com/state-of-subscription-apps).
- Lazyweb (July 2026, n=1,886 advertised paywalls): 7-day is the most common trial; 3-day is "roughly competitive with 7-day only in Education apps" — [Lazyweb trial-length research (snippet)](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/trial-length-3-vs-7-vs-14-day-which-to-pick). ### Inferences
- **The prototype and the code must be reconciled before launch.** Either (a) the first-run paywall sells the annual trial immediately (lab version), or (b) it holds the trial for Quit Day (code version). The code version as written is the weakest possible offer at the highest-intent moment: Monthly "billed today" and Lifetime only, no anchor, no trial, after a 12-step onboarding that just built commitment. If the founder keeps decision 15 (trial on Quit Day), the end-of-onboarding paywall should be removed entirely or turned into a "lock in founding price" lifetime/annual-prepay screen; showing a subscription screen with the best plan missing will depress conversion and confuse ("where's the trial I was told about?").
- **3-day trial started on Quit Day = charge during peak withdrawal.** Milestone copy itself says day 3 is "By now most of the nicotine has left your body" and "The first week is the hardest for most people." The first $39.99 charge lands on Mon 9 Nov (day 4) with the reminder on Sat 7 Nov (day 2). Expect above-category refund/cancel rates and some "charged while I was suffering" one-star reviews — the very theme the V1 handoff set out to avoid. A 7-day trial that ends after "One week clear" aligns the charge with the first real win and matches the most common industry trial length; RevenueCat's data (vendor) shows sub-4-day trials are the worst-converting bucket. Alternative: keep 3 days but start it at Week 1 Day 1 with the charge framed as "after your first week clear".
- **Legal links are a rejection risk now, not later.** No Privacy Policy URL anywhere + PostHog analytics on by default outside the EU + RevenueCat ID = a 5.1.1(i)/(ii) and subscription-screen checklist failure. This is the single cheapest fix in the whole critique (one URL).
- Price prominence is compliant: the Yearly card shows `$39.99/yr` at 24 pt with `$3.33/mo` at 12 pt; the offer card shows `$39.99/yr` large and `$3.33 a month` as detail. Keep.
- The first-run 3-second close delay is defensible (common pattern, VoiceOver exemption present) but adds nothing when there is no trial to sell; Lazyweb's data shows most paywalls hide the close entirely, so DayXero is already on the gentle side. Keep the delay only if the screen sells the trial; otherwise show the close at once and rely on the Quit Day offer.
- No EU state is required while the app is IAP-only. The only EU-specific behaviour in the prototype — analytics opt-in by region (`'EU (opt-in, off)'`) — is correct. If the founder ever adds a web/founding-price checkout link, the guideline 3.1.1(a) prohibition applies outside the US storefront and the EU entitlement/fee regime changes the economics; not worth it at this scale.
- Family Sharing is promised on the website for Lifetime ("Family Sharing Capable") but not in the app; enabling it in App Store Connect "can't be undone" and quit apps are frequently used by couples/households — either enable it deliberately on Lifetime (and say so on the paywall) or remove the website claim.
- The website's $19.99 founding first year and "First 500 get founding pricing" are not reflected in the app. Apple supports this as an introductory offer (pay-up-front or pay-as-you-go) and/or offer codes; it can coexist with a free trial only as one introductory offer per subscription group, so "3-day trial then $19.99 first year" would need to be a single pay-up-front intro offer or an offer code, not two stacked offers.
- SOS free forever is right ethically and commercially: it is the entry point for widgets, Lock Screen control, Siri and the Live Activity, and it is what reduces one-star "they paywalled the panic button" reviews. The code already keeps it free; the recommendation is to say it on *every* paywall surface (the first-run paywall and "all plans" screen currently list SOS under "What's included", which reads as if it were paid — the offer screen's "Always free" line is the right pattern).

## KQ3. Retention mechanics: day counters/streaks, notifications, widgets, evening check-in, milestones/celebrations, money-saved and health timelines — what is evidence-backed, what risks guilt/churn, what Finch/Duolingo/I Am Sober do, and the 2026 gamification backlash

### Takeaway
DayXero's retention loop is deliberately anti-guilt ("Never guilt. No streak-loss threats, no red badges"; slips "never change days clear"; widgets "Wins only"), which matches the 2025–2026 backlash against Duolingo-style streak pressure and the Finch "no streak loss" pattern. ### Inferences
- **Keep**: the no-guilt notification contract, adaptive back-off (3 ignores → halve, 3 more → stop with a card), quiet hours by default, `.passive` milestone level, badge off, "Wins only" widgets, free SOS on the Lock Screen, the pledge as a 10-second morning ritual, cited milestone facts, and share cards with no name/photo. These are the parts that the Finch and backlash literature reward and that the Smoke Free/Smart-T trials support (daily missions + timed craving prompts are the two mechanics with RCT evidence; both are in the handoff as N1/N2/Window Guard).
- **Contradiction to resolve: "streak" vocabulary.** The Today "Streak card" (`"Smoke-free · 3 days"`, `"Prep streak · 3 / 7"`) and the water `"5-DAY STREAK"` pill use the word the brand has otherwise banned. Because slips never reset "days clear", the counter is really "days since Quit Day" — a user who slipped on day 5 and sees "Smoke-free · 7 days" may feel the app is lying to them (I Am Sober users split exactly on this). Recommend renaming the card ("Day 7 since Quit Day · 6 clear days") or showing both numbers in Progress, and dropping the water streak pill for a "goal days this week" count.
- **Notification volume vs promise.** Onboarding promises "Two small nudges a day. That's it." The settings footer promises "At most three a day from the program", but water reminders have their own cap, NRT sits outside the cap, Live Activities (2 guards/day) and "away" notes are extra. Realistic worst case is 6–8 system interruptions a day in Week 1. Either make the onboarding screen honest ("two a day to start; cravings and water are separate and you choose them") or enforce one global cap of 3–4. The 8:00/21:00 preview times also differ from the handoff's 8:30 default (minor).
- **Milestone cadence is well chosen** (1/3/7/14/30 are the CDC/smokefree.gov inflection points) but the gap between day 30 (finale) and day 60 coincides with the end of the paid 4-week arc — the first post-programme month is where "ongoing value" (3.1.2(a)) and retention both thin out. Add a "what now" maintenance loop (weekly check-in, trigger-pattern insight, money milestone at $100/$250/$500) so the annual subscriber sees value after Day 30.
- **Evening check-in gating is ambiguous.** The reveal lists it as paid, the code does not gate it, and the notification priming promises it to everyone. Decide and make the three surfaces agree; a free check-in is a better retention hook than a paid one (it is the daily reason to open the app that feeds the slip flow and the craving model).
- Money-kept is used everywhere (tiles, widgets, milestones, share cards, reveal "$2,555 in year 1"). Privacy mode for widgets already hides it. Keep; consider a user-set "money goal" as the one explicit goal mechanic, since loss-framed streaks are off the table.
- Water tracker + HealthKit write (off by default) is a sensible "do something else" tool, but its Apple Health integration, Control Center control and reminders make it the most notification-heavy module; keep reminders off by default rather than "On by default" as the handoff says.

## KQ4. Trust and data: export, deletion, privacy copy, Apple privacy nutrition label and health-data expectations, and whether to use HealthKit

### Takeaway
The Me/Settings surfaces are unusually trustworthy for the category (no account, local-first, "Export my data" via the share sheet, "Delete all data" with a clear alert, a plain-language Privacy page naming PostHog and RevenueCat, EU opt-in analytics, Lock Screen privacy mode). The two gaps are the missing Privacy Policy URL (an Apple requirement, not a nicety) and an App Privacy label that must declare PostHog "Product Interaction"/identifier data and RevenueCat "Purchases" even though nothing health-related leaves the phone. HealthKit is already planned for water; writing Mindful Sessions from breathing/SOS is cheap, on-device-only (so not "collected"), and would put DayXero in the Health app's Mindfulness tile — worth doing, with permission asked only when the user turns it on.

- HealthKit privacy: each app needs explicit per-type write permission, must disclose HealthKit use and supply a privacy policy, should "request only what the app needs", and needs `NSHealthUpdateUsageDescription`; mindful sessions written via `HKCategoryTypeIdentifier.mindfulSession` appear in the Health app's Mindfulness section — [Apple, Protecting user privacy (snippet)](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/healthkit/protecting-user-privacy); [Apple HIG HealthKit (snippet)](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/healthkit); [freeCodeCamp mindful minutes tutorial](https://www.freecodecamp.org/news/read-write-mindful-minutes-from-healthkit-with-swift-232b65118fe2/).

### Inferences
- The App Privacy label cannot be "Data Not Collected": PostHog events (Product Interaction, probably Diagnostics/Crash, plus an anonymous ID that likely qualifies as "Other User ID"/Device ID) and RevenueCat's anonymous app user ID + purchase history are "collected" under Apple's definition. Quit-related answers (products, triggers, reasons, NRT, slips) stay on device, so no "Health & Fitness" data needs declaring unless the PostHog event payloads carry `primary_product`, `days_clear` or similar — the Superwall attributes list shows exactly those are sent to Superwall, and Superwall events are mirrored to PostHog, so "Health & Fitness — Analytics — Not linked to you" may in fact be required. Audit the event payloads before filling in the label.
- Analytics "on by default outside the EU" is defensible under 5.1.1(ii) only because a visible toggle exists and consent is implied by the Welcome legal line — but the legal line currently links only to Terms; tie the consent to the Privacy Policy once the URL exists, and consider "off until first launch completes" to be safe.
- HealthKit: writing Mindful Sessions from the Calm/SOS breathing screens is low-risk (write-only, on-device, no label impact beyond "Health & Fitness — not collected"), surfaces DayXero in the Health app, and is a feature competitors in the quit category rarely offer. Do not read heart rate or anything else; do not write "cigarettes not smoked" (no such HealthKit type) and keep the water write optional as planned.
- Export format matters for trust: the share sheet is correct, but the lab does not say what the file is (JSON? CSV?). A human-readable CSV/JSON with a one-line explanation on the Privacy page ("Export gives you a JSON file of everything the app stores") closes the loop and pre-empts GDPR portability questions.
- Make Settings reachable from the Me tab (decision 21 says it should be); a privacy-forward app that hides Export/Delete behind a header initial undermines its own story.

### Gaps
- Could not read Apple's App Store Connect help on the exact "Data Not Collected" criteria; inference is from the App Privacy Details page definitions.
- Export file format and contents are not described in the lab or the handoff.
- Whether PostHog event payloads include product/days-clear attributes was not verified in code (only the Superwall attribute list was read).

## KQ5. Recommendations — concrete changes and expected impact, plus what to keep

### Takeaway
Fix the lab/code mismatch and legal links first (zero-cost, rejection-blocking), move the trial to 7 days ending after "One week clear", simplify the end-of-onboarding paywall to match decision 15, make the streak/notification vocabulary consistent with the no-guilt brand, and keep the honest billing copy, free SOS/prep, trust card, states and widgets that already outperform the category.

### Inferences
- The biggest single revenue lever is not price but *when* the trial is sold and *when* it ends; the current design converts at the hardest emotional moment and in the lowest-converting trial bucket.
- The biggest single trust lever is one URL (privacy policy); the biggest retention lever is consistency between what onboarding promises (two nudges, no streaks) and what Today/Water/notifications actually do.

### Gaps
- Expected-impact numbers are inferred from vendor medians, not from DayXero data; the Superwall/PostHog events already defined (`paywall_open`, `freeTrial_start`, `transaction_complete`, `paywall_decline`) should be used to measure the 3-vs-7-day and placement changes after launch.
