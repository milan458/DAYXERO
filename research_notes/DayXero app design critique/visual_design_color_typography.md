## 1. Colour: is the 12-ground palette coherent, do the section codes read, what passes WCAG, is dark mode a real design?

### Takeaway
The warm family (coral, apricot, sun) plus navy ink and cream paper is a genuinely ownable identity, and every ink/muted token passes WCAG AA on its ground in both modes. But the palette is 12 grounds in name only: 39 of 69 screens sit on `reading` cream, six "codes" appear on a single screen each, and four of the cool grounds (lilac, mist, periwinkle, sky) differ from each other by 1.13–1.18:1, so the section-colour code cannot be learned. Dark mode is a real design on the coloured screens but three leftover black-OLED screens, bright hero art left unadjusted on plum, and a black "sheet backdrop" on 14 light-mode Settings screens break it.

### Cited Findings
- **Ground usage is lopsided.** From the registry: `reading` = 39 screens (all Sessions, Log, Calm tools, Me/Settings, Paywall, Today, progress, changing-detail); `lilac` = 8 (onboarding chat); `violet` = 6; `apricot` = 4; `mint` = 4; `sun` = 3; `coral`, `ocean`, `sky`, `lime`, `periwinkle` = 1 each; `mist` = 0 as a screen ground (it appears only inside the "Your patterns" card on `progress`) — [shots/_screens.json](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/_screens.json); grounds defined at the top of [lab.js](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/lab.js).
- **Cool grounds are near-duplicates.** Computed luminance contrast between grounds: mist #C9D6FF vs periwinkle #B4C4FF **1.18:1**; sky #9CCBFF vs mist **1.17:1**; mint #9FE3BC vs lime #C1F07A **1.13:1**; coral vs apricot 1.64:1; coral vs sun 1.88:1 — [contrast.js output](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/contrast.js). Side by side on the Calm sheet, `calm` (periwinkle) and the `progress` patterns card (mist) read as the same colour — [sheet-calm-light.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/sheets/sheet-calm-light.png), [progress--light--full.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/progress--light--full.png).
- **WCAG text contrast, light mode (ink / muted on ground):** coral 6.46 / 6.01; apricot 10.62 / 8.20; lilac 11.10 / 6.21; mint 11.22 / 6.79; ocean 5.98 / 5.25; sky 9.81 / 6.10; violet 8.89 / 6.00; sun 12.16 / 6.35; reading 15.57 / 6.22; lime 12.67 / 7.81; mist 11.51 / 7.15; periwinkle 9.72 / 6.04. **Dark mode:** every ink ≥ 14.2:1 and every muted ≥ 9.09:1. All pass AA 4.5:1; the tightest is ocean muted 5.25 — [contrast.js](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/contrast.js). Apple's own bar: "At a minimum, make sure the contrast ratio between colors is no lower than 4.5:1. For custom foreground and background colors, strive for a contrast ratio of 7:1, especially in small text." — [Apple HIG, Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode). Ocean (5.98/5.25) and coral (6.46/6.01) miss Apple's 7:1 "strive for" target.
- **Coral as text fails.** Coral #FF7A55 on reading cream = **2.41:1**, on the cream card = 2.44:1. It is used as *text* for the 12px kicker "TODAY · 6 MIN" on the highlighted program day card and "FROM QUIT DAY" on the changing timeline card — [program--light--full.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/program--light--full.png), [changing--light--full.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/changing--light--full.png); ratio from [contrast.js](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/contrast.js). Cream on coral (2.44) and cream on apricot (1.48) are also below 3:1, which matters wherever cream text or icons sit on those grounds (the "+" sparkles and the white "S" avatar chip on the apricot Today hero).
- **Translucent cards barely separate from their ground.** Card-vs-ground contrast of the 70% cream card: lime **1.16**, sun **1.20**, mint 1.27, lilac 1.28, apricot 1.32; the 20% navy card on violet is **1.13**, on ocean 1.23; the 10% white card in dark mode is 1.27–1.35 on every ground — [contrast.js](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/contrast.js). Visible on `checkin-form` (violet) where the "How are you feeling tonight?" card is a faint rectangle — [checkin-form--light.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/checkin-form--light.png).
- **Green does double duty.** #0F6E43 is simultaneously the Program's path colour, the universal "done" tick, the onboarding progress bar on lilac, the "today's session" action card on Today, and the selected answer state in sessions — [today--light--full.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/today--light--full.png), [sheet-onboarding-light.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/sheets/sheet-onboarding-light.png), [states-session-kit.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/sheets/states-session-kit.png). In iOS a page sheet dims the *actual* parent view, so this is a prototype artefact, but it means the single most-visited utility area has never been reviewed as it will look.
- **Dark reading surfaces are good.** #0E0B2B base with #1B1747 / #2A255F cards gives 1.27–1.39 separation and matches Apple's base/elevated idea ("The base colors are dimmer… the elevated colors are brighter") — [Apple HIG, Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode); [sheet-sessions-dark.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/sheets/sheet-sessions-dark.png).
- **Accent sprawl beyond the 12 grounds.** The health-timeline kit tiles on `changing` use at least ten hues (purple, strawberry red, yellow, peach, pink, blue, teal, navy, mint, coral) on a lime ground; program day cards use six more tile colours — [changing--light--full.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/changing--light--full.png), [program--light--full.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/program--light--full.png). Craving slider is a green→orange→red gradient — [log-craving--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/log-craving--light.png).
- Apple on colourful apps and the new tab bar: "If your app features colorful backgrounds or visually rich content, prefer a monochromatic appearance for toolbars and tab bars, or choose an accent color with sufficient visual differentiation." — [Apple HIG, Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color).

### Inferences
- The identity is really **three families**, not twelve colours: warm (coral/apricot/sun = Xero/day/celebration), deep (ocean/violet = Jelli/Glim, cream ink), and paper (reading cream/navy). The five pastel cools (lilac, sky, mist, periwinkle, mint/lime) are where coherence breaks: they look like one colour and carry five different meanings. **CHANGE:** collapse to one chat/onboarding pastel (lilac), one "relief" pastel (sky), and drop mist, periwinkle and lime as *screen* grounds (keep them as card tints if needed). Six codes is the most a user will ever learn; twelve is decoration.
- The colour-by-section idea reads only where a character owns the colour (ocean = Jelli, violet = Glim, apricot = Xero). Mint = Program, lime = body timeline, periwinkle = calm are arbitrary; they have no character and no recurring icon, so nothing teaches them. **KEEP** character-owned colours; **CHANGE** section colours to either inherit from a character or go to reading cream.
- Green should be reserved for progress/done (its strongest existing meaning) and the Program should stop using it as its brand colour; the "today" action card on Today could be apricot (Xero's) instead of green to stop the CTA looking like a completed state. **CHANGE.**
- **CHANGE** the coral kicker on cream to navy or the ground's muted token (coral stays as the decorative mark, where 2.4:1 is acceptable as non-informational). Add a 3:1 rule for any text/icon on coral or apricot: use navy, never cream.
- Raise the translucent card alpha on lime/sun/violet or give cards a 1px hairline so they separate at ≥1.3:1; the current 1.13–1.20 cards vanish under sunlight (Apple: "In bright surroundings, colors look darker and more muted" — [Apple HIG, Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). **CHANGE.**
- Dark mode needs a dark variant of the dawn horizon (dusk/night palette over plum, lower luminance sun) for the five hero screens, and the three black-OLED screens need to be rebuilt on the reading tokens. **CHANGE.** The lab should render sheets over their real parent rather than a flat black. **CHANGE (prototype).**

### Gaps
- No OLED device test of plum #241433 exists; the design system itself flags it "unconfirmed until tested on an OLED phone" — [tokens.json](/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/tokens.json).
- Increase Contrast variants are not defined for any token; Apple asks for "an increased contrast option for each variant" — [Apple HIG, Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color). Not evaluable in the prototype.
- WCAG 2.2 SC text could not be fetched (w3.org blocked); thresholds quoted are from Apple and secondary summaries.

## 2. Typography: Bricolage Grotesque + DM Sans hierarchy, sizes, legibility, and fit with iOS norms

### Takeaway
The pairing is distinctive and legible: Bricolage 800 for titles gives the app a voice that SF Pro cannot, and DM Sans is a quiet, well-hinted body face. The problems are scale and overuse: 14px has become the de facto body (334 instances vs 215 at 16px), 12px caps kickers appear 253 times so they no longer create hierarchy, and nothing in the prototype demonstrates Dynamic Type, which Apple treats as mandatory for custom fonts.

### Cited Findings
- **Type scale as built:** display 40/800/−1.2, large 32/800/−1.0, title 24/800/−0.6, headline 19/700 (all Bricolage); body 16/400, footnote 14/500, caption 12/700/+0.8 caps (DM Sans) — [lab.js Lab.type](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/lab.js).
- **Size census across all 69 default screens (text nodes):** 14px ×334, 12px ×253, 16px ×215, 17px ×128 (mostly status clock and list rows), 19px ×109, 24px ×40, 32px ×39, 20px ×30, 13px ×28, 34px ×10, 40px ×6, 48px ×5, 54 and 57px ×1 each; sub-12px only on the `milestone-share` preview card (9.6px "DayXero", 10.8px "days nicotine-free") — [_audit.json summary](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/_audit.json).
- **Apple's iOS defaults (Large):** Large Title 34, Title 1 28, Title 2 22, Title 3 20, Headline 17 semibold, Body 17, Callout 16, Subhead 15, Footnote 13, Caption 1 12, Caption 2 11; platform default text size 17pt, minimum 11pt — [Apple HIG, Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography). DayXero body is 16 (Apple Callout), its footnote 14 sits between Apple's Subhead and Footnote.
- Apple on custom fonts: "If you use a custom font, make sure it implements the same behaviors [Dynamic Type, Bold Text]… be guided by the recommended minimum font sizes." and "Minimize the number of typefaces you use, even in a highly customized interface." — [Apple HIG, Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography).
- Apple on tracking: SF "dynamically adjusts tracking at every point size" and at 40pt SF uses **+0.37pt** tracking — [Apple HIG, Typography tracking table](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography). DayXero's display uses **−1.2px** at 40px; the display face therefore reads noticeably tighter than any system text around it (e.g. the status bar).
- **Bricolage is a display face by design.** A type blog describes it as "a contemporary, eccentric blend of many influences" and "a bit too distracting for a lot of body text, so it is better suited to headings"; it is variable with weight, width and optical-size axes and SIL OFL licensed — [pimpmytype via search snippet](https://pimpmytype.com/?p=16410), [Google Fonts / Figma listing](https://www.figma.com/fonts/bricolage-grotesque/). DayXero uses it only ≥19px, which is correct.
- **Visible quirks:** the swash-tailed Q in "Quit Day" / "Pick your Quit Day, Sam" and the two-storey g are the most "Bricolage" moments — [onb-quit-day--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/onb-quit-day--light.png), [milestone--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/milestone--light.png).
- **Hierarchy breaks where everything is a kicker.** Today (day 3) stacks eight 12px caps labels in one viewport (SUN 8 NOV · DAY 3, YOUR COFFEE WAVE, TODAY · DAY 2 · 3 MIN, YOUR DAY, …) — [today--day3--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/today--day3--light.png). The session beats put a 12px kicker above a 19–24px question above 14px option text, every beat — [states-session-kit.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/sheets/states-session-kit.png).
- **Numbers are the best typography in the app:** 24–32px Bricolage numerals in stat tiles ("$2,555", "26 not smoked", "31 · 61 · 26") are the clearest hierarchy on any screen — [finale--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/finale--light.png), [onb-baseline-summary--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/onb-baseline-summary--light.png).
- **Settings use 34px Bricolage large titles on iOS-style grouped lists** (`settings`, `notifications`, `settings-privacy`, `settings-health`) — the only place the app looks like a stock iOS app — [sheet-me-light.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/sheets/sheet-me-light.png).
- **Duplicated titles:** `lesson-audio` shows "The noisy passengers" twice (header and 310px lower); `milestone` day-1 shows "1 Quit Day" then "Quit Day" on the next line — [lesson-audio--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/lesson-audio--light.png), [milestone--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/milestone--light.png).

### Inferences
- **KEEP** Bricolage 700/800 for titles and numerals, DM Sans for body; two families is within Apple's "minimize typefaces" advice and the pairing is the app's most mature decision.
- **CHANGE** the body/footnote split: make 16px (or 17 to match Apple Body) the floor for any sentence a user must read; reserve 14px for metadata (times, "1 min", sources). Today's card bodies, the baseline explanation cards and session option rows are all at 14px and feel smaller than iOS around them.
- **CHANGE** kicker policy: one kicker per card maximum, none on list rows; let the Bricolage title carry the hierarchy. Measure: Today day-3 should drop from eight kickers to three.
- **CHANGE** display tracking from −1.2/−1.0 to about −0.6/−0.4 at 40/32px; the current setting is tighter than SF and makes "Morning, Sam" collide with the hand-drawn circle.
- **ADD** a Dynamic Type demonstration state (at least xxxLarge and AX3) to the lab for Today, SOS, session beat and tab bar; fixed 58px buttons, 70px tab bar with 12px labels, and 40px segmented tabs will all need to grow. Apple treats this as part of custom-font legitimacy ("make sure it implements the same behaviors").
- Fix the two duplicated titles (copy, trivial).

### Gaps
- No measured legibility data (reading speed, error rate) for DM Sans 14px on a 402pt device; only the size census is available.
- Could not fetch an independent review of DM Sans on mobile; its suitability is inferred from the screenshots.

## 3. Layout and components: consistency, cards, spacing, buttons, tab bar, status bar, white space, density; weakest and strongest screens

### Takeaway
The coloured, character-led screens (Welcome, Today, SOS, Passed, Evening, Check-in, Pledge, Program path, Finale) share one convincing system: 58px navy pill with lip shadow, 20–24px gutters, 18–24px radii, hero-plus-card stacks. The 39 cream screens do not: session beats are 60–70% empty, Settings borrow stock iOS, Calm/Water/breathe beats use a different black UI with their own button style, the paywalls use a third card style, and milestones repeat one template eleven times. Weakest by id: `milestone`, `water`, `calm-box`/`calm-478`, `session-kit` (breathe, commit, quiz beats), `session-mission`, `lesson-audio`, `paywall`, `log-menu`, `changing`, `onb-trial`. Strongest: `finale`, `onb-welcome`, `sos`, `sos-passed`, `today` (day3), `evening`, `checkin-form`, `onb-quit-day`, `program`, `log-slip`, `onb-baseline-summary`, `changing-detail`.

- **CHANGE** Calm tools, Water and the breathe beat onto the reading tokens: sage ring → mint/ocean ring on cream (or on violet at night), khaki buttons → navy pill / cream pill. This removes the third visual language and the last OLED remnant.
- **CHANGE** Today: merge the "Your day" checklist with the action card(s) (one "Now" card, one "Later today" list), move the two log tiles into one "Log" row, and cap the viewport at four modules. Day-3 Today currently shows two stacked coloured CTAs (ocean + green) competing.
- **CHANGE** Program: either the path or the card list, not both; the path is the differentiator, so keep the path and make nodes tappable, demote the list to "completed" only.
- **CHANGE** Milestones: make each state visibly different (stage art, sky clarity, a different character present, a different health fact) and fill the yellow void with the share card preview; a template repeated eleven times is the opposite of a celebration.
- **CHANGE** paywall plan cards to one style (two cards + "Pay once" link, as `offer` already does); uncrop the horizon or remove it.
- **CHANGE/ADD** tab bar: either adopt the native iOS 26 `TabView` (free Liquid Glass, scroll-minimise, no private-API risk) and express SOS through its symbol plus tint-on-select, or keep the custom pill but give it `glassEffect` and monochrome inactive icons per Apple's advice for colourful backgrounds. - Break onboarding monotony with one change of ground at the baseline summary (`onb-baseline-summary` already has the navy stat card; give that screen the mint/building ground) and a hero on `onb-triggers`.

- Scroll-under behaviour with the tab bar (blur/dim) is not modelled in the lab; content simply ends 96px above the bottom.

## 4. Illustration and mascots: Xero, Jelli, Glim, the art.js scenes, and the marked-word motif

### Takeaway
The Crew is the app's biggest asset and its clearest differentiator against Smoke Free, Kwit, I Am Sober and Quittr, none of which has a character. The drawing quality (paper-cut horizon layers, grain, soft glow, SMIL breathing) is above most indie wellness apps and will age better than flat Corporate-Memphis blobs. Three things undercut it: a second, derivative illustration language (the Alegria-style human figure) sits beside the Crew; Xero barely changes across 365 days despite the "sky clears as you do" story; and the hand-drawn mark appears on so many titles that it stops being a signature.

### Cited Findings
- **Character construction:** all three share one face system (two dot eyes, small mouth, blush, blink on hello/cheer) and `pose` tracks; Jelli's tentacles morph with SMIL; Glim has a radial yellow tail glow; the horizon uses layered paper-cut hills with `feDropShadow`, blurred far clouds and `feTurbulence` grain; a `clarity` parameter hazes the sky — [art.js](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/art.js) sections "The crew (200×200)", "HorizonScene"; design doc §6 — [DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md).
- **Xero reads as a coral egg/pebble with a white-and-green "belly window"** that resembles a Tamagotchi screen or a capsule; Jelli is a pink jellyfish with closed eyes; Glim a violet firefly with translucent wings — [onb-welcome--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/onb-welcome--light.png), [sos--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/sos--light.png), [evening--light--full](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/evening--light--full.png).
- **Growth is invisible.** Across milestone states day 1, 3, 7, 14 Xero is pixel-identical; from day 30 a sun halo appears; days 30–365 are identical again — [states-milestone.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/sheets/states-milestone.png). The spec promises "Character stage = days clear <7 → 1, <14 → 2, <28 → 3, else 4" and "the sky clears as you do" — [DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md §7](/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md).
- **A second illustration language:** a human figure (round head, orange/beige jumper, raised arm) appears on `program`, `changing`, and seated on a cloud on `calm` — [program--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/program--light.png), [changing--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/changing--light.png), [calm--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/calm--light.png); `PersonFigure (100 × 120)` in [art.js](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/art.js). This is the "Corporate Memphis / Alegria" idiom: "crisp sans-serif fonts, pastel palettes and long-limbed cartoon figures", widely criticised and by 2023 "oversaturated to the point of self-cannibalisation" — [search snippets on Corporate Memphis](https://www.plushcap.com/content/webflow/blog/webflow-after-corporate-memphis-what-comes-next-and-how-to-stay-ahead-of-the-trends), [feedme.design](https://feedme.design/why-do-people-hate-corporate-memphis-a-dive-into-the-alegria-aesthetic-apocalypse).
- **Headspace benchmark (snippet-level):** "Headspace's illustrations are its most recognizable design element… the system follows strict rules that maintain consistency across thousands of assets"; "Headspace's characters are more recognizable than its logo" — [Blake Crosley design guide via search](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/headspace). A 2024 critique of Headspace's onboarding notes "visual inconsistency (mixing cartoon style and photo-heavy promotion screens)" — [Contra case study via search](https://contra.com/p/R3MmcOeU-headspace-app-feature-redesign).
- **Finch benchmark (snippet-level):** "a self-care app that works like a digital Tamagotchi"; the "birb" is customisable and cared for by completing tasks; a 2026 Pratt critique warns "A mascot with no functional logic becomes decoration" — [Pratt IXD via search](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/design-critique-finch-self-care-pet-ios-app/), [screensdesign via search](https://screensdesign.com/showcase/finch-self-care-pet).
- **Kit tiles:** 22 "kit" kinds plus 64×64 health illustrations with soft glows are used as 40–64px squares on program cards and the health timeline — [art.js KitArt, Health timeline illustrations](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/art.js); fourteen different tiles on one lime screen — [changing--light--full](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/changing--light--full.png).
- **Marked word usage:** coral underline/circle appears on `today` ("Sam", circled), `program` ("program"), `onb-reveal` ("ready"), `changing` ("changing"), `progress` ("progress" and "patterns"), `calm` ("Calm", circled), `checkin-plan` ("tomorrow", sun yellow), `log-slip` ("telling me"), `milestone` ("Quit Day") — [sheet-progress-light.png](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/sheets/sheet-progress-light.png), [today--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/today--light.png). Spec: stroke 4, one per screen, coral on light grounds, cream on saturated, sun on ocean/violet — [tokens.json mark](/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/tokens.json). It redraws (0.6s dash animation, 0.35s delay) on every render — [lab.css .marked](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/lab.css).
- **Finale** puts all three characters, confetti in six token colours and a glowing sun in one hero; it is the single most "brand" image in the set — [finale--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/finale--light.png).

### Inferences
- **KEEP** the Crew, the paper-cut horizon and the grain; they are charming rather than kitsch because the faces are restrained (no big anime eyes, no speech-bubble exclamation marks) and the scenes are quiet. Derivative risk is moderate: dot-eye blobs are the common idiom of Headspace/Finch/Duolingo, but a sun-egg, a jellyfish and a firefly tied to day/wave/night is a distinct cast with a reason to exist, which Headspace's generic shapes lack.
- **CHANGE** remove `PersonFigure` entirely (program, changing, calm). It is the one element that would make a 2026 reviewer say "stock illustration", and it breaks the rule that the Crew are the only people in the app.
- **CHANGE** make growth legible: stages 1–4 must differ in silhouette (size, posture, an added feature) not just a halo at 28 days, and the Today hero should use the `clarity` parameter so the sky visibly clears between day 1 and day 28; the prototype's Today hero looks the same in prep, Quit Day and day 3 — [today--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/today--light.png), [today--quitday--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/today--quitday--light.png), [today--day3--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/today--day3--light.png).
- **CHANGE** ration the mark: use it on moments (reveal, passed, slip-thanks, finale, Quit Day) and never on persistent tab titles ("program", "progress", "Calm"); animate it once per session, not on every render. At the current frequency it reads as a template feature rather than a hand.
- **CHANGE** restrict kit tiles to the warm/deep families (apricot, coral, ocean, violet, navy) so a list of fourteen tiles reads as one set.
- **ADD** one Xero "stage sheet" screen in the lab showing stages 1–4 and clarity 20/50/80/100 side by side, so the growth promise can be reviewed.

### Gaps
- Direct visual comparisons with Headspace/Finch/Kwit screens were not possible (image galleries blocked); claims about those apps rest on text snippets.
- No user testing of character appeal with the target 25–45 smoker/vaper audience exists in the repo.

## 5. Motion: what exists and is it purposeful?

### Takeaway
The prototype carries a lot of ambient SMIL motion (245 `<animate>` elements across the default screens, up to 27 on one screen) plus two CSS animations (marked-word draw-on, breathing words), but none of the system-level motion the design doc specifies (arrive, settle, celebrate, count-up, hold fill). The purposeful pieces (SOS breath, Jelli pulse, hold buttons) are right; the ambient twinkle on Evening/Check-in and the animated health icons on the lime timeline are the ones to cut.

### Cited Findings
- **Animation census (default states):** finale 27, changing 25, program 23, evening 17, checkin-form 17, checkin-saved 17, checkin-dayend 17, calm 14, today 9, sos 8, evening-breathing 8, log-slip 8, milestone 8, onb-welcome 7, onb-quit-day 7, pledge 7, sos-passed 7, session 4, onb-breathe 3, onb-building 3, session-done 3, lesson-audio 3, progress 2, paywall 1; all other screens 0. None of Arrive/Settle/Celebrate/Count is implemented in the lab (no CSS transitions or keyframes besides the three above) — [lab.css](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/lab.css).
- Breathing words: two stacked text layers cross-fade on a 10s loop (`bw-in` 0–40%, `bw-out` 40–94%) — [lab.css](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/lab.css); visible as "Breathe in… 4" on `sos` and "Breathe in" on `evening-breathing` — [sos--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/sos--light.png).

### Inferences
- **KEEP** SOS/evening breathing (text + ring + Jelli pulse on one 4/6 clock), the hold-to-commit fill, and the program path's pulsing play node; these are the only animations tied to a user action or a bodily rhythm.
- **CHANGE** cut ambient animation on information screens: `changing` (25 animating health icons on lime) and the Glim night sky on `checkin-form`/`checkin-dayend` (twinkles behind a form you are typing into). Apple's HIG does not forbid ambient motion, but the app's own doc says "Always slow"; twinkle at 1.5–3s is not slow.
- **ADD** the Arrive/Count/Celebrate tokens to the lab (even as CSS) so the thing that most determines "feel" can be reviewed before SwiftUI; count-up numerals on Today and Finale are the cheapest high-impact motion in the set.
- Motion volume is currently inversely related to task weight: Finale (27) is fine, but the heaviest cream task screens have 0 and feel static while the ambient scenes feel busy.

## 6. Accessibility: contrast, touch targets, reduced motion, Dynamic Type readiness, colour-only meaning

### Takeaway
Text colour contrast is in good shape (all token pairs ≥ 5.25:1), Reduce Motion is modelled, and status is never colour-only on the Program path or checklists. The gaps are coral-as-text (2.4:1), a handful of sub-44pt controls (program tabs 40px, share segmented control 26px, source pills 26px, text-link rows 17–33px), card boundaries at 1.1–1.2:1, a craving slider that encodes intensity by hue, and no Dynamic Type state anywhere in 275 states.

### Cited Findings
- Contrast results: see §1 (all ink/muted pass; coral text 2.41:1 fails; cream-on-coral 2.44, cream-on-apricot 1.48) — [contrast.js](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/contrast.js).
- **Targets under 44pt found by DOM audit:** `program` segment tabs 40px; `onb-baseline-vape` toggle segments 40px; `milestone-share` Story/Square/Dawn/Night segments 26px; `onb-nrt` and `nrt-setup` source pills 26/19px; `onb-past-attempts` slider labels row 14px; `lesson-audio` speed chip 34px; `progress` "+ Log" 36px; `water` "Personalise goal" 36px / "Undo last" 40px; `water-goal` unit toggle 34px; `paywall`/`paywall-plans` "Restore purchases" 17px text and footer links 33px; `offer` footer links 16px; `changing-detail` notice rows 34px — [_audit.json](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/_audit.json). Apple's HIG recommends a 44×44pt default control size; WCAG 2.2 AA (2.5.8) requires 24×24 CSS px or equivalent spacing, AAA (2.5.5) 44×44 — [Tetralogical summary of target sizes](https://tetralogical.com/blog/2022/12/20/foundations-target-size/), [Deque iOS touch target guidance](https://docs.deque.com/devtools-mobile/2025.7.2/en/ios-touch-target-spacing).
- **Sub-12px text:** only the `milestone-share` preview card (9.6px, 10.8px); everything else ≥ 12px, meeting the design rule "nothing smaller" and Apple's 11pt minimum — [_audit.json](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/_audit.json), [Apple HIG, Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography).

Apple: "Avoid relying solely on color to differentiate between objects, indicate interactivity, or communicate essential information." — [Apple HIG, Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color).
- **Colour-only cases:** craving slider green→red gradient (value is also shown as "5 / 10", so partially mitigated) — [log-craving--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/log-craving--light.png); quiet-hours bar "Grey is quiet. Sage marks are your daily notifications" (legend only, marks are 2px ticks) — [notifications-quiet-hours--light](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/notifications-quiet-hours--light.png); prep-streak dots grey vs green — [today--light--full](/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero%20app%20design%20critique/shots/today--light--full.png).
- **Dynamic Type:** every size in the lab is a fixed px value (`Lab.font` emits `font-size:${size}px`) and no state exercises larger text — [lab.js](/tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/lab.js). Apple: "Using text styles with the system fonts also ensures support for Dynamic Type… If you use a custom font, make sure it implements the same behaviors." — [Apple HIG, Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography).
- Apple on testing dark + accessibility together: "in Dark Mode with Increase Contrast and Reduce Transparency turned on (both separately and together), you may find places where dark text is less legible" — [Apple HIG, Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode). The 20% navy card on violet (1.13:1) and 10% white cards in dark mode (≈1.3:1) are the candidates.
- Reduce Motion handling: see §5.

### Inferences
- **CHANGE** coral kicker text → navy/muted (§1); enforce "cream text only on ocean/violet/navy/plum".
- **CHANGE** raise program segment tabs, share segments, unit toggles and source pills to 44pt hit areas (visual height can stay smaller with padding); make footer links 44pt rows.
- **CHANGE** give the craving slider a labelled scale ("Mild / Moderate / Strong") and the quiet-hours bar tick labels, so hue is not the only carrier.
- **ADD** an "AX3 / xxxLarge" state to Today, SOS, a session beat and Settings in the lab, and a rule that Bricolage/DM Sans sizes are declared `relativeTo:` an iOS text style.

- VoiceOver order, labels and `aria-live` behaviour cannot be evaluated from a static HTML lab; the design doc's rules (§9) are untested.
- WCAG 2.2 normative text could not be fetched (w3.org blocked); thresholds cited via secondary summaries.

## 7. Benchmark against 2025–2026 best-in-class health/habit apps and current iOS design direction

### Takeaway
Against the quit-smoking field (Smoke Free, Kwit, I Am Sober, Quittr) DayXero is visually far ahead: those apps are described as "utilitarian", "clean and intuitive" or data-dashboard-led and none has a character cast. Against the broader wellness tier (Headspace, Finch, Calm, Opal) the gap is the opposite: those brands win on *consistency and systemisation* of their illustration and on characters that visibly respond to the user, which is exactly where DayXero's prototype is thinnest. iOS 26's Liquid Glass moves the platform toward floating translucent navigation over colourful content, which suits DayXero's coloured grounds but penalises its opaque custom tab bar.

### Cited Findings

### Inferences
- **What DayXero has that none of the quit apps have:** a three-character narrative tied to time of day, warm non-clinical colour, hand-drawn marks, no streak-shaming red. **KEEP** and lead with it in App Store screenshots: Welcome, SOS, Passed, Evening, Finale.
- **What the wellness leaders do that DayXero does not yet:** (1) a strict illustration rulebook applied to every asset (Headspace) — DayXero has three idioms (Crew, PersonFigure, multi-hue kit tiles); (2) a character whose state is a function of user behaviour and is visible every day (Finch) — Xero's growth is a halo at day 28; (3) a designed reveal moment for milestones (Opal) — DayXero's eleven milestones are one template. **CHANGE/ADD** accordingly (see §3, §4).
- **Platform direction:** Apple is normalising exactly what DayXero's coloured grounds want — translucent, floating navigation over rich content — so the native iOS 26 tab bar would make the app look *more* on-brand, not less; the opaque white custom pill is the one component that will date the app on day one. **CHANGE.**
- Minimalism as a "2026 trend" is vendor opinion; the stronger signal is Apple's own guidance toward restrained colour in controls and rich colour in content, which DayXero can satisfy by keeping grounds colourful and controls monochrome.

## 8. Summary scorecard: change / keep / add (with screen ids)

**Keep**
- Warm family + navy + cream identity; Bricolage/DM Sans pairing; 58px lip-shadow pill; stat tiles; the Crew's restrained faces and paper-cut horizon; SOS/evening breathing motion; hold-to-commit; the strongest screens `finale`, `onb-welcome`, `sos`, `sos-passed`, `today` (as composition, not density), `evening`, `checkin-form`, `onb-quit-day`, `program` path, `log-slip`, `onb-baseline-summary`, `changing-detail`.

**Change**
- Palette: collapse 12 grounds to ~6 (drop mist, periwinkle, lime as screen grounds); reserve green for done/progress; coral never as text; raise card alpha / add hairlines on lime, sun, violet.
- Dark mode: dark variants of the dawn hero (`onb-welcome`, `onb-quit-day`, `pledge`, `milestone`, `finale`); rebuild `water`, `calm-box`, `calm-478`, `session-kit` breathe beat on reading tokens; render sheets over the real parent in the lab (14 Settings/Me screens).

- Layout: de-duplicate Today and Program; fill the session/calm/log task screens (`session`, `session-kit`, `session-mission`, `calm-anchor`, `calm-notice`, `log-slip-what`, `checkin-dayend`) with top-anchored content and an 88px character; one plan-card style across `paywall`, `paywall-plans`, `offer`, `upgrade`; differentiate the 11 `milestone` states.
