## 1. Vaping cessation: how many vapers want to quit, and what works

### Takeaway
Roughly 6 in 10 US adult vapers say they plan to quit and 15-25% try each year, but the adult data are old (2015-2019 PATH waves) and I found no UK "want to quit vaping" percentage. Trial evidence exists for a text-message program (young people) and varenicline (and, in one small trial, cytisinicline); Cochrane rates all of it low certainty, and no medication is licensed specifically for vaping cessation.

### Cited Findings

**Desire and attempts to quit (US)**
- US adults, PATH Wave 3 (2015-2016): 62.4% of e-cigarette users reported plans to quit vaping; >25% reported at least one past-year quit attempt (12.8% all at once, 13.5% by cutting down). — [Rosen & Steinberg, PMC7171275](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7171275)
- US adults, PATH Wave 5 (2018-2019), n=2,922 established users: 68.2% planned to quit vaping; 17.3% attempted to quit in the past year; 29.3% attempted by cutting back. — [Interest in quitting e-cigarette use by device type and smoking history in US adults (index record)](https://ouci.dntb.gov.ua/works/4VggAbkl)
- US adults, PATH (Palmer et al. 2021 research letter): 60.7% (1,208) endorsed future plans to quit e-cigarettes; 15.2% (302) made a past-year quit attempt. — [JAMA Network Open](https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2834558) (URL as returned by search; verify it resolves to the Palmer letter)
- US, ages 15-36, current e-cigarette users, 2021 (Truth Longitudinal Cohort; published in Preventive Medicine): 54.2% general intention to quit; 15.3% intend to quit within 30 days; 33.3% past-year quit attempt. — [Truth Initiative](https://truthinitiative.org/research-resources/quitting-smoking-vaping/most-young-e-cigarette-users-want-quit-1-3-has-tried)
- US young adults 18-24, PATH Wave 7 (Jan 2022-Apr 2023): 29.1% (855) had quit or attempted to quit electronic nicotine products in the past 12 months. — [search-summary of PATH analysis; source list](https://tobaccopreventioncessation.com/pdf-186271-108255) (attribution of this figure to this specific URL is uncertain; verify)
- US middle/high-school daily e-cigarette users: share who tried to quit but could not rose from 28.2% (2020) to 53% (2024). — [Truth Initiative](https://truthinitiative.org/research-resources/quitting-smoking-vaping/most-young-adult-nicotine-users-want-quit-2026) (reported via search summary of Truth Initiative pages; underlying dataset is presumably NYTS but not confirmed)

**Prevalence and quitting vaping (UK)**
- Great Britain adults 18+, ASH/YouGov Smokefree GB survey: 10.4% current vaping in 2025 (~5.5 million); 2026 factsheet reports ~10% (10.3%), "unchanged from last year", growth fastest 2020-2024 and "appears to have now plateaued". — [ASH adult vaping factsheet](https://ash.org.uk/resources/view/use-of-e-cigarettes-among-adults-in-great-britain)
- GB adults who quit smoking with a vape in the last five years: 67% are still vaping, 32% have also quit vaping. — [ASH adult vaping factsheet](https://ash.org.uk/resources/view/use-of-e-cigarettes-among-adults-in-great-britain)
- Top reasons GB adults give for vaping: to help quit smoking (19%), to prevent relapse to smoking (14%). — [ASH](https://ash.org.uk/key-topics/vaping-what-you-need-to-know)

**Trial evidence: text-message program (This is Quitting, Truth Initiative)**
- Graham et al. 2021, JAMA Intern Med (doi:10.1001/jamainternmed.2021.1793); n=2,588 US young adults aged 18-24 who vaped in past 30 days and wanted to quit within 30 days. Self-reported 30-day point-prevalence abstinence at 7 months, ITT with non-responders counted as vaping: 24.1% (95% CI 21.8-26.5) vs 18.6% (16.7-20.8) assessment-only control; OR 1.39 (1.15-1.68). 76.0% follow-up. Not biochemically verified. — [Truth Initiative press release](https://truthinitiative.org/press/press-release/truth-initiatives-quit-vaping-program-effective-helping-18-24-year-olds); [NAQC summary](https://www.naquitline.org/news/570292/Effectiveness-of-a-Vaping-Cessation-Text-Message-Program-among-Young-Adult-E-cigarette-Users-A-Rand.htm)
- Graham et al. 2024, JAMA; n=1,503 US adolescents aged 13-17 (mean 16.4; 759 intervention, 744 control). Self-reported 30-day abstinence at 7 months: 37.8% vs 28.0% (the "35% more likely" headline is relative). One news summary cites 1,681 including a 178-person waitlist arm. — [Contemporary Pediatrics](https://www.contemporarypediatrics.com/view/text-messaging-program-increases-vaping-cessation-rates-for-adolescents); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240811/Can-a-text-message-program-help-teens-quit-vaping.aspx)

**Trial evidence: varenicline**
- Evins et al. 2025, JAMA (doi:10.1001/jama.2025.3810); n=261 US youth aged 16-25 vaping nicotine >=5 days/week, not regularly smoking. Biochemically verified continuous vaping abstinence weeks 9-12: 51% varenicline vs 14% placebo; weeks 9-24: 28% vs 7% (adjusted OR 6.0). Versus enhanced usual care: 51% vs 6% (weeks 9-12), 28% vs 4% (weeks 9-24). All drug/placebo participants also got weekly remote counselling plus referral to This is Quitting. No drug-related serious adverse events. — [Mass General Brigham press release](https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/about/newsroom/press-releases/smoking-cessation-drug-helps-young-adults-quit-vaping); [ACC journal scan](https://www.acc.org/Latest-in-Cardiology/Journal-Scans/2025/04/30/12/03/Varenicline-Plus-Behavioral-Counseling)
- Correction to the brief: the 2025 JAMA youth varenicline trial is Evins et al. (Rigotti is a co-author). The "Rigotti 2024" paper is the cytisinicline trial below, in JAMA Internal Medicine, not JAMA. — [EurekAlert](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1081314); [MGH press release](https://www.massgeneral.org/news/press-release/trial-shows-cytisinicline-can-help-quit-vaping)

**Trial evidence: cytisinicline (ORCA-V1)**
- Rigotti et al. 2024, JAMA Intern Med 184(8):922-930; n=160 US adults who vaped nicotine daily and did not currently smoke; 12 weeks cytisinicline 3 mg three times daily vs placebo, all with behavioural support. Continuous e-cigarette abstinence weeks 9-12: 31.8% vs 15.1%; OR 2.64 (95% CI 1.06-7.10; P=.04). 3.8% discontinued cytisinicline for adverse events. Authors say it needs confirmation in a larger, longer trial. Cytisinicline is not FDA-approved. — [MGH press release](https://www.massgeneral.org/news/press-release/trial-shows-cytisinicline-can-help-quit-vaping); [ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05431387](https://clinicaltrials.gov/study/NCT05431387)
- "No medication has been approved by the FDA for vaping cessation in the United States" (Rigotti, 2024). — [MGH press release](https://www.massgeneral.org/news/press-release/trial-shows-cytisinicline-can-help-quit-vaping)

**Cochrane review: Interventions for quitting vaping (Butler, Lindson, Hartmann-Boyce et al.; CD016058)**
- Two versions exist with different numbers. Earlier version (published January 2025): varenicline vs placebo at 6 months RR 2.00 (95% CI 1.09-3.68; 1 study, 140 participants), low certainty. November 2025 update (CD016058.pub3): RR 2.71 (95% CI 1.33-5.49; 2 studies, 315 participants), low certainty (imprecision). — [UEA repository record](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/98166); [EurekAlert, Jan 2025](https://www.eurekalert.org/news-releases/1069598)
- Text-message interventions "may increase" vaping cessation vs no/minimal support in 13-24-year-olds: low certainty (indirectness). — [UEA repository record](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/98166)
- Combination NRT, cytisine, and nicotine/vaping reduction: "inconclusive due to risk of bias and imprecision". — [UEA repository record](https://ueaeprints.uea.ac.uk/id/eprint/98166)
- Most studies measuring serious adverse events reported none; more data needed. Co-author: "This is a really early area of research." — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250108/New-study-identifies-effective-strategies-to-help-people-quit-vaping.aspx)

### Inferences
- A defensible onboarding line: "Most people who vape say they want to stop (around 6 in 10 US adult vapers in national surveys)". Avoid attaching a recent year; the adult figures are 2015-2019.
- Absolute effects are modest for text programs (about +5.5 points in young adults, about +10 points in adolescents) and both are self-reported. Quote absolute rates, not "40% more likely".
- Varenicline has the largest effect size but only in 16-25-year-olds/small adult samples with counselling; DayXero should describe it as "early trial evidence" and note it is prescription-only and not licensed for vaping cessation.
- NRT for vaping cessation: the only systematic statement found is Cochrane's "inconclusive". DayXero's NRT support for vapers rests on extrapolation from smoking evidence and should say so.
- Control-arm quit rates are high (18.6% and 28.0% at 7 months by self-report), which suggests many motivated young vapers quit unaided; this is not a population quit rate.

### Gaps
- No UK figure found for the share of current vapers who want to stop or have tried (ASH factsheet and Smoking Toolkit Study not located with that statistic). ONS data on quitting vaping not found.
- No adult-wide US quit-intention figure from 2022 or later was found; NYTS-specific "seriously thinking about quitting" percentages were not retrieved (cdc.gov blocked).
- Exact numerator/denominator and RR for the Cochrane text-message comparison were not retrieved.
- No vaping-cessation data found for Australia, Germany or India.
- Pub3 of the Cochrane review should be opened to confirm the 2.71 figure and which second trial was added (presumably Evins 2025).

## 2. Nicotine pouches and smokeless tobacco

### Takeaway
Pouch sales in the US more than tripled from January 2023 to December 2025 while adult prevalence remains low (current use around 0.4-2.5% depending on survey and definition). I found no randomised cessation trial for nicotine pouches and no population figure for the share of pouch users who want to quit; the only cessation evidence is for traditional smokeless tobacco (Cochrane 2015).

### Cited Findings

**Prevalence and growth**
- US adults, 2022 survey of 39,557 adults (USC/Nebraska, published JAMA 2024): 2.9% had ever used nicotine pouches; 0.4% current use. — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240711/Prevalence-of-nicotine-pouch-use-found-to-be-low-among-US-adults.aspx)
- US adults, 2022-2023 federal survey data (Rutgers, "Patterns of Nicotine Pouch Use Among Adults in the US, 2022-2023"): 2.5% of adults use/used pouches (measure as reported in the press summary; ever vs current not confirmed), mostly current or former tobacco users; highest current and daily use among people who recently quit tobacco; daily use nearly four times more likely among recent quitters. — [Rutgers](https://www.rutgers.edu/index%2ephp/news/nicotine-pouches-may-offer-path-reduced-tobacco-harm); [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20250908/Nicotine-pouch-use-may-be-a-step-toward-reducing-or-quitting-more-dangerous-forms-of-tobacco.aspx)
- US adult smokers (Penn State cross-sectional analysis, 2025): pouch use about 0.5% of adults overall and 1.2% of current smokers. — [Penn State Pure](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/nicotine-pouch-use-and-intentions-to-quit-cigarette-smoking-among/)
- US high-school students, 2025: 2.3% used a nicotine pouch in the past 30 days. — [Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids fact sheet 0427](https://cdn.tobaccofreekids.org/resourceFiles/Fact-Sheets/US/0427.pdf)
- US nicotine pouch sales in traditional retail outlets "more than tripled" between January 2023 and December 2025; Zyn is by far the leading brand. — [Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids fact sheet 0427](https://cdn.tobaccofreekids.org/resourceFiles/Fact-Sheets/US/0427.pdf)
- FDA authorised marketing of 20 Zyn products in January 2025, the first pouch authorisation (marketing authorisation, not an "FDA approved as safe" or cessation claim). — [UCHealth](https://www.uchealth.org/today/are-nicotine-pouches-bad-for-you/)
- Great Britain: UCL analysis of >127,000 people; pouch use rising, driven by young men; pouches used in 6.5% of recent quit-smoking attempts in 2025 (tripled over the study period). — [UCL News, Dec 2025](https://ucl.ac.uk/news/2025/dec/nicotine-pouch-rise-driven-young-men)

**Pouches as a quit aid (for smoking), not quitting pouches**
- US adults who smoked and tried to quit in the past year (2022): 5.2% used pouches vs 21.6% e-cigarettes. — [Prova summary of JAMA 2024 study](https://provaeducation.com/news/few-us-adults-use-nicotine-pouches-nationally-representative-study-finds/2467303)
- PATH Study (Nicotine & Tobacco Research, Dec 2025): only 90 respondents used pouches in their most recent attempt to quit cigarettes/other products and 65 for vapes. — [NAQC listing](https://www.naquitline.org/news/718624/Correlates-of-the-Use-of-Nicotine-Pouches-to-Quit-Other-Nicotine-and-Tobacco-Products-among-United-S.htm)
- Retailer-sponsored poll (Nicokick/Censuswide, n=1,000 US adults): 42% of pouch users started in order to quit smoking. Industry source; low reliability. — [Business Wire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20250723122578/en/2025-Nicotine-Pouch-and-Oral-Nicotine-Report-U.S.-Nicotine-Pouch-Market-Surges-40-as-Smokers-Seek-Smoke-Free-Alternatives)

**Nicotine content and delivery**
- German Federal Institute for Risk Assessment (BfR) survey of 44 pouch products: nicotine content ranged from about 1.8 to 47.5 mg per pouch; tobacco-specific nitrosamines detected in many samples. — [Frontiers in Pharmacology 2024 (citing BfR)](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2024.1392027/epub)
- On average about 15-20% of a pouch's nicotine content is extracted and absorbed, with large inter-individual variation. — [PMC11150668](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC11150668/)
- Crossover study, 15 smokers: a 30 mg pouch produced plasma nicotine peaks similar to a cigarette; cardiovascular and other side effects rose with dose; all products reduced acute cigarette craving, including the nicotine-free pouch. — [Frontiers in Pharmacology 2024](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/pharmacology/articles/10.3389/fphar.2024.1392027/epub)
- Nicotine delivery from pouches scales with labelled content (6, 9, 11.2 mg Nordic Spirit study, Cmax and AUC). — [Psychopharmacology 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s00213-024-06721-7)

**Cessation evidence (smokeless tobacco only)**
- Cochrane "Interventions for smokeless tobacco use cessation" (Ebbert et al., CD004306.pub5, 26 Oct 2015; no later update found): varenicline RR 1.34 (95% CI 1.08-1.68; 2 studies, 507 participants), moderate quality. — [Cochrane Library](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD004306.pub5/information/hi)
- Same review: nicotine lozenges RR 1.36 (95% CI 1.17-1.59; 5 studies, 1,529 participants), low quality (result sensitive to excluding three trials); two bupropion trials showed no benefit. — [Cochrane abstract PDF](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD004306.pub5/pdf/CDSR/CD004306/CD004306_abstract.pdf)
- Behavioural interventions RR 1.37 (95% CI 1.23-1.53) overall, per a secondary evidence summary (verify against the review; trials were heterogeneous). — [Cochrane PEARLS summary](https://primarycare.cochrane.org/sites/primarycare.cochrane.org/files/public/uploads/pearls/pearls%20310.pdf)

### Inferences
- DayXero should state plainly: "There are no published trials of how best to quit nicotine pouches. Our approach draws on evidence for smokeless tobacco and smoking." The smokeless trials were largely in US chewing tobacco/snuff and Scandinavian snus users, so applicability to modern tobacco-free pouches is indirect.
- For onboarding dose estimates, labelled mg x pouches/day overstates systemic nicotine by roughly 5x (15-20% absorbed); a personalised "nicotine per day" figure should either use labelled content and call it that, or apply the absorption range and show it as an estimate.
- Prevalence numbers across surveys are not comparable (ever vs current vs daily; 2022 vs 2023); quote one with its definition.

### Gaps
- No source found for the share of nicotine pouch users who want to quit or have tried to quit pouches.
- No pouch-specific cessation RCT found; a Tobacco Control study reportedly finding pouch use not associated with higher odds of quitting tobacco appeared only as an unverified reference-list snippet.
- No head-to-head dependence comparison (pouches vs cigarettes) found; Zyn 3/6 mg delivered-dose figures (Lunell 2020) not retrieved.
- No pouch prevalence data retrieved for Australia, Germany (sale is restricted) or India (where smokeless tobacco such as gutkha/khaini is the relevant category; not researched here).
- Whether CD004306 has an update after 2015 beyond the 2019 "no change" edit is unconfirmed.

## 3. Nicotine dependence facts for onboarding

### Takeaway
Three facts are well sourced: inhaled nicotine reaches the brain in 10-20 seconds; nicotine's plasma half-life is about 2 hours; withdrawal symptoms peak in the first week and last 2-4 weeks (Hughes 2007).

### Cited Findings
- After a puff, high levels of nicotine reach the brain in 10-20 seconds, faster than intravenous administration (applies to smoked cigarettes). — [Benowitz, Hukkanen & Jacob, "Nicotine chemistry, metabolism, kinetics and biomarkers", PMC2953858](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2953858)
- Same 10-20 second puff-to-brain lag stated in an NCBI Bookshelf chapter. — [NCBI Bookshelf NBK294308](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK294308/)
- Plasma half-life of nicotine averages about 2 hours; cotinine (main metabolite) about 16 hours. Seen in secondary teaching sources this session; the primary citation is the Benowitz review above (verify the sentence there). — [Indiana Prevention Resource Center course page](https://iprc.iu.edu/training/courses/Tobacco%20Dependence%20and%20Treatment/a_04_03_01.html); [UCSF Rx for Change pharmacology slides](https://rxforchange.ucsf.edu/file_downloads/02%20PCOL.pdf)
- Hughes 2007, Nicotine & Tobacco Research 9(3):315-327: "Anger, anxiety, depression, difficulty concentrating, impatience, insomnia, and restlessness are valid withdrawal symptoms that peak within the first week and last 2-4 weeks." Constipation, cough, dizziness, increased dreaming and mouth ulcers "may be" abstinence effects; drowsiness and fatigue are not. The author notes the time course needs clearer definition. — [PubMed 17365764](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17365764/)

### Inferences
- The 10-20 second figure is for cigarette smoke. It should not be applied to pouches (buccal absorption is much slower, peaking over tens of minutes) and only cautiously to vapes, where delivery speed varies by device and liquid; I found no equally authoritative single number for vapes.
- With a ~2 hour half-life, "nicotine is essentially gone from your blood within about a day" is arithmetically reasonable, but it is a derived statement; the widely quoted "nicotine-free in 48/72 hours" lines are not quoted from a primary source.
- Hughes 2007 is based on cigarette abstinence studies. Applying "peaks in week 1, eases over 2-4 weeks" to vape and pouch users is an extrapolation and should be labelled as "based on research in people who stopped smoking".

### Gaps
- DSM-5 tobacco withdrawal criteria (symptom list, onset within 24 hours) were not retrieved from a citable page this session.
- Increased appetite/weight gain and craving time course (which outlast 4 weeks in Hughes' review) were not confirmed from the abstract.
- No withdrawal time-course study specific to vaping or pouch cessation was found.

## 4. Health recovery timeline after stopping smoking: milestone audit

### Takeaway
The long-term milestones (heart disease, stroke, lung cancer, life expectancy) are supported by Surgeon General reports and cohort studies, but the 2020 Surgeon General report and current CDC wording are deliberately less precise than the classic poster ("risk falls sharply 1-2 years after cessation", lung cancer risk halves after "10-15 years"). The short-term milestones (20 minutes, 8/12 hours, 48 hours taste and smell, 72 hours) are repeated by WHO/NHS/ACS without strong primary studies behind the exact times, and agencies disagree with each other on them. No evidence-based recovery timeline exists for quitting vaping or pouches.

### Cited Findings

**What the agencies currently say**
- CDC "Benefits of Quitting Smoking": timeline begins "Minutes: heart rate drops" (CDC does not say "20 minutes"); CHD: "risk falling sharply 1-2 years after cessation and then declining more slowly over the longer term". **[search-summary]** — [CDC](https://www.cdc.gov/tobacco/about/benefits-of-quitting.html)
- American Cancer Society "Benefits of quitting smoking over time": 20 minutes heart rate and blood pressure drop; 1-2 years risk of heart attack drops; "15 years after quitting: your risk of coronary heart disease is close to that of a non-smoker". **[search-summary]** — [American Cancer Society](https://www.cancer.org/cancer/risk-prevention/tobacco/benefits-of-quitting-smoking-over-time.html)
- WHO Q&A "Tobacco: health benefits of smoking cessation": "Within 20 minutes, your heart rate and blood pressure drop"; "At about 30: gain almost 10 years of life expectancy"; about 40: 9 years; about 50: 6 years. **[search-summary]** — [WHO](https://www.who.int/news-room/q-a-detail/health-benefits-of-smoking-cessation); [WHO EMRO timeliner](https://www.emro.who.int/timeliner/index.html)
- WHO EMRO: 2 weeks to 3 months, circulation improves and lung function improves "up to 30%". **[search-summary]** — [WHO EMRO timeliner](https://www.emro.who.int/timeliner/index.html)
- Carbon monoxide timing differs between sources: NHS-derived pages give 8 hours (CO halved/oxygen recovering) or "8-12 hours". **[search-summary]** — [Lincolnshire Partnership NHS FT](https://www.lpft.nhs.uk/smokefree/improve-your-health); [HSE Ireland leaflet](https://www.hse.ie/eng/about/who/tobaccocontrol/resources/benefits-of-quitting.pdf)
- 2020 Surgeon General report "Smoking Cessation": CHD risk decreases "rapidly in the period immediately following cessation" with a slower long-term decline; cessation "reduces the risk of stroke morbidity and mortality"; lung cancer relative risk falls to about half that of continuing smokers after roughly 10-15 years; cessation "reduces the risk of premature death and can add as much as a decade to life expectancy". — [HHS key findings](https://www.hhs.gov/surgeongeneral/reports-and-publications/tobacco/2020-cessation-sgr-factsheet-key-findings/index.html); [US Pharmacist review of the report](https://uspharmacist.com/article/reviewing-the-2020-surgeon-general-report-on-smoking-cessation); [full report, NCBI Bookshelf](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/books/NBK614481/)
- Cho et al. 2024, NEJM Evidence; 1.5 million adults in US, UK, Canada, Norway followed ~15 years: benefit evident within 3 years of quitting; cessation for <3 years averted about 5 years of life lost; cessation for >=10 years averted about 10 years, with survival approaching never-smokers. — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240209/New-study-shows-dramatic-health-benefits-of-quitting-smoking.aspx); [PubMed 38329816 record](https://pesquisa.bvsalud.org/sms/resource/pt/mdl-38329816)

**Milestone-by-milestone verdict (cigarettes only)**

| Popular claim | Who states it | Verdict |
|---|---|---|
| 20 minutes: heart rate (and blood pressure) drop | WHO, ACS, NHS (pulse); CDC says "minutes" | Agency-endorsed; physiologically plausible (acute nicotine effect wearing off). Primary source is the 1988/1990 Surgeon General reports via ACS. Safe if worded "within about 20 minutes" and attributed to WHO/ACS. |
| 8 hours / 12 hours: carbon monoxide returns to normal | NHS family: 8 h (CO halved); WHO: 12 h (CO normal) **[recall, not re-verified]**; CDC/ACS: "a few/several days" **[recall, not re-verified]** | Sources disagree by an order of magnitude. COHb half-life is roughly 4-6 hours breathing air (standard physiology; not sourced this session), which fits "falls substantially within 12-24 hours". Use a range and name the source. |
| 48 hours: taste and smell improve | NHS **[recall, not re-verified]** | Weakest popular milestone. Not in CDC, WHO, or 2020 SG lists as far as I could confirm. Treat as folklore-grade timing; say "many people notice taste and smell improving within days". |
| 72 hours: breathing easier, energy up | NHS **[recall, not re-verified]** | Same status as 48 hours: no primary study behind the exact time. |
| 2-12 weeks: circulation improves, lung function increases | WHO, NHS, ACS ("2 weeks to 3 months") | Agency-endorsed, direction supported; the "up to 30%" (WHO EMRO) and "up to 10%" (NHS, **[recall]**) lung function figures conflict and lack a clear primary source. Do not quote a percentage. |
| 1-9 months: coughing and shortness of breath decrease | WHO; ACS/CDC say 1-12 months **[recall]** | Agency-endorsed; reasonable. Use "1 to 12 months". |
| 1 year: heart disease risk is half that of a smoker | WHO, NHS ("heart attack risk halved"), older ACS | Revised. Derived from the 1990 SG report ("excess risk of CHD is reduced by about half after 1 year"). CDC now says risk "falls sharply 1-2 years after cessation"; ACS says 1-2 years. Use the CDC/2020 SG wording, not "halves at 1 year". |
| 5 years: stroke risk falls to that of a non-smoker | WHO ("5-15 years") **[recall]**, 1990 SG | Softened. 2020 SG says only that cessation reduces stroke risk and that risk approaches never-smoker levels over time. Use "5-15 years" at most, or "stroke risk decreases". |
| 10 years: lung cancer risk about half that of a continuing smoker | WHO, NHS, ACS, 2020 SG ("10-15 years") | Well supported. Use "after 10-15 years". Risk stays above a never-smoker's. |
| 15 years: heart disease risk same as a non-smoker | WHO, ACS ("close to that of a non-smoker") | Supported in softened form: "close to" rather than "the same as". |

- The "classic" timeline traces to the 1990 Surgeon General report "The Health Benefits of Smoking Cessation", which the 2020 report updates. — [HHS key findings](https://www.hhs.gov/surgeongeneral/reports-and-publications/tobacco/2020-cessation-sgr-factsheet-key-findings/index.html)

**Vaping and pouches**
- The only human follow-up evidence found for vaping cessation and lung recovery is a Mass General case series of four chronic vapers with small-airway fibrosis: partial reversal 1-4 years after stopping, incomplete because of residual scarring. — [Mass General press release, Sept 2022](https://www.massgeneral.org/news/press-release/Mass-General-study-finds-damage-in-the-lungs-of-chronic-e-cigarette-users)
- A Vancouver study is recruiting to test lung and immune effects of stopping vaping for 72 hours in young adults (i.e. this is still being established). — [VCHRI](https://vchri.ca/participate/disorders-and-conditions/smoking-0)
- Commercial sites publish vaping recovery timelines ("lung function improves after a few weeks", "six months to a year") without citing studies. — [Respiratory Therapy Zone](https://www.respiratorytherapyzone.com/?p=315912); [Recovered.org](https://recovered.org/blog/benefits-of-quitting-vaping)

### Inferences
- For cigarette users DayXero can show a milestone timeline if each card names its source and uses the current, softer wording: "minutes", "within days" for CO, "1-2 years: heart attack risk falls sharply", "10-15 years: lung cancer risk about half that of someone still smoking", "15 years: heart disease risk close to a non-smoker's".
- For vape and pouch users there is no evidence-based timeline. The honest options are (a) nicotine-specific milestones that do not depend on smoke (nicotine clearance, withdrawal peak in week 1 and easing over 2-4 weeks, money saved, days nicotine-free) and (b) an explicit note that long-term recovery data do not yet exist. CO, coughing, lung cancer and CHD milestones must not be shown to vape-only or pouch-only users; pouches produce no CO at all.
- The percent-of-lung-function claims (10% vs 30%) are the clearest example of folklore inside official pages and should be dropped.

### Gaps
- Could not open CDC, WHO, ACS, NHS pages or the 2020/1990 Surgeon General report PDFs directly; exact current wording of every row above needs a final check, especially the rows marked **[recall, not re-verified]** (NHS 48 h/72 h, WHO 12 h and 5-15 years, CDC "several days" and "3-6 years added CHD risk halves").
- Did not find the specific 2004 and 2010 Surgeon General report passages relevant to milestones.
- No primary study located for the 20-minute, 48-hour or 72-hour claims.
- No Australian (Quit/Department of Health) or German (DKFZ/BZgA) timeline wording was checked beyond one search hit.

## 5. Life expectancy, mortality and mental health

### Takeaway
Quitting at about 30, 40, 50 or 60 gains about 10, 9, 6 and 3-4 years of life versus continuing (Doll 2004; Jha 2013); up to two-thirds of deaths in current smokers are attributable to smoking (Banks 2015); and quitting is associated with better, not worse, mental health (Cochrane 2021), though the certainty of that evidence is low to moderate.

### Cited Findings
- Doll, Peto, Boreham, Sutherland 2004, BMJ (34,439 male British doctors, 1951-2001): "Cessation at age 60, 50, 40, or 30 years gained, respectively, about 3, 6, 9, or 10 years of life expectancy"; cigarette smokers died on average about 10 years younger than lifelong non-smokers. — [BMJ / PMC437128](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC437128)
- Jha et al. 2013, NEJM (US National Health Interview Survey 1997-2004, ~200,000 adults, deaths to 2006): quitting at ages 25-34, 35-44, 45-54 gained about 10, 9 and 6 years of life versus continuing; smokers lose at least a decade of life expectancy versus never-smokers. — [NEJM PDF (dcp-3.org mirror)](https://www.dcp-3.org/sites/default/files/resources/NEJMsa1211128.pdf)
- Jha 2013: quitting at 55-64 gained about 4 years (figure from the paper as reported by Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids; not in the abstract). — [Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids statement](https://www.tobaccofreekids.org/press-releases/2013_01_23_nejm)
- Cho et al. 2024, NEJM Evidence (1.5 million adults; US, UK, Canada, Norway): quitting at any age was associated with lower mortality; <3 years since quitting averted about 5 years of life lost, >=10 years averted about 10 years. — [News-Medical](https://www.news-medical.net/news/20240209/New-study-shows-dramatic-health-benefits-of-quitting-smoking.aspx)
- Banks et al. 2015, BMC Medicine 13:38 (Australia, 45 and Up Study, 204,953 people aged >=45, followed to mid-2012): "up to two-thirds of deaths in current smokers can be attributed to smoking"; all-cause mortality RR 2.96 (95% CI 2.69-3.25) for current vs never smokers; current smokers die an estimated 10 years earlier; mortality in those who quit before 45 did not differ significantly from never-smokers. — [BMC Medicine PDF](https://bmcmedicine.biomedcentral.com/track/pdf/10.1186/s12916-015-0281-z); [PubMed 25857449](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/25857449/)
- The older "half of long-term smokers are killed by smoking" comes from Peto's commentary on the British doctors study, not its abstract; men born around 1920 who kept smoking had about two-thirds killed by it. — [Cancer Research UK, 2004](https://news.cancerresearchuk.org/2004/06/22/50-year-tobacco-study-shows-smokers-lose-a-decade-of-life/)
- Taylor et al. 2021, Cochrane CD013522 (102 studies): compared with continuing to smoke, quitting was associated with lower anxiety (SMD -0.28, 95% CI -0.43 to -0.13; 15 studies, 3,141 participants; low certainty), depression (SMD -0.30, -0.39 to -0.21; 34 studies, 7,156 participants; very low certainty), and mixed anxiety and depression (SMD -0.31, -0.40 to -0.22; 8 studies, 2,829 participants; moderate certainty). — [PubMed 33687070](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33687070/); [Cochrane plain-language summary](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD013522_does-stopping-smoking-improve-mental-health)
- Taylor et al. 2014 (BMJ) as cited in the Cochrane protocol: anxiety SMD -0.37 (95% CI -0.70 to -0.03), depression SMD -0.25 (-0.37 to -0.12); effect sizes described as equal to or larger than antidepressant treatment for mood and anxiety disorders. — [Cochrane protocol, PMC6984645](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6984645)

### Inferences
- Safe onboarding copy: "Quitting by about 40 avoids around 90% of the excess risk / gains about 9 years" is consistent with Jha and Doll, but the "90%" figure itself was not verified this session; the sourced statement is the years gained.
- The age-60 figure differs by study: 3 years (Doll, male British doctors born 1900-1930) vs about 4 years (Jha, US men and women, ages 55-64). Show "3-4 years".
- "Two in three long-term smokers" (Banks) is the more recent figure, from Australia; "one in two" is the older British figure. Both are defensible if attributed; "up to two-thirds" is the authors' wording.
- Mental health: the evidence is observational (quitters vs continuers within cohorts and trials), so "is associated with" is the correct verb. The antidepressant comparison comes from the 2014 paper and should be used cautiously if at all.
- All of this is cigarette evidence. No life-expectancy or mental-health-after-quitting evidence exists for vape-only or pouch-only users.

### Gaps
- Original Taylor 2014 BMJ paper not opened; numbers are via the Cochrane protocol's citation.
- Jha 2013 55-64 figure not seen in the primary text.
- Did not retrieve India-specific mortality or life-years data (e.g. Jha's Indian cohorts), nor German (DKFZ) figures.

## 6. Money: cost of cigarettes, vapes and pouches

### Takeaway
A pack-a-day habit costs very roughly US$3,300-3,700, about £6,500, about A$18,000-20,000 and about EUR 2,900 a year at 2025-2026 average prices; I could not obtain an official pack price for India. Official statistical series (ONS, Destatis) were not directly readable, so most pack prices below come from advocacy bodies or secondary reports and must be labelled as approximate. The app should compute savings from the user's own stated spend wherever possible.

### Cited Findings

**United States**
- Weighted national average retail price about $9.16 per pack (including statewide sales tax, excluding most local taxes), January 2024 version of the fact sheet; federal tax $1.01 per pack. — [Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids fact sheet 0097](https://www.tobaccofreekids.org:443/assets/factsheets/0097.pdf)
- Average state cigarette excise tax $2.06 per pack as of 1 July 2026; state excise ranges from $0.17 (Missouri) to $5.35 (New York). — [Campaign for Tobacco-Free Kids tobacco taxes page](https://www.tobaccofreekids.org/what_we_do/state_local/taxes); [Review42 summary of CTFK data](https://resources.review42.com/cigarette-prices-by-state/)
- Secondary sites citing a CTFK table dated 18 Sept 2026 give a national average near $10.15 per pack, range $8.01 (Missouri) to $14.83 (New York). Not confirmed against CTFK directly. — [DataPandas](https://www.datapandas.org/ranking/cigarette-prices-by-state)

**United Kingdom**
- Average price of a pack of 20 roughly £17.74 after the latest duty rise (ASH email; date unclear). — [ASH email archive](https://politicalemails.org/messages/2037264)
- Marlboro Red King Size 20: £17.14 in July 2026 (up from £16.21 a year earlier), single-brand supermarket index. — [Trolley.co.uk](https://www.trolley.co.uk/grocery-price-index/brand/?id=365573)
- Tobacco duty rose again on 1 October 2026 by RPI + 2 percentage points plus an additional £2.20 per 100 cigarettes (44p per 20 before VAT). — [GOV.UK policy paper](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/tobacco-duty-rate-changes/changes-to-tobacco-duty-rates-from-26-november-2025-and-1-october-2026)
- ONS publishes the official series "RPI: Ave price - Cigarettes 20 king size filter" (series CZMP); values were not readable via search. — [ONS CZMP](https://www.ons.gov.uk/economy/inflationandpriceindices/timeseries/czmp)

**Australia**
- September 2025: mainstream 20-stick packs averaged A$54.99. — [Tobacco in Australia, section 13.3](https://www.tobaccoinaustralia.org.au/chapter-13-taxation/13-3-the-price-of-tobacco-products-in-australia)
- 1 September 2026 pack price A$49.73 (20s) and an average 11.7 cigarettes/day give about A$10,619 a year for the average daily smoker (commercial comparison site calculation). — [Compare the Market](https://www.comparethemarket.com.au/health-insurance/features/cost-of-smoking/)

**Germany**
- Weighted average price EUR 8.05 per 20-pack (July 2026, attributed to the Federal Finance Ministry by a consumer site), of which EUR 4.05 tobacco tax and EUR 1.29 VAT; projected EUR 8.77 in 2027 and EUR 11.36-11.78 by 2030 under tax plans. Secondary source. — [MonsterDealz Magazin](https://www.monsterdealz.de/magazin/zigarettenpreis); [heidelberg24](https://www.heidelberg24.de/politik-und-wirtschaft/zigaretten-bald-bei-1178-euro-streit-um-tabaksteuer-zr-94489523.html)

### Inferences
(All arithmetic is mine: price x 365, 20 cigarettes a day.)

| Market | Pack price used | Pack-a-day per year | Confidence in price |
|---|---|---|---|
| US | $9.16 (CTFK, Jan 2024) to ~$10.15 (secondary, Sept 2026) | about $3,340 to $3,700 | Medium; varies ~2x by state |
| UK | £17.74 (ASH, pre-October 2026 duty rise) | about £6,475 | Medium; likely higher after 1 Oct 2026 |
| Australia | A$49.73 (Sept 2026, commercial) to A$54.99 (Sept 2025 mainstream brands) | about A$18,150 to A$20,070 | Medium |
| Germany | EUR 8.05 (July 2026, secondary) | about EUR 2,940 | Medium-low |
| India | about INR 25-28 per king-size stick | about INR 182,500-204,400 for 20 king-size sticks a day | Low; a pack a day is atypical and most Indian tobacco use is bidis/smokeless |

- UK vaping: £8.39 a week is about £436 a year for the average vaper (around 7% of a pack-a-day smoker's spend).
- Zyn at MSRP: one can a day is about $2,077 a year; half a can about $1,040. At Illinois prices a can a day is about $3,620.
- Typical consumption is well below a pack a day in most markets (Australia 11.7-13 a day), so "pack-a-day" figures overstate the average smoker's spend by roughly 40%. Personalised calculation from the user's own inputs (units per day x price paid) is both more accurate and avoids citing an unstable national average.
- Prices move at least annually with duty changes (UK October 2026, Australia March/September indexation, Germany stepped rises to 2030, India February and May 2026), so any hard-coded default needs a dated source and a refresh schedule.
