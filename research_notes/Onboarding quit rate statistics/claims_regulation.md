## 1. FTC (US): substantiation, implied claims, borrowed research, "clinically proven", testimonials, own-user statistics, enforcement

### Takeaway
The FTC's Health Products Compliance Guidance (Dec 2022, still the current version; no later revision found) expressly covers health apps, judges the "net impression" including implied claims, and generally expects randomized controlled human trials on the product itself or an essentially equivalent one. Citing third-party studies of NRT or behavioural support is lawful only if the ad attributes the result to the method, not to DayXero, and the surrounding screen does not imply the app delivers that result.

### Cited Findings
- The guidance was issued 20 Dec 2022 by the Bureau of Consumer Protection, replacing the 1998 dietary supplement guide, with 23 new examples. [S] — [FTC press release](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2022/12/ftc-announces-new-business-guidance-marketers-sellers-health-products); [Venable](https://www.venable.com/insights/blogs/2022/12/ftc-announces-health-products-compliance-guidance)
- Scope: it "expressly applies to advertising for all health-related products, including foods, OTC medicines, homeopathic products, apps, equipment, and diagnostic tests" (law-firm summary). [S] — [Cooley](https://www.cooley.com/news/insight/2023/2023-03-02-ftc-revises-health-products-compliance-guidance)
- Substantiation: "substantiation of health-related benefits will need to be in the form of randomized, controlled human clinical testing to meet the competent and reliable scientific standard" (quoted by a law firm from the guidance). No fixed number of RCTs; animal, in vitro and most observational studies generally do not suffice; the guidance warns against "p-hacking". [S] — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/after-almost-25-years-ftc-issues-new-health-products-compliance-guidance/); [guidance PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/Health-Products-Compliance-Guidance.pdf)
- Definition of "competent and reliable scientific evidence": "tests, analyses, research, or studies that (1) have been conducted and evaluated in an objective manner by experts in the relevant disease, condition, or function to which the representation relates; and (2) are generally accepted in the profession to yield accurate and reliable results." [R] — [guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)
- Net impression and implied claims: evaluation looks at the overall impression (text, product name, visuals), not isolated wording; advertisers are responsible for express and implied claims; a disclosure fails if a significant minority of consumers are still misled. [S] — [McDermott](https://www.mcdermottlaw.com/insights/after-almost-25-years-ftc-issues-new-health-products-compliance-guidance/); [FoodChain ID summary](https://www.foodchainid.com/resources/united-states-updated-ftc-guidance-on-claims-in-advertising/)
- Relevance of borrowed research: research must be consistent with the totality of evidence and "relevant to the specific product and claimed effect" (summary). [S] — [McDermott/MWE](https://www.mwe.com/insights/after-almost-25-years-ftc-issues-new-health-products-compliance-guidance/). The guidance's own wording is approximately: advertisers should make sure the research "isn't just internally valid, but also relevant to the specific product being promoted and to the specific benefit being advertised", asking whether dose, formulation, manner of administration and study population match. [R] — [guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)
- Qualified claims: qualifiers must be clear and conspicuous; "vague qualifying terms" such as that the product "may" have a benefit or "helps" achieve it "are unlikely to be adequate"; if evidence is limited, say so plainly rather than hint. [R] — [guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance); clear-and-conspicuous emphasis confirmed [S] — [Mofo](https://lifesciences.mofo.com/topics/ftc-issues-updated-health-products-claims-guidance)
- "Clinically proven" / "science-backed" are establishment claims: the advertiser must hold the level of proof it claims to have, i.e. clinical proof on the product. [R] — [guidance](https://www.ftc.gov/business-guidance/resources/health-products-compliance-guidance)
- Testimonials (Endorsement Guides, 16 CFR Part 255, revised 26 Jul 2023): if the advertiser lacks substantiation that the endorser's experience is representative, it must clearly and conspicuously disclose the generally expected results (§255.2(b)); "clear and conspicuous" means "difficult to miss (i.e., easily noticeable) and easily understandable by ordinary consumers"; where outliers skew a mean, the disclosure should use the median. [S] — [FTC Endorsement Guides PDF](https://www.ftc.gov/system/files/ftc_gov/pdf/P204500%20Guides%20Concerning%20Endors%20and%20Testimonials.pdf); [King & Spalding](https://www.kslaw.com/news-and-insights/ftc-revamps-the-guides-concerning-the-use-of-endorsements-and-testimonials-in-advertising)
- The guidance "doesn't have the force or effect of law"; Kelley Drye argues it overstates the RCT requirement relative to case law. [S] — [Kelley Drye](https://www.kelleydrye.com/viewpoints/blogs/ad-law-access/misguided-the-ftc-attempts-to-redefine-the-law-with-its-health-products-compliance-guidance)
- Lumosity (Jan 2016): $2 million redress; FTC alleged "unfounded claims" that games improve performance and delay cognitive decline; undisclosed incentives for testimonials; order requires competent and reliable scientific evidence for future claims. [S] — [Arent Fox](https://www.arentfox.com/newsroom/alerts/lumos-labs-settles-ftc-over-brain-training-program-claims); [Healthcare IT News](https://healthcareitnews.com/node/476096)
- 2026 enforcement climate: FTC Chairman Ferguson created a Healthcare Task Force (Mar 2026); a Fifth Circuit ruling in Intuit v. FTC (Mar 2026) reportedly bars the FTC from pursuing deceptive-advertising claims through its in-house administrative process (federal court route remains). [S] — [Debevoise](https://www.debevoise.com/insights/publications/2026/03/ftc-developments-impacting-healthcare-enforcement); [CFS Law Monitor](https://www.consumerfinancialserviceslawmonitor.com/2026/03/ftc-launches-healthcare-task-force/)

### Inferences
- "Up to 2x more likely to quit with DayXero" is an implied product-efficacy claim with no product RCT: non-compliant. "In clinical trials, people who used nicotine replacement were about 50 to 60% more likely to quit than those who did not (Cochrane review, year). DayXero has not been tested in a clinical trial." attributes the result to the method and discloses the limit: defensible, provided the screen design does not undo it.
- An app is not "the same product" as face-to-face counselling or a quitline studied in trials. Statistics for "behavioural support" should name the form studied (e.g. "counselling from a trained adviser") and not be presented as if app content were equivalent.
- Avoid "clinically proven", "scientifically proven to work", "science-backed program". Safer: "built on methods studied in clinical research" plus citation and the no-trial disclosure.
- Own-user data later: "72% of users who finished the program were smoke-free at 30 days" conveys a typical-result claim to ordinary users. Under the net-impression test and §255.2-style reasoning, it needs the denominator disclosed adjacent to the number (how many started, how many finished, self-report, no control group), or better, an intention-to-treat figure. Self-selected completers are survivorship bias; the FTC treats the headline, not the footnote, as the claim.

### Gaps
- The exact guidance text could not be opened; all [R] quotations need checking against the PDF.
- Not verified this session (recalled only, check ftc.gov): Carrot Neurotechnology/Ultimeyes (2015), Aura Labs Instant Blood Pressure app (2016), Pact fitness app (2017), the April 2023 Notices of Penalty Offenses on substantiation sent to roughly 670 companies, Operation AI Comply (Sept 2024, AI-capability claims, not health-specific), and older smoking-cessation product cases. No source was retrieved for any of them.
- No FTC action specifically against a quit-smoking app was found.

## 2. Apple App Store rules

### Takeaway
The written guidelines contain no explicit "citations" sentence; the citation requirement is reviewer practice under 1.4.1, and it is enforced in-app. A quit-nicotine app in Health & Fitness must also declare regulated medical device status (new apps since 26 Mar 2026, not 2025 as assumed in the brief).

### Cited Findings
- 1.4.1: "Medical apps that could provide inaccurate data or information, or that could be used for diagnosing or treating patients may be reviewed with greater scrutiny." and "Apps should remind users to check with a doctor in addition to using the app and before making medical decisions." [V] — [App Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 1.4.3: "Apps that encourage consumption of tobacco and vape products, illegal drugs, or excessive amounts of alcohol are not permitted." [V] — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 2.3: metadata must "accurately reflect the app's core experience". 2.3.1(a): "marketing your app in a misleading way... whether within or outside of the App Store, is grounds for removal". 2.3.7: subtitles "should not include... unverifiable product claims". [V] — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 3.1.2(a): "Apps that attempt to scam users will be removed... This includes apps that attempt to trick users into purchasing a subscription under false pretenses". 3.1.2(c): "Before asking a customer to subscribe, you should clearly describe what the user will get for the price." [V] — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- 5.1.3(i): health data may not be used "for advertising, marketing, or other use-based data mining purposes other than improving health management, or for the purpose of health research, and then only with permission". 5.1.3(ii): no "personal health information in iCloud". [V] — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)
- Citations: the fetched guideline text (first 100k characters, through section 5.1) has no sentence requiring citations. Developer reports show reviewers rejecting under 1.4.1 and asking that "links to the sources should be easily accessible to the user when they receive in-app medical recommendations", with citations expected inside the app from reputable scholarly sources. [S] — [Apple Developer Forums thread 703502](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/703502); [thread 708478](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/708478)
- A wellness-only app was still flagged as providing "medical-related data, health-related measurements, diagnoses or treatment advice without appropriate regulatory clearance". [S] — [Forums thread 807508](https://developer.apple.com/forums/thread/807508)
- Regulated medical device status (26 Mar 2026): required for apps distributed in the EEA, UK or US whose "primary or secondary category is Health & Fitness or Medical" or that declare frequent medical/treatment references in the age rating. "Starting today, this status is required for new apps"; existing apps "by early 2027"; "If your app is not a regulated medical device, you can select No." The status shows on the product page. [V] — [Apple news](https://developer.apple.com/news/?id=nyqbfz1y); [help page](https://developer.apple.com/help/app-store-connect/manage-app-information/declare-regulated-medical-device-status)

### Inferences
- Every statistic in onboarding should carry a visible in-app source (author/body, year, tappable reference list), not only a website link. This satisfies Apple and also India's ASCI 1.2 (section 5).
- Declaring "No" for medical device while the listing says "treats nicotine addiction" creates an inconsistency Apple can act on under 2.3.1 and regulators can use as evidence of intended purpose.
- Onboarding quiz answers about smoking are health data; using them for ad targeting conflicts with 5.1.3(i).

### Gaps
- No documented rejection of a quit-smoking app specifically was found. The last 14k characters of the guidelines and their "last updated" date were not read.

## 3. When a quit-nicotine app becomes a medical device

### Takeaway
In every market the test is intended purpose as shown by claims. "Helps you build quit habits, track progress, learn about cravings" stays wellness; "treats nicotine addiction/dependence", "therapy", or a product-specific "increases your chance of quitting" asserts a medical purpose. Germany proves the point: quit-smoking apps making treatment claims are CE-marked DiGA.

### Cited Findings
- US: FDA's device software functions policy lists for enforcement discretion "software functions that provide periodic educational information, reminders, or motivational guidance to" smokers trying to quit and people recovering from addiction. FDA can withdraw discretion. [S] — [FDA Device Software Functions](https://www.fda.gov/mobile-medical-applications); [NIH SEED summary](https://seed.nih.gov/sites/default/files/2024-12/FDAs-Enforcement-Discretion-Policy.pdf)
- US: General Wellness guidance revised and reissued final on 6 Jan 2026, superseding Sept 2019; two-factor framework kept (general wellness use only, low risk); definition unchanged but more products may fit; firms disagree on how big the change is. The line is crossed when claims move toward diagnosis, treatment or disease management. [S] — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/news-and-insights/insights/2026/01/fda-issues-revised-guidance-on-general-wellness-products); [Ropes & Gray](https://www.ropesgray.com/en/insights/alerts/2026/01/fda-adapts-with-the-times-on-digital-health-updated-guidances-on-general-wellness-products); [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download)
- US: second wellness category permits claims relating "the role of healthy lifestyle with helping to reduce the risk or impact of certain chronic diseases or conditions and where it is well understood and accepted that healthy lifestyle choices may play an important role in health outcomes". [R] — [FDA guidance](https://www.fda.gov/media/90652/download)
- US: FDA lists approved/cleared cessation products (NRT and others) and a searchable device classification for "smoking cessation"; Carrot's Pivot programme paired coaching with an FDA-cleared CO sensor (2017). [S] — [FDA consumer update](https://www.fda.gov/consumers/consumer-updates/want-quit-smoking-fda-approved-and-fda-cleared-cessation-products-can-help); [CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2017/10/03/fda-approves-first-device-app-to-help-quit-smoking-carrot.html)
- EU: MDCG 2019-11 rev.1 published June 2025 is current; qualification "is driven by the intended medical purpose expressed through labels, instructions, promotional material, technical documentation and the software's actual functions". [S] — [EC MDCG guidance list](https://health.ec.europa.eu/medical-devices-sector/new-regulations/guidance-mdcg-endorsed-documents-and-other-guidance_en); [GMED](https://lne-gmed.com/news/revision-mdcg-2019-11-qualification-classification-software-mdr-ivdr)
- EU: MDR Recital 19: software "intended for life-style and well-being purposes is not a medical device". MDR Art. 2(1) covers software intended for "diagnosis, prevention, monitoring, prediction, prognosis, treatment or alleviation of disease". [R] — [Regulation (EU) 2017/745](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2017/745/oj)
- EU: MDR Art. 7 prohibits misleading claims in device labelling and advertising; claims must stay within the documented intended purpose. [S] — [CMS Germany](https://cms.law/de/deu/legal-updates/preventive-healthcare-vermarkten-starke-claims-statt-abmahnrisiko)
- Germany DiGA: NichtraucherHelden permanently listed 30 Jun 2023 for tobacco dependence (F17.2) after provisional listing from Jul 2021; Smoke Free was listed provisionally; these were the only two for this indication as of May 2025. [S] — [PTAheute](https://www.ptaheute.de/aktuelles/2023/08/11/dauerhafte-di-ga-bei-binge-eating-und-tabakabhaengigkeit); [apotheke adhoc](https://www.apotheke-adhoc.de/nachrichten/detail/pharmazie/smoke-free-virtuelles-haustier-zur-rauchentwoehnung/); [KBV](https://www.kbv.de/praxis/tools-und-services/praxisnachrichten/2025/05-15/G-BA%20regelt%20Verordnung%20von%20Arzneimitteln%20zur%20Tabakentw%C3%B6hnung)
- Australia: Therapeutic Goods (Excluded Goods) Determination 2018, item 14A excludes software "intended for self-management of an existing disease or condition that is not serious"; "self-management" means enabling "healthy lifestyle choices... that are medically accepted to help reduce the risk or impact of an existing disease or condition"; representations must not stray outside excluded functions. [S] — [TGA excluded software](https://www.tga.gov.au/resources/resource/guidance/excluded-software); [HSF](https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2021-06/regulatory-reform-for-software-based-consumer-health-can-rules-keep-up-with-reality)

### Inferences
- Wording that pushes over the line: "treats/cures nicotine addiction", "nicotine dependence therapy", "clinically proven to help you quit", "DayXero doubles your chance of quitting", dosing or tapering instructions for NRT personalised to the user (therapy decisions, Rule 11).
- Wording that stays wellness: "a program to help you plan, track and stay motivated", "learn techniques researchers have studied for handling cravings", "educational; not a medical device; does not diagnose or treat any condition; talk to a doctor or pharmacist about stop-smoking medicines".
- Describing NRT evidence is educational; telling a user which NRT product and dose to take is not.

### Gaps
- MHRA's software/app guidance could not be retrieved; no sourced statement on UK treatment of cessation apps. (Recalled only: MHRA looks at medical-purpose words such as "treat", "diagnose", "monitor" in app-store copy.)
- Whether nicotine dependence counts as "not serious" for Australia's item 14A was not established.
- Device class of the German DiGA apps not confirmed. Full text of FDA's 2026 guidance not read; no smoking-specific example confirmed in it.

## 4. UK ASA/CAP, EU UCPD, Germany, Australia, India

### Takeaway
The UK is the strictest written regime on cessation wording: CAP treats product claims to help people stop smoking as medicinal (rule 12.11), demands proof for any "without effort" implication (12.8), and warns against "x% success rate" claims without rigorous substantiation. Germany's HWG adds a ban on implying success is certain.

### Cited Findings
- CAP rule 12.1: "Objective claims must be backed by evidence, if relevant consisting of trials conducted on people. Substantiation will be assessed on the basis of the available scientific knowledge." [S] — [CAP Code section 12 PDF](https://www.asa.org.uk/static/23b1dcc3-b62f-422f-a8871775456040d3/cap-code-12-medicines.pdf)
- CAP rule 3.7: marketers "must hold documentary evidence to prove claims that consumers are likely to regard as objective and that are capable of objective substantiation. The ASA may regard claims as misleading in the absence of adequate substantiation." [R] — [ASA substantiation advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/substantiation.html). Confirmed in substance: objective claims without adequate evidence are likely ruled misleading even if intended as puffery, and "Self-reported consumer data or other subjective data will not be sufficient". [S] — same link
- CAP advice "Health: Smoking, Stopping": "The ASA and CAP are yet to see evidence that any product is effective at helping people quit smoking without any effort on the part of the smoker (rule 12.8)"; claims like "Stop Smoking the easy way" or "guaranteed success" likely breach; marketers "should avoid making specific claims such as 'x% success rate' without rigorous substantiation". [S] — [ASA advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/health-smoking-stopping.html)
- Same advice: "Products claiming to help people stop smoking are regarded as medicinal, and marketers must hold a relevant marketing authorisation from the MHRA (rule 12.11)". For hypnotherapy, ASA/CAP "have generally accepted that hypnotherapy can potentially help support smokers", but practitioners "should avoid making success rate claims without robust substantiation". [S] — [ASA advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/health-smoking-stopping.html); [CAP news](https://www.asa.org.uk/news/quit-non-compliance-filtered-tips-for-stop-smoking-ads.html)
- Testimonials: "Testimonials alone do not constitute substantiation"; a ruling on BCCR Ltd (2025) found testimonials about quitting smoking and other conditions breached rules 3.1, 3.7 and 12.1 because they implied efficacy. [S] — [ASA health substantiation advice](https://www.asa.org.uk/advice-online/substantiation-for-health-beauty-and-slimming-claims.html); [BCCR ruling](https://www.asa.org.uk/rulings/bccr-ltd-a25-1297374-bccr-ltd.html)
- E-cigarette precedent: VPZ ruling (2023) treated claims that a product could help users quit or cut down as medicinal claims. [S] — [Planet of the Vapes report](https://www.planetofthevapes.co.uk/news/vaping-news/2023-02-20_asa-finds-against-vpz.html)
- EU UCPD (Directive 2005/29/EC) Annex I point 17 blacklists "Falsely claiming that a product is able to cure illnesses, dysfunction or malformations"; Arts. 6 and 7 cover misleading actions and omissions. [R] — [Directive 2005/29/EC](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2005/29/oj)
- Germany HWG §3: misleading advertising is prohibited for medicines, devices and any other means or procedures to which therapeutic effects are attributed; it is misleading to create the impression that success can be expected with certainty (§3 sentence 2 no. 2a). Health advertising is assessed under HWG and UWG §5 in parallel. [S] — [Händlerbund](https://www.haendlerbund.de/de/ratgeber/recht/3881-heilmittelwerbegesetz); [RA Plutte](https://www.ra-plutte.de/gesundheitsbezogene-werbung/)
- Australia: where software is excluded from TGA regulation, representations must stay within the excluded functions. [S] — [HSF](https://www.hsfkramer.com/insights/2021-06/regulatory-reform-for-software-based-consumer-health-can-rules-keep-up-with-reality)
- India ASCI Code Chapter I: 1.1 claims "should be capable of substantiation"; 1.2 "Where advertising claims are expressly stated to be based on or supported by independent research or assessment, the source and date of this should be indicated in the advertisement." [R] — [ASCI Code](https://www.ascionline.in/the-asci-code/)

### Inferences
- UK copy should describe DayXero as support for the user's own quit attempt (behavioural/wellness service), never as a product that makes people stop. The hypnotherapy line of advice is the closest analogue for a non-medicinal behavioural service: support claims tolerated, success rates not.
- A pre-launch "x% success rate" is unusable in the UK. A later own-data figure based on self-report alone is likely insufficient per the "self-reported consumer data" advice; biochemical verification or an independent study would be needed for an objective quit-rate claim.
- For India, date and source on screen is an explicit requirement, so "Source: Cochrane Review, 2023" style captions should be standard everywhere.
- Comparatives such as "X times more likely than willpower alone" are objective claims needing evidence matching the exact comparison (same comparator, same outcome, same time point) and must be attributed to the studied intervention.

### Gaps
- Exact text of CAP 12.8, 12.11 and the testimonial rules not verified; CAP Code rule numbering may have shifted with the 2025 DMCC-related update. No ASA ruling against a stop-smoking app found.
- Not sourced this session: Australia's Therapeutic Goods Advertising Code 2021 and ACCC/Australian Consumer Law provisions; India's CCPA 2022 misleading-ads guidelines; HWG §11 testimonial limits and the §12 annex; German case law on the "strict standard" for health advertising.

## 5. Subscription and dark-pattern rules when statistics sit next to a paywall

### Takeaway
The FTC click-to-cancel rule is not in force (vacated July 2025; new rulemaking only at ANPRM stage as of March 2026), but ROSCA, FTC Act section 5 and Apple 3.1.2 apply now. A misleading statistic on the screen before the paywall is treated as inducing the subscription.

### Cited Findings
- The Eighth Circuit vacated the 2024 click-to-cancel amendments on 8 Jul 2025 on procedural grounds; FTC restored the pre-2024 Negative Option Rule in Feb 2026; ANPRM announced 11 Mar 2026, published 13 Mar 2026, proposing no specific requirements yet. FTC "still retains its authority under ROSCA and the FTC Act". [S] — [Covington](https://www.cov.com/en/news-and-insights/insights/2026/03/ftc-launches-new-rulemaking-on-the-negative-option-rule); [Crowell](https://www.crowell.com/en/insights/client-alerts/clicking-all-the-right-boxes-ftc-moves-to-revive-click-to-cancel-rule-following-eighth-circuit-vacatur); [Sidley](https://www.sidley.com/en/insights/newsupdates/2026/02/us-ftc-signals-renewed-interest-in-click-to-cancel-rulemaking)
- FTC and states sued Hims & Hers over privacy, billing and subscription practices (allegations unproven). [S] — [Frier Levitt](https://www.frierlevitt.com/articles/ftc-lawsuit-hims-hers-telehealth-privacy-compliance/)
- Apple 3.1.2(a) and (c) as quoted in section 2. [V] — [Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)

### Inferences
- Avoid: personalised "your success probability: 87%" computed from quiz answers with no validated model; countdown timers and fake scarcity beside health statistics; a statistic whose qualifier only appears after purchase; "guaranteed" quitting.
- Price, renewal period and trial terms must be on the paywall itself; the disclosure for any statistic must be on the same screen as the statistic.

### Gaps
- Status of the FTC rulemaking after March 2026, UK DMCC subscription regime start date, and state auto-renewal laws were not verified.

## 6. Practical wording guide (synthesis of the above; researcher's inferences)

| Do not say | Why | Safer alternative |
|---|---|---|
| "Clinically proven to help you quit" | Establishment claim; no product trial (FTC); medicinal claim (CAP 12.11); device claim | "Built around methods studied in clinical research. DayXero itself has not been tested in a clinical trial." |
| "Users are 3x more likely to quit" | Product efficacy with no data | "In trials, people who combined stop-smoking medicine with behavioural support were more likely to quit than those who tried unaided [source, year]. Those studies did not test this app." |
| "Treats nicotine addiction" | Medical purpose (MDR, FDA, MHRA, HWG) | "Helps you plan your quit, track progress and practise craving techniques" |
| "Quit the easy way / guaranteed" | CAP 12.8; HWG §3 no. 2a | "Quitting takes effort. This program gives you structure and tools." |
| "94% success rate" | CAP advice; FTC typicality | No rate until robust data exists; then show denominator, method, time point |
| "72% of finishers were smoke-free at 30 days" in headline, caveats in footnote | Survivorship bias; net impression | "Of 1,000 people who started, 310 finished; 223 of those reported not smoking at day 30 (self-reported, no comparison group)." |

Placement rules: source and year on the same screen as the number; tappable in-app reference list; "not a medical device, not medical advice, talk to a doctor or pharmacist" reminder in onboarding and listing; identical claims across app, listing, ads and website.
