# Keep the Crew, fix the contradictions

DayXero's Screen Lab (69 screens, 275 states) shows the best-looking and kindest quit-nicotine app in its category, wrapped around a set of promises that its own screens break. The roughly 25 coloured, character-led screens (`onb-welcome`, `onb-quit-day`, `today`, `sos`, `sos-passed`, `evening`, `checkin-form`, `pledge`, `finale`) form one convincing system that no competitor found in this research has; the slip flow, the fairness architecture (free prep week, trial on Quit Day, nothing ever resets) and the 30-beat session kit are genuinely rare. But the **39 cream "task" screens** do not share that system, the **12 colour grounds behave as three**, Xero does not visibly grow for 365 days, and `milestone` is one template repeated eleven times. More serious than any pixel is that the app contradicts itself where trust is built: `onb-baseline-summary` says the brain "steps down" in prep week while `onb-welcome` says use as normal; `onb-trial` promises "two nudges… That's it" while `notifications` ships four on by default; `today` shows "DAY 3" and "DAY 2" on one screen; `pledge` asks prep-week users not to use; the lab's first-run `paywall` sells a 3-day trial on 30 Oct that the Swift code withholds until Quit Day, leaving a monthly-plus-lifetime screen with no anchor; and **no privacy-policy link exists anywhere in the app**, which Apple requires. The program spine is evidence-consistent (set date, abrupt cessation, ACT craving skills, NRT, shame-free slips) but its NRT copy discourages the best-evidenced regimen, missed prep sessions silently push Quit Day back, and the first $39.99 charge lands on day 4 of withdrawal. The order of work is therefore: fix the promise breaks and launch blockers (mostly copy and one URL), then move the trial to 7 days ending after "One week clear", then cull the palette and rebuild the cream screens on the coloured-screen system, and only then invest in character growth and motion.

Screenshot and contact-sheet paths are relative to `/home/user/DAYXERO/research_notes/DayXero app design critique/` (`shots/<id>--<state>--light.png`, `sheets/states-<id>.png`). Web claims about competitors rest on search snippets and vendor pages (apps.apple.com, Mobbin and most benchmark hosts were unreachable from the research session) and are flagged where they are thin.

## What should stick: the Crew-led screens, the slip flow and the fairness rules

The thing to protect is the system visible on the coloured screens. `finale`, `onb-welcome`, `sos`, `sos-passed`, `evening`, `checkin-form`, `onb-quit-day` (including its `calendar` state), `pledge`, the path half of `program`, the day-3 hero and ocean card on `today`, `log-slip`, `onb-baseline-summary` and `changing-detail` share one grammar: a hero scene, a Bricolage title with one marked word, cards, and a **58 px navy pill with a lip shadow**, on 20–24 px gutters and 18–24 px radii ([shots/finale--light.png](shots/finale--light.png), [shots/sos--light.png](shots/sos--light.png), [elements.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/elements.js)). `finale` in particular, three characters, confetti in six token colours, three stat tiles, one health card, two actions and nothing redundant, is the single most "brand" image in the set and the one a reviewer will screenshot. Every ink and muted token passes WCAG AA on every ground in both modes (**tightest pair is ocean muted at 5.25:1**; dark-mode ink is ≥14.2:1 everywhere) ([contrast.js](contrast.js)). The Bricolage Grotesque 800 / DM Sans pairing is the most mature decision in the app: two families, within Apple's "minimize the number of typefaces" advice, with Bricolage correctly confined to ≥19 px where a display face belongs, and the 24–32 px numerals in stat tiles ("$2,555", "31 · 61 · 26") are the clearest hierarchy on any screen ([Apple HIG, Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography), [shots/onb-baseline-summary--light.png](shots/onb-baseline-summary--light.png)).

The Crew is the differentiator. Xero (sun-egg), Jelli (jellyfish) and Glim (firefly) each own a part of the day and hand over explicitly ("Xero passed you to Jelli" on `sos`, the hand-back row on `sos-passed`); the faces are restrained (dot eyes, small mouth, no exclamation marks), the horizon is layered paper-cut with grain, and the SOS breathing ring, the Jelli pulse and the hold-to-commit fills are the only animations tied to a bodily rhythm or a user action, which is exactly the motion to keep ([art.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/art.js), [lab.css](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/lab.css)). No quit-smoking competitor surfaced in this research has a named character: Smoke Free is described as "utilitarian", Kwit's design rated 3.5/5, and the 2025–26 vaping wave (Vapover, FREED, Expire, Quitory, Nixd, Velm) presents as dark dashboards with an SOS button ([heycravo review, competitor-authored](https://heycravo-web.pages.dev/blog/best-quit-smoking-apps-2026/), [jumpgrowth](https://jumpgrowth.com/?p=12433), [Vapover](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id6757370777)).

The copy on the hard moments is the product's voice and should not be touched. "Don't quit today. Quit on your Quit Day." (`onb-welcome`), "Every attempt is data, not failure." (`onb-past-attempts`), "Most cravings pass within a few minutes. Ride it with me." (`sos`), "You rode it out." (`sos-passed`), "Thanks for telling me. … Nothing resets" (`log-slip`), "No judgement, just the facts so we can plan." (`log-slip-what`), "A quiet day. Those count too." and "Today was hard. You're still here." (`checkin-form`), "Free until Tue 10 Nov, then $39.99 a year. We'll remind you 2 days before." (`paywall-plans`), "Always free: your counter and Craving SOS." (`offer`) ([lab: sos.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/sos.js), [lab: evening.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/evening.js), [lab: paywall.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/paywall.js)). The slip flow (`log-slip` → `log-slip-saved` → optional `log-slip-what`) is the best-aligned part of the product with the relapse literature: Marlatt's abstinence-violation effect is driven by "internal attribution of blame, reduced abstinence self-efficacy, and feelings of guilt", and the flow answers each with an external attribution ("What set it off?"), an immediate self-efficacy repair ("Now you've got a plan for this one.") and an if-then plan ([PMC3296289](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3296289)). The SOS design, acceptance over avoidance with no countdown, is the pattern that won in the only large app RCT with a positive result: iCanQuit's ACT approach beat the avoidance-based NCI QuitGuide on 12-month 30-day abstinence, **28.2% vs 21.1%, OR 1.49** ([Contextual Science summary](https://contextualscience.org/publications/efficacy_smartphone_applications_smoking_cessation)). Every competitor SOS found uses a countdown or fixed delay (Expire 60 s, quitOS 90 s, FREED "a clear countdown"); DayXero's count-up is the one SOS detail nobody else has ([Expire](https://mwm.ai/apps/expire-stop-smoking/6779735826), [FREED](https://apps.apple.com/app/id6759827720)).

The fairness architecture is the invisible moat. Billing traps and progress resets are the dominant one-star themes across Smoke Free, Kwit and Puff Count ([App Imp Smoke Free](https://appimp.app/apps/smoke-free-quit-smoking-now), [Kimola Kwit](https://kimola.com/reports/unlock-insights-kwit-smoking-app-review-analysis-app-store-fr-157159), [marlvel Puff Count](https://marlvel.ai/apps/puff-count-quit-vaping-now)); DayXero answers both by rule: prep week and Day Xero free (`FreeTier.isFree` in `ProgramEngine.swift`), exact charge dates on `onb-reveal`, `paywall-plans` and `offer`, a "How billing works" sheet with the reportaproblem.apple.com refund route, restore/failed/pending states all drawn, and "A logged slip has no effect on the streak or progress visual" locked in the handoff ([/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/ProgramEngine.swift](/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/ProgramEngine.swift), [/home/user/DAYXERO/docs/DAYXERO_V1_HANDOFF.md](/home/user/DAYXERO/docs/DAYXERO_V1_HANDOFF.md)). Price prominence already meets Apple's rule (the annual total is the largest element, the per-month breakdown subordinate) ([Apple, Auto-renewable subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/)). The state coverage across `onb-welcome` restore, `paywall` loading/notice/unlocked/pending/failed, `notifications` denied, `nrt-taper` no-fit and `sos` no-reason is above average for a pre-launch build and should stay. Finally, the Me-area trust surfaces (no account, local-first, "Export my data", "Delete all data" with a correct destructive alert, a Privacy page naming PostHog and RevenueCat, EU opt-in analytics, widget privacy mode) are unusually honest for the category and need only the one URL discussed below.

## Colour and type: twelve grounds behave like three

The palette is coherent as an identity and incoherent as a code. From the lab registry, **39 of 69 screens sit on `reading` cream**, lilac takes 8 (the onboarding chat), violet 6, apricot 4, mint 4, sun 3, and coral, ocean, sky, lime and periwinkle appear on **one screen each**; mist is never a screen ground at all ([shots/_screens.json](shots/_screens.json)). A section-colour code that appears once cannot be learned, and the cool grounds cannot even be told apart: mist vs periwinkle **1.18:1**, sky vs mist 1.17:1, mint vs lime 1.13:1 ([contrast.js](contrast.js)); on the Calm sheet, `calm` (periwinkle) and the patterns card on `progress` (mist) read as the same colour ([sheets/sheet-calm-light.png](sheets/sheet-calm-light.png), [shots/progress--light--full.png](shots/progress--light--full.png)). What actually works is colour owned by a character: ocean is SOS, the SOS tab, the coffee-wave card and Jelli, and nothing else; violet is Glim; apricot is Xero. Mint for Program, lime for the body timeline and periwinkle for Calm have no character and no recurring icon, so nothing teaches them. Collapse to about six grounds: keep the warm family (coral/apricot/sun), the deep family (ocean/violet), reading cream and one chat pastel (lilac); drop mist, periwinkle and lime as screen grounds (keep them as card tints if needed).

Green does double duty and it costs the Today screen its call to action. #0F6E43 is the Program path colour, the universal "done" tick, the onboarding progress bar, the selected-answer state in sessions and the "today's session" action card on `today`, so the main CTA on the home screen looks like a completed state ([shots/today--light--full.png](shots/today--light--full.png), [sheets/states-session-kit.png](sheets/states-session-kit.png)). Apple's rule is blunt: "Avoid using the same color to mean different things" ([Apple HIG, Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). Reserve green for done/progress, and make the Today action card apricot (Xero's colour).

Two contrast failures are real accessibility bugs rather than taste. Coral #FF7A55 is used as *text* for the 12 px kickers "TODAY · 6 MIN" on `program` and "FROM QUIT DAY" on `changing`, at **2.41:1 on cream** ([shots/program--light--full.png](shots/program--light--full.png), [shots/changing--light--full.png](shots/changing--light--full.png)); cream on coral is 2.44:1 and cream on apricot **1.48:1**, which matters for the white "S" avatar chip and sparkles on the apricot `today` hero. Coral stays as the decorative mark; as text it becomes navy or the ground's muted token, with a standing rule that nothing cream sits on coral or apricot. Second, the translucent cards barely separate from their grounds: the 70% cream card is 1.16:1 on lime and 1.20:1 on sun, the 20% navy card on violet is **1.13:1**, and the 10% white card in dark mode is 1.27–1.35 on every ground, so on `checkin-form` the "How are you feeling tonight?" card is a faint rectangle ([shots/checkin-form--light.png](shots/checkin-form--light.png), [contrast.js](contrast.js)). Raise card alpha or add a one-pixel hairline so cards hit ≥1.3:1; Apple notes that "In bright surroundings, colors look darker and more muted" ([Apple HIG, Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)), and these cards vanish in sunlight. Ocean (5.98/5.25) and coral (6.46/6.01) also miss Apple's "strive for 7:1" target for custom colours, which is worth knowing when picking which grounds survive the cull ([Apple HIG, Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)).

Dark mode is a real design on the coloured screens (plum #241433 replaces the warm grounds, primaries flip to apricot-on-plum, `onb-reveal` goes deep green, and the reading surfaces #0E0B2B/#1B1747/#2A255F give the base/elevated separation Apple describes) but three things break it ([sheets/sheet-onboarding-dark.png](sheets/sheet-onboarding-dark.png), [sheets/sheet-sessions-dark.png](sheets/sheet-sessions-dark.png)). The full-brightness peach dawn scene sits unchanged above a plum body on `onb-welcome`, `onb-quit-day`, `pledge`, `milestone` and `finale`, roughly 40% of the screen, where Apple asks for "separate light and dark assets" when one looks good in only one mode ([shots/onb-welcome--dark.png](shots/onb-welcome--dark.png), [Apple HIG, Dark Mode](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/dark-mode)). `water`, `calm-box`, `calm-478` and the `session-kit` breathe beat still render on pure #000 with an olive ring and a khaki "Coach me" / "Log 8 fl oz" button that exists in no token, a leftover of the Biophilic OLED system the tokens file says v4 replaced ([shots/water--light.png](shots/water--light.png), [shots/calm-box--light.png](shots/calm-box--light.png), [/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/tokens.json](/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/tokens.json)). And 14 Settings/Me sheets (`settings`, `settings-products`, `settings-product-editor`, `settings-currency`, `settings-quit-day`, `settings-subscription`, `settings-privacy`, `settings-health`, `notifications`, `notifications-quiet-hours`, `nrt-step-editor`, `water-goal`, plus `log-menu`, `paywall-billing`, `milestone-share`) are drawn over a flat black band in light mode because the lab models the dimmed parent as a colour; this is a prototype artefact, but it means the most-visited utility area has never been reviewed as it will actually look ([sheets/sheet-me-light.png](sheets/sheet-me-light.png)). Fix the lab to render `today` dimmed behind sheets before judging any of them.

Typography has a scale problem, not a face problem. Across the 69 default screens, **14 px appears 334 times, 12 px 253 times and 16 px only 215 times**, so 14 px has become the de facto body and 12 px caps kickers are so frequent they create no hierarchy ([shots/_audit.json](shots/_audit.json)). Apple's Body is 17 pt and its platform default 17, with Footnote at 13 ([Apple HIG, Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)); DayXero's card bodies, baseline explanation cards and session option rows all sit at 14 and feel smaller than the iOS around them. `today` in its `day3` state stacks eight 12 px kickers in one viewport ([shots/today--day3--light.png](shots/today--day3--light.png)). Make 16 (or 17) the floor for any sentence a user must read, reserve 14 for metadata, allow one kicker per card and none on list rows. Display tracking is **−1.2 px at 40 px** where SF Pro uses +0.37 pt at the same size, so "Morning, Sam" reads tighter than the status bar above it and collides with the hand-drawn circle; loosen to about −0.6/−0.4 ([Apple HIG, Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)). Nothing in 275 states demonstrates Dynamic Type; every size is a fixed px value and the 58 px buttons, 70 px tab bar with 12 px labels and 40 px segmented tabs will all need to grow. Apple treats this as the price of a custom font: "If you use a custom font, make sure it implements the same behaviors" ([Apple HIG, Typography](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/typography)). Add an xxxLarge/AX3 state for `today`, `sos`, one session beat and `settings` to the lab. Two duplicated titles are trivial fixes: `lesson-audio` shows "The noisy passengers" twice, and `milestone` day-1 shows "1 Quit Day" then "Quit Day" ([shots/lesson-audio--light.png](shots/lesson-audio--light.png), [shots/milestone--light.png](shots/milestone--light.png)).

The tab bar is the one component that will date the app on day one. It is a custom 96%-opaque white capsule, 70 px high, floating 26 px from the bottom ([elements.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/elements.js)). iOS 26's Liquid Glass "forms a distinct functional layer for controls and navigation elements — like tab bars and sidebars — that floats above the content layer", the standard tab bar picks it up automatically on rebuild, and practitioners report custom recreations lack the glass blob because it depends on a private API ([Apple HIG, Materials](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/materials), [MacStories iOS 26 review](https://www.macstories.net/stories/ios-and-ipados-26-the-macstories-review/3)). Apple also advises that "If your app features colorful backgrounds… prefer a monochromatic appearance for toolbars and tab bars" ([Apple HIG, Color](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/color)). Adopt the native `TabView` with monochrome inactive icons and SOS expressed through its symbol and tint; the platform is moving exactly where DayXero's coloured grounds want it to. Keep glass structural and off the saturated SOS screen, where NN/g's readability critique of translucent overlays would bite ([NN/g summary via anderegg.ca](https://anderegg.ca/2025/10/12/nielsen-norman-group-on-ios-26-usability)).

## Thirty-nine cream screens, a nine-section Today and one milestone template

The cream task screens do not use the system the coloured screens established, and they are where a user spends most of their minutes. Session beats (`session` W1D2.1–2.3, `session-kit` quiz, choice, slider, commit, nrt-choice, and `calm-anchor`, `calm-notice`, `checkin-dayend`, `log-slip-what`) put a question and two or three option rows under a progress bar, pin "Continue" to the bottom and leave **450–550 px of blank cream** in between; `session-mission` is a single apricot card floating mid-screen and `session-kit` commit-done is the word "Committed." alone in the middle of the screen ([sheets/states-session.png](sheets/states-session.png), [sheets/states-session-kit.png](sheets/states-session-kit.png)). Motion volume is inverted against task weight: `finale` carries 27 SMIL animations and `changing` 25, while the heaviest task screens carry zero and feel static ([shots/_audit.json](shots/_audit.json)). The fix is to borrow from the coloured screens rather than redesign: give every beat an 88 px character or kit tile at the top, anchor the question in the top third, and let "Continue" sit directly under the last option when content is short. Three competing button systems also live on cream, the navy pill, the small white "▶ Start" plus khaki "Coach me" pills on the black Calm canvases, and the khaki full-width "+ Log 8 fl oz" on `water` ([sheets/sheet-calm-light.png](sheets/sheet-calm-light.png)); rebuilding Calm, Water and the breathe beat on the reading tokens removes the third visual language and the last OLED remnant in one move.

`today` is dense in a way that competes with itself. The full prep state stacks a hero with three stat tiles, a green action card, a three-row "Your day" list, two log tiles with four buttons, prep-streak dots, an NRT card and a health card, eight modules of which two (the action card and "Your day" row 2) say the same thing; the lab's `today.js` returns nine sections in one scroll, and `day3` adds the ocean coffee-wave card above the green one so two coloured CTAs fight ([shots/today--light--full.png](shots/today--light--full.png), [shots/today--day3--light.png](shots/today--day3--light.png), [lab: today.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/today.js)). Finch's loop is praised precisely because the list of today's actions is the whole screen, and its critics say "the home screen can get busy" ([Deconstructor of Fun](https://www.deconstructoroffun.com/blog/x0hd2ssr80y5n7gv0w967pg7hwd7tl)). Cut to five modules on a normal day: header, one "Now" card, "Your day", one log row, body change; fold the lesson card and NRT invitation into "Your day" rows and drop the streak card, which duplicates the header's "3 days clear" tile. `program` shows the same seven sessions twice, as nodes on the serpentine path and again as seven day cards below it ([shots/program--light--full.png](shots/program--light--full.png)); the path is the differentiator, so keep it, make nodes tappable and demote the list to completed items. The "TODAY · 6 MIN / The stress loop" label collides with the pulsing play node in the `week1` state ([shots/program--week1--light.png](shots/program--week1--light.png)).

`milestone` is the opposite of a celebration. All eleven states (`quit-day` through `day-365`) share one layout, cheering Xero on dawn, a number, a title, one line, two stat tiles, roughly 380 px of empty sun yellow and "Keep going"; across a year the only visual change is a sun halo appearing from day 30, and Xero is pixel-identical at days 1, 3, 7 and 14 ([sheets/states-milestone.png](sheets/states-milestone.png)). The spec promises four character stages and a sky that "clears as you do" ([/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md) §7), yet the Today hero looks the same in `prep`, `quitday` and `day3` ([shots/today--quitday--light.png](shots/today--quitday--light.png)). This is the gap between DayXero and the companion apps it will be compared to: Finch's bird visibly grows from completed tasks, and the Pratt critique's warning is that "A mascot with no functional logic becomes decoration" ([Pratt IXD](https://ixd.prattsi.org/2026/02/design-critique-finch-self-care-pet-ios-app/)). Make stages 1–4 differ in silhouette (size, posture, an added feature), drive the Today hero's `clarity` parameter from days clear so day 1 and day 28 look different, and make each milestone state differ in art, character present and health fact; fill the yellow void with the share-card preview. Add one "stage sheet" screen to the lab showing stages 1–4 and clarity 20/50/80/100 side by side so the promise can be reviewed before it is built.

Two illustration decisions undercut the Crew. A second, derivative language, the round-headed human figure in an orange jumper (`PersonFigure`), sits on `program`, `changing` and, on a cloud, `calm` ([shots/program--light.png](shots/program--light.png), [shots/calm--light.png](shots/calm--light.png)); this is the Corporate Memphis/Alegria idiom that critics have called "soulless" and "oversaturated to the point of self-cannibalisation" since 2021–23, and it is the one element that would make a 2026 reviewer say "stock illustration" ([Wikipedia, Corporate Memphis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Corporate_Memphis), [Protocol via Slashdot](https://tech.slashdot.org/story/21/08/11/1822225/why-is-tech-illustration-stuck-on-repeat)). Delete it; the Crew are the only people in the app. Second, accent sprawl: `changing` puts at least ten hues of kit tile on a lime ground and `program` adds six more ([shots/changing--light--full.png](shots/changing--light--full.png)); constrain tiles to the warm/deep families so fourteen tiles read as one set. Ration the marked word too: it appears on `today`, `program`, `onb-reveal`, `changing`, `progress` (twice), `calm`, `checkin-plan`, `log-slip` and `milestone` and redraws on every render, so it reads as a template feature rather than a hand ([sheets/sheet-progress-light.png](sheets/sheet-progress-light.png)); use it on moments (reveal, passed, slip-thanks, finale, Quit Day), never on persistent tab titles, and animate it once per session. Cut ambient twinkle on screens where the user is typing (`checkin-form`, `checkin-dayend`, 17 animations each) and the 25 animating health icons on `changing`; the design doc's own rule is "Always slow", and a 1.5–3 s twinkle is not.

Information architecture needs two merges and one route. The tab bar is Today · Program · SOS · Evening · Me, but "Me" opens `progress` ("Your progress") with no way into `settings`, which is reachable only from the 44 pt "S" button on Today's header, despite HomeView's comment that "Me holds Progress and Settings (decision 21)" ([lab: me.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/me.js)). A privacy-forward app that hides Export and Delete behind a header initial undermines its own story. "Evening" duplicates Today's evening state as a whole tab: `today` already switches host, ground and top card by time of day and lists "Evening check-in · 21:00" in "Your day", so at 09:41 the Evening tab shows a night sky and "Wind down with Glim" ([lab: evening.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/evening.js)). Fold Evening into Today, move the wind-down tool list under `calm`, keep `checkin-form`, `checkin-saved` and `evening-breathing` as full-screen flows from Today's evening card and the 21:00 notification, and end with four tabs, Today · Program · SOS · Progress, which is both the handoff's original model and Apple's "fewer tabs" advice ([Apple HIG, Tab bars](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/tab-bars)). SOS in the centre technically breaks Apple's "navigation, not actions" rule but is the one action a quit app must make one tap away; it is reachable in ≤2 taps from everywhere traced, and the only addition needed is a persistent SOS affordance inside the two longest full-screen flows, `session` and `nrt-setup`. `water` and `water-goal` are a second app inside a quit app, a weight-based hydration calculator with four drink types, a custom-amount alert and a forced-black canvas, collecting body weight as new sensitive data with no cessation evidence behind it; the code's own copy rule is that water "gives your hands and mouth something to do; it doesn't flush nicotine or cure cravings" ([/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/Water.swift](/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/Water.swift)). Reduce it to one "Water · 3 of 8" row in "Your day" during Week 1 and delete the goal calculator; if kept, it must follow the app's appearance as `water-goal` already does.

Interaction conventions are mostly right and inconsistently applied. Native alerts are used correctly for delete and for changing Quit Day; the denied-notifications banner deep-links to Settings. But there are **three hold durations** (1 s on `pledge`, 1.5 s on `onb-quit-day`, 3 s for Day Xero), **twelve screens with silent half-strength CTAs** that give no hint which field is missing (`onb-products`, `onb-triggers`, `onb-personal`, `onb-past-attempts`, `onb-nrt`, `onb-building`, `log-slip`, `checkin-form`, `checkin-plan`, `nrt-setup`, `nrt-taper`, `calm-notice`), destructive quiet buttons drawn in ink instead of red ("Remove this step" on `nrt-step-editor`, "Remove the taper" on `nrt-taper`), two different day-end pickers (`checkin-dayend` and `settings-day-end`) with two label formats, sheets without grabbers (`session-mission` follow-ups, `upgrade`), a `notifications` denied state whose toggles still render green, and sub-44 pt targets on `program` segment tabs (40 px), `milestone-share` segments (26 px), `onb-nrt`/`nrt-setup` source pills (26/19 px), `paywall` footer links (17–33 px) and `water-goal` unit toggle (34 px) ([shots/_audit.json](shots/_audit.json), [shots/notifications--denied--light.png](shots/notifications--denied--light.png)). Pick one hold (1 s everywhere, or 1.5 s only for the two commitment moments), show a one-line reason under disabled buttons, use red for destructive, merge the pickers, and give every segmented control a 44 pt hit area.

## Onboarding and copy: six places the app breaks its own promises

The onboarding shape is right and shorter than the field. For a dual user it is 14 screens and 13 counted steps (breathe, welcome, products, two baselines, summary, triggers, personal, past attempts, NRT, Quit Day, building, reveal, reminders), under the Health & Fitness median of about 20.5 screens that Lazyweb reports (vendor data, n=26, read via snippets) and far under Finch's 34 steps with a mid-onboarding paywall ([Lazyweb](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/health-fitness-onboarding-length), [Lazyweb Finch flow](https://experiments.lazyweb.com/flow/finch/onboarding)). Value lands at step 4 (`onb-baseline-summary`), and the `onb-reveal` trial card, "We'll ask you on Quit Day, not before" with exact dates and a cancel path, is better than any competitor pattern found. Keep the shape, keep the hold-to-set gesture on `onb-quit-day` (a commitment ritual with an accessibility fallback), keep the restore outcomes on `onb-welcome`.

What needs to change is that the flow says things the product then contradicts. First, `onb-baseline-summary`, the founder's own linked screen, ends with "Good news: in your prep week your brain steps down from this dose, without the shock of quitting cold turkey today." Two screens earlier `onb-welcome` says "Prep for 7 days while you still use as normal", `onb-quit-day` says "Keep using as normal until then", nothing in the program reduces dose in prep, and the Cochrane review of abrupt versus gradual cessation finds no advantage to stepping down (RR 1.01, 22 trials) ([Cochrane CD008033](https://cochranelibrary.com/cdsr/doi/10.1002/14651858.CD008033.pub4/information)). The line is both false under the protocol and an unsourced health claim on a screen whose other numbers are honest. Replace it with something true: "Nothing changes this week. On Quit Day this is the dose your body stops expecting, and we'll have a plan for every wave." The same screen frames money as "YEARLY DRAIN · $7 gone every day" while `onb-reveal` frames it as "MONEY YOU'LL KEEP IN YEAR 1 $2,555"; the handoff's tone rule is "progress, not guilt", so use the keep-frame on both. "TIME BACK 490 hours" labels time currently lost as a gain. The tray label "YOUR ANSWER" is rendered although nothing is answered (it is the `onbTray` default), on this screen and on `onb-under-age`. Keep the navy "≈ 21 cigarettes a day" equivalence card, the per-product rows and the "Rough estimates" small print; it is the most persuasive screen in the flow because its assumptions are shown, but the constants behind it (1.2 mg absorbed per cigarette, 0.12 mg per 5% puff) carry a code comment "The source link is still to add." ([/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/OnboardingEstimates.swift](/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/OnboardingEstimates.swift)).

Second, `onb-trial` says "Xero says good morning, Glim checks in at night. That's it." The `notifications` default state has four "Your day" items on by default (Morning pledge 08:00, Lesson ready 12:00, Evening check-in 21:00, Milestones 09:00) plus NRT and "When you've been away" notes, and the notifications handoff adds water reminders with their own cap, two Live Activity guards a day and craving heads-ups, so a Week 1 worst case is six to eight interruptions ([lab: me.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/me.js), [/home/user/DAYXERO/docs/DAYXERO_NOTIFICATIONS_WATER_HANDOFF.md](/home/user/DAYXERO/docs/DAYXERO_NOTIFICATIONS_WATER_HANDOFF.md)). Either ship only pledge and check-in on by default and offer "Remind me when the next session opens" in context from `session-done`, which matches Apple's "wait to request permission until people actually use an app feature" ([Apple HIG, Privacy](https://developer.apple.com/design/human-interface-guidelines/privacy)), or rewrite to "two a day to start" and enforce one global cap. The check-in time is also 21:00 on `onb-trial` and `today`, "9:00 PM" on `pledge` and 21:30 in the design-system strings; pick one. Keep the two-button soft ask: Apple's one-button pre-alert rule is scoped to protected-resource alerts, and a decline here preserves the one-shot system prompt for later.

Third, the progress model argues with itself. The bar is computed as index/12, `onb-products` starts at 0.1 and *shrinks* to 0.083 once a product is picked, the baselines carry a nested "Cigarettes · step 1 of 2 / Vape · step 2 of 2", and `onb-reveal` captions the bar "Step 6 of 6", a leftover of the six-step v4 spec ([lab: onboarding.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/onboarding.js)). One total, one bar, computed up front from the products picked.

Fourth, the handoff specifies "one-tap skip where a question is optional" and calls past attempts "short, optional", yet no onboarding screen has a Skip except `onb-breathe` after 10 s; `onb-past-attempts` requires an answer, `onb-personal` requires both a name and a reason, and the obstacles, confidence score and years-of-use collected on `onb-past-attempts`, `onb-baseline-cigarettes` and `onb-baseline-vape` are never shown on any later screen ([/home/user/DAYXERO/docs/DAYXERO_V1_HANDOFF.md](/home/user/DAYXERO/docs/DAYXERO_V1_HANDOFF.md) S2.7, S2.13). Keep the reason required (it is reused on `sos`, `pledge`, `evening`, `finale`), make the name and past attempts skippable, and either feed the unused answers ("You said stress got in the way last time; Prep 4 is the stress loop" on `onb-building`) or delete the questions. Collected-but-unused questions are the cheapest drop-off to remove. `onb-breathe` itself puts a silent 10 s screen with a hidden Skip before any promise; NN/g's 70-participant study found front-loaded instruction made tasks feel harder (ease 4.92 vs 5.49) with no gain in success ([NN/g, Mobile tutorials](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/mobile-tutorials/)). Show Skip at once or move the breath to `sos` and the Day Xero session where the 4/6 rhythm has a job. And `onb-quit-day` forbids any date earlier than seven days out; NICE's advice is to stop "in one go" and nothing in the evidence requires blocking someone who installed the app because they decided to quit this week ([NICE NG209](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG209)). Offer a shorter prep with the 7-day default still recommended.

Fifth, the voice slips. The design system's rule is that characters speak in the first person and "The app never says 'us' on character screens", yet Xero's bubbles read "We'll set up your counters and biology for each one" (`onb-products`), "Tell us about your vape" (`onb-baseline-vape`), "We'll time your prep check-ins and defences" (`onb-triggers`), "When a craving hits, we'll show you this" (`onb-personal`), "Tell us what tripped you up so we can block it" (`onb-past-attempts`), "We'll suggest talking to a pharmacist" (`onb-nrt`); only `onb-quit-day` ("I recommend Fri 6 Nov") is first person ([/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md) §8). "Dual use detected" is surveillance tone where the approved line is "Using both is common. I'll plan for each, so neither sneaks back in." "TOTAL DAILY NICOTINE LOAD", "what your body processes" and "biology" are clinical where the rest of the app is plain. The "wave" metaphor appears first as "Waves to watch" on `onb-building` and is never defined anywhere; one line in Xero's voice there ("I call cravings waves. They rise, peak and pass, usually in a few minutes. Jelli will ride them with you.") makes every later use land. Plurals are missing ("In 1 days" on `settings-quit-day`, "1 weeks" on `nrt-taper`), the W1D2.3 quiz says "Right." on wrong answers, and metric labels drift against the one-label rule: "days nicotine-free" on `milestone-share`, "Smoke-free · 3 days" on `today`, "DAY 30" above "31 days" on `finale`, and finale tiles suffixed "· Xero / · Jelli / · Glim" that read as typos.

Sixth, and most damaging to the daily loop, `today` in `day3` shows "SUN 8 NOV · DAY 3" in the header and "TODAY · DAY 2 · 3 MIN · The noisy passengers" on the lesson card, because days clear count Quit Day as Day 1 while sessions are labelled one day later; the design system already names this ("Two day counts on one screen will confuse people") ([shots/today--day3--light.png](shots/today--day3--light.png), [/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/ProgramUnlock.swift](/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/ProgramUnlock.swift)). `pledge` in its `prep` state uses the same constant as after Quit Day, "Today, I won't use nicotine.", while prep's promise is "use as normal" ([lab: evening.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/evening.js)); give prep its own wording ("Today, I'll use with my eyes open", Prep 3's own title) or hide the pledge until Quit Day. `progress` in its `early` state shows "0 waves ridden out" during prep, failure before the quit has started; hide wave and check-in tiles until Quit Day. And the `today` note admits that "'Of 7 prep done' and the prep streak read the old program's completions, which Program v2 sessions don't write, so the app shows 0 even after Prep 1–3", a bug to fix before launch ([lab: today.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/today.js)). Mood scales also differ: `log-craving` uses five (Low/Meh/Okay/Good/Great) and `checkin-form` four drawn faces (Rough/Okay/Good/Proud); use the four faces everywhere.

## The program and the paywall: right spine, wrong timing

The curriculum is a compact, defensible CBT/ACT relapse-prevention course. Prep 1–7 cover motivation and commitment, cue-response mapping, arousal regulation, urge-surfing rehearsal, environment and support; Day Xero sets expectations; Week 1 handles withdrawal basics, defusion ("The noisy passengers"), implementation intentions, behavioural activation, refusal skills and AVE prevention ("Just one"); Weeks 2–4 add substitution, sleep, cue reactivity, consolidation and identity, roughly **105 minutes across 24 sessions** ([/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Resources/program.json](/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Resources/program.json)). The choices match guidance: NICE tells clinicians to advise "stopping smoking in one go", USPSTF's Grade A recommendation combines behavioural support with pharmacotherapy, with the most effective combined interventions involving four or more sessions and 90–300 minutes of contact, and WHO's 2024 guideline positions apps as add-ons to medication and support ([NICE NG209](https://www.nice.org.uk/guidance/NG209), [USPSTF](https://uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/tobacco-use-in-adults-and-pregnant-women-counseling-and-interventions), [WHO 2024](https://www.who.int/news/item/02-07-2024-who-releases-first-ever-clinical-treatment-guideline-for-tobacco-cessation-in-adults)). The NRT statistic on `onb-nrt` and `nrt-setup` is accurate: Cochrane's 133-study review gives RR 1.55 and the plain-language "50% to 60%" ([Cochrane CD000146](https://www.cochrane.org/CD000146/TOBACCO_can-nicotine-replacement-therapy-help-me-quit-smoking)). The first-use-after-waking question is the strongest single Fagerström item and the app acts on it ("We plan your mornings first", Prep 2) ([PMC7976930](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7976930)). The inactivity rule replays the last finished day instead of resetting to Day 1 ([/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/Inactivity.swift](/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/Inactivity.swift)).

Where the program is weakest is exactly where the evidence is loudest: medication and adherence. Standalone apps have little demonstrated effect; the 3,143-smoker ASSET trial of Smoke Free found no detectable increase in quitting from offering the app, with only 25.3% of the offered arm downloading it, and a 2023 meta-analysis found app interventions alone did not raise abstinence but did when combined with pharmacotherapy ([JMIR 2024 ASSET](https://jmir.org/2024/1/e50963), [PMC10160935](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC10160935/)). So how hard DayXero pushes medication matters more than any lesson. Yet `nrt-setup` says "Using two together? Only if a pharmacist or doctor suggested it.", which cuts against high-certainty evidence that combination NRT (patch plus a fast-acting form) beats single-form, **RR 1.25** across 14 studies ([Cochrane CD013308](https://www.cochrane.org/evidence/CD013308_which-combinations-stop-smoking-medications-and-e-cigarettes-help-people-quit)); every NRT plan starts on Quit Day although pre-quit patch use ("preloading") also shows RR 1.25 at moderate certainty ([Cochrane 2023](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37335995/)); and varenicline, cytisine and bupropion, first-line in WHO and NICE guidance, are not mentioned anywhere in the app. None of this requires dosing advice; it requires reframing combination NRT as the best-evidenced regimen to confirm with a pharmacist, and adding "ask about prescription options" to the "I'd like to try it" path and to the tough-day card on `log-slip-saved`. Keep the label-only posture and the "DayXero doesn't give dosing advice" line, which is the right mitigation for Apple's 1.4.2 scrutiny of dosage calculators, but source the presets to regulator or manufacturer labels (DailyMed, EMC, TGA) rather than the retail pharmacy pages currently in `HealthSources.swift` ([Apple App Store Review Guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/), [/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Model/HealthSources.swift](/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Model/HealthSources.swift)).

A hidden mechanic weakens the commitment the hold gesture builds: `ProgramUnlock.dayXeroDate` opens Day Xero on the later of the chosen Quit Day and the day after the last prep session was finished, so a user who misses prep sessions has their quit date pushed back automatically, and no screen says so ([/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/ProgramUnlock.swift](/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Logic/ProgramUnlock.swift)). Make Quit Day fire on the chosen date and mark unfinished prep as optional catch-up. The SOS screen also under-delivers on the program's own promises: W2S1 tells the user "Your break menu is saved. It'll show up on Craving SOS when boredom hits." and W4S1 "It'll show up on Craving SOS as an option.", but `sos` offers only breathing and the reason; the one direct comparison found suggests distraction reduces acute craving distress faster than urge surfing, so a second row of saved actions (break menu, lifts, movement, "use your gum now" for NRT users) under the breathing is both evidence-aligned and already written ([UGA thesis](https://getd.libs.uga.edu/pdfs/murphy_cara_m_201208_ms.pdf)). Mission follow-up answers (gap lengths, sighs-first, peak ratings from `session-mission`) are stored per session but nothing in 275 states shows them being used later; "your craving peaks: 8 → 6 → 4 this week" would turn stored data into the self-efficacy evidence the AVE literature says protects against relapse. The slip-debrief rehearsal (W4S2) arrives on Day 28, after the highest-risk period; a shortened version belongs in Prep 6 or 7. And the program ends at Day 31 with only SOS and milestones after, while milestones run to 365 and the Quit Genius trial's effect was present at 26 weeks but not at 52 ([PubMed 35470860](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/35470860/)); add light sessions at Days 45, 60 and 90 and a weekly Glim check-in so an annual subscriber sees value after the finale.

"Nothing resets" is right for a slip and wrong for a relapse, and the counter needs to be honest about the difference. Counters are date arithmetic from the baseline, "Cigarettes not smoked = floor(12 × e), Kept = $7 × e", and ignore logs entirely, so after three cigarettes on Day 3 the screen still says "3 days clear" and "26 not smoked"; a person who returns to daily use for weeks sees a rising clear count unless they find the Settings-only alert on `settings-quit-day` ([/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md) §7, [lab: me.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/me.js)). I Am Sober's users split on exactly this: a one-tap reset is praised for not scolding, and resented because "it also makes you feel bad when you relapse" ([I Am Sober listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/i-am-sober-sobriety-counter/id672904239)). Keep the lock but rename the metric "days since Quit Day" everywhere (`today`, `progress`, `milestone`, `milestone-share`, `finale`), subtract logged slips from "cigarettes not smoked", define a relapse threshold (slips on three consecutive days or five in seven) and surface the existing "Start counting from…" alert as a Jelli offer inside `log-slip-saved`'s `tough` state: "Looks like nicotine's back in your days. Want to pick a fresh Quit Day? Your lessons, logs and plans stay." The "streak" vocabulary should go too: the Today "Streak card" ("Smoke-free · 3 days", "Prep streak · 3 / 7") and the water "5-DAY STREAK" pill use the word the brand has otherwise banned, and the water streak is the only one in the app that can actually be broken ([lab: today.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/today.js), [/home/user/DAYXERO/docs/DAYXERO_NOTIFICATIONS_WATER_HANDOFF.md](/home/user/DAYXERO/docs/DAYXERO_NOTIFICATIONS_WATER_HANDOFF.md)).

The vape and pouch content is a lightly re-skinned smoking program for the stated launch audience. The vape baseline on `onb-baseline-vape` rests on self-reported puffs, which a 2026 ITC paper says have limited reliability and validity, and the cigarette-equivalent load may understate salt-based disposables, which drive about 25% more puffing in a lab RCT ([Gomes et al., Tobacco Control 2026 preprint](https://davidhammond.ca/wp-content/uploads/2025/04/2026-ITC-Youth-Vaping-consumption-Tob-Cont-Gomes-et-al.pdf), [PMC11316233](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/11316233/)). Pods per week, time to first vape and a self-perceived addiction item are better-supported anchors; keep puffs optional. The evidence that is actually positive for 18–24 vapers points to a higher-cadence text layer (This Is Quitting: 24.1% vs 18.6% at 7 months) and to varenicline with counselling (51% vs 14% placebo at 12 weeks in 16–25-year-olds), neither of which the app reflects ([PMC8129897](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8129897), [Mass General Brigham](https://www.massgeneralbrigham.org/en/about/newsroom/press-releases/smoking-cessation-drug-helps-young-adults-quit-vaping)). The `nrt-taper` pouch schedule (halve weekly to about 1.5 mg) is an invented heuristic with no evidence either way; present it as a preference and drop the implied endpoint, and never offer "Move Quit Day to Mon 23 Nov" to make a taper fit. The dual-user variant is undefined beyond "Smoking program, with notes for vape" on `program`, and gum copy for pouch users still says "first cigarette within 30 minutes of waking".

Safety and claims need a short pass. Nine health-timeline items in `changing` have no source (taste, breath, lips, skin, hair, skin tone, sleep, lips/gums, hydrated skin) against the handoff rule that "Every item carries a source citation the user can open" ([/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Resources/health-timeline.json](/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXeroCore/Resources/health-timeline.json)); move them to a labelled "Commonly reported, not measured" section. "Each one you ride makes the next a little quieter" (`sos-passed`) and "Your brain is learning that nothing is missing" (`checkin-form`) are motivational neuroscience without a source. Pregnancy appears only in NRT safety text while the app actively nudges NRT ("I'd like to try it", the Today invitation); USPSTF recommends behavioural counselling only for pregnant persons, so one onboarding question should switch the NRT path to "talk to your midwife or doctor first" ([USPSTF](https://uspreventiveservicestaskforce.org/uspstf/recommendation/tobacco-use-in-adults-and-pregnant-women-counseling-and-interventions)). The age gate is a single tap on `onb-welcome`, `onb-under-age` is reachable only through a legacy flag and its three service rows are placeholders that link nowhere; add one explicit question and wire the resources. Low mood has helpline copy but no trigger: "Rough" or "Really struggling" on three or more days should surface the existing block. Keep marketing and in-app copy at "education and support to help you stop using nicotine" and away from "treat", "clinically proven" or individual risk predictions; the FDA's January 2026 general-wellness guidance turns on intended use and marketing claims ([AGG analysis](https://www.agg.com/news-insights/publications/dont-fear-the-regulator-fda-clarifies-when-software-and-wellness-products-are-not-medical-devices/)).

The paywall copy is excellent and the paywall timing is wrong. Two facts first. The lab and the Swift code disagree about what the first-run `paywall` sells: the lab draws a Yearly card with "3 DAYS FREE · SAVE 66%" and "Start my 3-day free trial" on 30 Oct, while `PaywallModel.load()` strips every plan with a trial before Quit Day (`plans = holdTrial ? loaded.filter { $0.trialDays == nil } : loaded`), so the shipped end-of-onboarding paywall offers only Monthly $9.99 "Billed today" and Lifetime $79.99 with a note that the trial "opens on your Quit Day", no anchor plan and no trial, at the highest-intent moment after a 13-step onboarding ([/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXero/Paywall/PaywallModel.swift](/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXero/Paywall/PaywallModel.swift) lines 104–108, [lab: paywall.js](file:///tmp/claude-0/-home-user-DAYXERO/73730766-32ca-5035-85de-413102bcd243/scratchpad/screenlab/screens/paywall.js)). Do not ship that path: honour decision 15 and drop the post-onboarding paywall (it also breaks the promise made two screens earlier on `onb-reveal`), or sell the trial there and remove the hold-back; and remove the 3-second hidden Close in the `arriving` state, which reads as a dark pattern beside otherwise honest copy. Second, **no Privacy Policy URL exists anywhere**: `AppLinks.privacy` is nil, so `onb-welcome` links only Terms and `settings-privacy` shows no policy link, while Apple's subscription page requires "links to your Terms of Use and Privacy Policy" and guideline 5.1.1(i) requires one "within the app in an easily accessible manner" ([/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXero/Settings/SettingsView.swift](/home/user/DAYXERO/DayXero/Settings/SettingsView.swift) lines 644–646, [Apple subscriptions](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/subscriptions/), [Apple guidelines](https://developer.apple.com/app-store/review/guidelines/)). This is the cheapest fix in the whole critique and a rejection risk now.

Then the timing. A 3-day trial that starts on Quit Day converts to a $39.99 charge on Mon 9 Nov, day 4 of withdrawal, with the reminder on day 2, at the moment the app's own `milestone` copy calls the hardest ("The first week is the hardest for most people"). RevenueCat's vendor data puts trials of four days or fewer at a **26.8% median trial-to-paid against a Health & Fitness median of 39.9%**, 35% of annual cancellations happen in the first month, and annual subscribers who cancel almost never return (about 5% reactivation) ([Athletech News on RevenueCat 2025](https://athletechnews.com/fitness-apps-monetizable-winner-take-all-or-most/), [RevenueCat 2026 via lavx](https://news.lavx.hu/article/annual-app-subscribers-almost-never-return-after-canceling-new-revenuecat-report-shows)). A 7-day trial ending after "One week clear" (day 7–8) aligns the first charge with the first real win and is the most common trial length in Lazyweb's 1,886-paywall sample ([Lazyweb trial length](https://www.lazyweb.com/research/trial-length-3-vs-7-vs-14-day-which-to-pick)). Pricing itself is fine and already anchored by the website: $9.99/$39.99 sits at the low end against Kwit ($127.99/yr on a 2024 US snapshot), Headspace ($69.99) and Finch ($69.99), in line with I Am Sober ($39.99–$49.99) ([Kwit listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id525441365), [Headspace pricing](https://carepaths.com/headspace-pricing/), [ChoosingTherapy I Am Sober](https://www.choosingtherapy.com/i-am-sober-app-review/)). Lifetime at 2× annual is cheaper than every competitor lifetime found except I Am Sober's and cannibalises the subscribers most likely to renew; $99.99–$119.99 is the common 2.5–3× ratio, and the website's "Family Sharing Capable" promise is not in the app and is irreversible once enabled. Three copy mismatches to fix with it: `upgrade` says "You finished Day 1." though the free part now ends after Day Xero; `onb-reveal` lists "Day 1" as free where code says Day Xero; and `paywall`/`paywall-plans` list Craving SOS under "What's included" as if paid, where `offer`'s "Always free: your counter and Craving SOS." is the pattern to copy onto every surface. Decide the evening check-in's gating too: `onb-reveal` lists it as paid, the code does not gate it, and `onb-trial` promises it to everyone; a free check-in is the better retention hook because it feeds the slip flow and the craving predictor.

## Originality: the moat is structural, the risk is the name

Mapped against competitor listings, roughly 31 of the 69 screens are table stakes (counter, money, health timeline, cravings log, missions, badges, SOS breathing, settings, paywall), about 24 are rare (pledge plus evening loop, NRT scheduling, pouch taper, if-then plans, trigger heat map, slip-as-data, share cards, quiet hours) and about 14 have no match in any competitor description: the 30-beat interactive session kit with mission follow-ups, the plan-first three-step slip flow with a rehearsal state, the count-up SOS, Evening as its own realm with its own character, the five-tool Calm set with a helpline line, the Quit Day offer tied to the program calendar, the 4 am day boundary and the 30-day finale ([SCREEN_INVENTORY.json](SCREEN_INVENTORY.json), [QuitNic](https://quitnic-app.com/en), [Smoke Free listing](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/-/id577767592), [QuitNow PRO](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id483994942)). "Not found" means not advertised, not proven absent, since none of these apps was used hands-on. The session kit is the deepest moat: 30 beat types imply content-authoring effort that the 2026 "AI coach" wave (SmokeShift, QuitGPT, QuitMode, Quitto, QuitPath AI) shortcuts, and it should be named in the listing ("3-minute sessions you do, not read") ([Product Hunt SmokeShift](https://www.producthunt.com/products/smokeshift-the-ai-way-to-quit-smoking/launches), [QuitMode](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/quitmode/id6758901959)). The genuinely scarce asset is the combination of program structure, billing timing and the no-reset rule; individually each is a known idea, together they form the "fair" product the category's one-star reviews are begging for, and it is invisible until the App Store screenshots and the paywall headline say it out loud. "SOS button + breathing + waves + widget" is commoditised across the indie vaping wave; DayXero can own only the framing (Jelli, the count-up, "we never count down a craving"). The deliberate absence of an AI coach must be stated as a virtue ("a program with three friends, not a chatbot") or it will read as a gap next to a dozen "AI" titles.

Visually, nobody will mistake DayXero for Smoke Free or Kwit; it will be read through Headspace first, Finch second. The design doc studies Headspace by name and two of its rules, one saturated ground per screen and simple shapes with no age or gender, are copied literally ([/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md](/home/user/DAYXERO/design/handoff/DAYXERO_DESIGN_SYSTEM.md) §4.2); Headspace's illustration team works "under strict rules that maintain consistency across thousands of assets" and its characters are "more recognizable than its logo" ([Blake Crosley guide](https://blakecrosley.com/guides/design/headspace)). What already separates DayXero is that its characters have jobs and hand-overs where Headspace's blobs are anonymous, its progress is a picture (the clearing sky) rather than a ring, the seriousness tiers pull the characters off screen at slips, NRT and paywall, and the marked word is zine-like rather than clean vector. What still reads as borrowed is flat two-tone shapes with soft glow, and the one way to be unmistakable at thumbnail size is to make the paper-cut texture (layered edges, lip shadows, grain) visible on device rather than a claim in the spec. Mascot fatigue applies to guilt mascots (Duolingo's owl, publicly criticised for "How do you say quitter in Spanish?") and dependent-pet loops (Finch critics warn against making users "feel they have harmed a dependent character") ([Design Buddy](https://designbuddy.substack.com/p/monthly-issue-may-2024), [svgapp Finch analysis](https://svgapp.ai/app-mascots/finch-birb/)); DayXero avoids both by rule, and should write that rule into the voice guide: Xero, Jelli and Glim never ask for anything, never miss you, never look sad after a slip. Three named characters is a heavy lift for a solo founder (three animation sets, three voices, four growth stages each), so the doc's own character-light Option B should stay alive as an A/B arm with a "fewer characters" toggle at launch; the design doc's stated risk is that expressive characters push the app younger than its 25–45 heavy-user audience.

The name is the one originality finding that is also a legal flag. "Xero" is a defended software trademark: Xero Limited reports **4.9 million subscribers and NZ$2.75B FY26 revenue**, holds live US registrations in software classes 9 and 42 with a December 2025 application pending, filed a TTAB opposition in 2021 citing likelihood of confusion, and won a 2023 WIPO domain decision over a "Xero Academy" site ([Rask Media](https://www.raskmedia.com.au/2026/05/14/xero-asxxro-share-price-on-watch-after-31-growth-in-fy26-result/), [trea.com USPTO record](https://trea.com/information/79375658---xero/trademark/f2121e0c-b551-4b17-a45b-e1faa2300d06), [TTAB 91271688](https://ttabvue.uspto.gov/ttabvue/ttabvue-91271688-OPP-1.pdf), [WIPO D2023-3889](https://wipo.int/amc/en/domains/decisions/pdf/2023/d2023-3889.pdf)). This is a non-legal read: "DayXero" as a compound health app is distinguishable in meaning and goods, but the bare word "Xero" as the mascot and logo, and "Day Xero" as a lesson title, put the shared invented spelling in headline positions, and the exposure is highest in Australia and New Zealand, which are on the launch list and where Xero has 2.7M subscribers. Renaming only the sun character before submission removes most of the headline exposure cheaply; the "Day Zero" framing itself is unowned in the category (only "Zero Smoke"-style titles exist) and the X is a usable sky mark. Get a trademark search either way, and move the marketing domain off `.in` for a US/UK/AU/CA launch.

## Conclusion

The critique resolves to one idea: DayXero's problems are not where a design review usually finds them. The brand, the voice on hard moments, the fairness rules and the program spine are better than the category and better than most of the wellness tier; what is broken is consistency between what one screen promises and what the next screen, or the Swift code, actually does. A prototype that says "use as normal" and "your brain steps down", "two nudges, that's it" and four defaults, "Day 3" and "Day 2", "we'll ask you on Quit Day" and a paywall after onboarding, cannot be trusted by the 45-year-old heavy smoker the seriousness tiers were designed for, no matter how good `finale` looks. Those contradictions are also the cheapest things in this report to fix, which is why they rank above every visual recommendation.

The second implication is that the visual work should proceed by subtraction before addition. Twelve grounds to six, nine Today modules to five, three button systems to one, two illustration languages to one, the mark rationed to moments, the water tracker to a row. Each of these makes the existing coloured-screen system the whole app rather than a quarter of it, and only once that is done does investment in Xero's growth, the milestone reveals and motion tokens pay off, because a character that visibly changes is only persuasive inside a system the user already trusts. The one decision that cannot wait for either pass is the mascot's name, which is a trademark exposure the founder can remove in a day now and may not be able to remove after launch.
