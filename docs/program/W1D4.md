## Chapter 1 · Day four (0:00)

[warmly] Hello again. Day four.

The sharpest part is usually behind you by now, or close to it. And sometimes what comes next isn't a craving at all. It's a flat feeling. Grey. A bit bored. As if someone turned the color down on the day.

If that's you today, I want you to know two things. It's common. And it passes.

## Chapter 2 · Why it feels flat (0:27)

Here's what's going on. [pause]

For a long time, nicotine gave you a small lift, many times a day. You saw in the prep week what that lift mostly was. The gap closing. Relief from a restlessness the last one left behind. But it was still a lift, and your day was built around dozens of them.

Now they've stopped, and for a little while, the ordinary good things can feel quieter than they are. A cup of coffee. A song. Finishing a job.

There's a thought that likes to show up here. It says, "See? You were happier before." [pause] You know that passenger now. It's describing the dip, not your life. Studies of people who stopped smoking found that, once withdrawal settled, they were on average less anxious and less stressed than people who kept smoking.

So this isn't a sign that something's missing for good. It's the stretch in between.

## Chapter 3 · One lift, now (1:36)

[calmly] You don't fix a flat day with one big thing. You nudge it, with small things, often. Let's do one right now.

Wherever you are, look for the nearest daylight. A window, a doorway. If you can, go and stand in it. If you can't, just turn toward it. [long pause]

Let your shoulders drop. [pause] And take two slow breaths, the way you practiced in the prep week. In through your nose, a little extra sip at the top, and a long, slow breath out. [long pause]

Once more. [long pause]

Now notice your body. Not whether you feel good. Just whether anything shifted, even slightly. [long pause]

A long, slow breath out helps many people settle. And that took less than a minute.

## Chapter 4 · Pick them while you're okay (2:44)

That's a lift. Daylight, a breath, music, cold water on your face, a short walk, a call with someone who makes you laugh. None of them is dramatic. They add up.

The trick is to choose them now, while you're listening to me and feeling reasonably steady. On a flat day, you won't feel like choosing. You'll just need the list.

In a moment, you'll pick three.

And one more thing, because it matters. Feeling low for a while after stopping is common. But if low mood lasts more than about two weeks, or gets in the way of daily life, please talk to your doctor. And if you ever feel you can't cope or keep yourself safe, get help straight away. There are numbers for where you live in the app.

[warmly] Four days. The color comes back. I'll see you tomorrow.

## Step 3 · Myth or fact · Mood (3 min)

### Statement 1
"If I feel flat without it, I must have been happier using." · Answer: **Myth** · Verdict: It's the dip, not your life. · Explanation: A lot of the relief nicotine seemed to bring was it settling the restlessness left by the last dose. Without those small reliefs, things can feel quiet for a while. In studies of people who stopped smoking, mood ended up better on average, not worse. · Source: Taylor, BMJ 2014

### Statement 2
"Feeling low for a while after stopping is common." · Answer: **Fact** · Verdict: Common, and usually temporary. · Explanation: Low mood is a recognized part of withdrawal and usually eases within a few weeks. If it lasts more than about two weeks, or gets in the way of daily life, talk to your doctor. You don't have to wait it out alone. · Source: smokefree.gov

### Statement 3
"I need something big to turn a flat day around." · Answer: **Myth** · Verdict: Small things, often. · Explanation: Flat days rarely lift in one go. A few minutes of daylight, a short walk, music or a call each move things a little, and they stack. That's why you chose yours in advance. · Source: Haasova, Addiction 2013 (movement)
