## Chapter 1 · One week (0:00)

[warmly] Hi. [pause] One week.

Seven days ago, you held your thumb down and started Day Xero. Since then there's been a first morning. A day when the thoughts got loud. A day when getting through was enough. A flat one, maybe. And somewhere in there, stretches where you forgot about it completely.

You did every one of those days. I just kept you company.

## Chapter 2 · The faucet (0:29)

On your very first day with me, before you'd changed anything, I described a faucet dripping in another room. How after a while you stop hearing it, but part of you stays a little tense. And how a person in a house with no dripping faucet has that quiet all day long, and never thinks of it as a treat.

For years, the gap was that drip. Every hour or two.

A week ago, you turned it off. [pause]

It didn't go silent straight away. The first few days, if anything, you heard it more than ever. That was the sound of it stopping.

But it has been getting quieter. And today I'd like you to listen for that.

## Chapter 3 · Listen for it (1:22)

[softly] Take a breath, in a way that feels comfortable for you. [long pause]

Let your shoulders drop. Let your hands rest. [long pause]

Now go looking for the gap. That restless, slightly empty, something's-missing feeling you rated on your first prep day. Check the places you used to find it. Your chest. Your throat. Your hands. [long pause] [long pause]

It may still be there. If it is, notice its size. Is it the same as it was on day one? [long pause]

And if you can't find it right now, stay with that. [long pause] That's the quiet. Nothing is pulling at you. You aren't holding anything back. There's simply nothing missing. [long pause]

This is what people who don't use nicotine feel like most of the time. It isn't exciting. It's just free. [long pause]

It won't be like this every hour yet. Certain places, smells and moments can still set off a craving, even weeks after you stop. Each time you get through one, that link tends to get weaker. The first time in each new situation is often the hardest.

But you've now felt the quiet for yourself. You know it's real, and you know where it is.

## Chapter 4 · What comes next (3:17)

In a moment, your week will be on your screen. Your days, the cravings you rode out, and what you've kept. Then the note you wrote to yourself the night before Day Xero. Read it as the person it was written for.

Then I'd like you to do two things. Name the one thing that helped most this week, because that's the one to keep reaching for. And choose something small to mark the week. You've kept money this week that used to go somewhere else. Spend a little of it on yourself, on purpose.

Next week changes shape. The hard part won't be the cravings so much as the empty minutes. The breaks, the pauses, the spaces in your day where nicotine used to sit. We'll fill those with things that are actually yours. After tomorrow, I'll be here every few days, not every day. You don't need me every day anymore.

[warmly] One week. Well done. I'll see you tomorrow.

## Step 3 · Myth or fact · One week in (3 min)

### Statement 1
"After one week, the cravings are over." · Answer: **Myth** · Verdict: Fewer, not finished. · Explanation: The strongest withdrawal is usually behind you, but places, smells and moments can still set off a craving for weeks. Each time you get through one, that link tends to get weaker. The first time in each new situation is often the hardest. · Source: Hughes, Nicotine Tob Res 2007

### Statement 2
"The restless, empty feeling between uses was caused by nicotine itself." · Answer: **Fact** · Verdict: That was the gap. · Explanation: Nicotine leaves the body quickly, and the drop brings a restless, on-edge feeling that the next dose relieves. People who don't use nicotine don't have it. A week in, you've started to find out what its absence feels like. · Source: Benowitz, NEJM 2010

### Statement 3
"A week isn't long enough to count for anything." · Answer: **Myth** · Verdict: It's the steepest week. · Explanation: Withdrawal symptoms are usually at their strongest in the first week and ease from here. You've just done the part most people find hardest, and you did it one craving at a time. · Source: Hughes, Nicotine Tob Res 2007
