# W2S1 · The empty minutes

**Label:** Day 8 · **Theme:** The empty minutes · **Big idea:** Where the gap used to be, there is time. The break was always yours; only the nicotine has gone.

## Step 1 · Listen · The five minutes were yours (4 min)

**Commit button:** "The break is mine"

### Script (Eleven v4)

Voice direction: relaxed and unhurried, a little lighter than Week 1; leave real room in chapter 3.

## Chapter 1 · Week two (0:00)

[warmly] Welcome back. Day eight, and the start of your second week.

Something may be shifting by now. The cravings tend to come a little less often. And in their place, you might be noticing something quieter and stranger. Empty minutes. [pause] The end of a meal. Waiting for someone. The pause between one job and the next. Moments that used to have a shape, and now just sit there.

## Chapter 2 · What filled them (0:31)

The night before Day Xero, I asked you a question. Is it the nicotine you'd miss, or the five minutes?

This week you find out. Because for years, nicotine marked your breaks for you. {s: It gave you a reason to step outside, and a clear end when the cigarette was finished. | v: It was always in reach, so every small pause got filled before you noticed it was a pause. | p: It sat quietly in your lip, so a break never looked like a break at all.}

The autopilot you mapped in the prep week is still looking for its cue in those moments. And an empty minute with nothing in it is exactly where a craving finds room.

But here's the thing. The five minutes were always yours. Stepping away, breathing, letting your mind wander. You were allowed those all along. Nicotine just came along for the ride, and took the credit.

## Chapter 3 · Take one now (1:23)

[calmly] So let's take a break right now. A real one, with nothing in it.

## Chapter 4 · Your menu (2:37)

An empty minute is easier when you've already decided what goes in it. Not a rule. A short menu. Step outside. Make a proper cup of something. Stretch. Text someone. Look out of a window and do nothing at all.

In a moment, you'll pick three. They'll be waiting for you in Craving SOS, so when boredom turns up, the next step is already there.

[warmly] Keep the break. It was always the good part. I'll see you in a couple of days.

# W2S4 · The flat days

**Label:** Day 12 · **Theme:** The empty minutes · **Big idea:** Flat, foggy days are common around now. They aren't a sign you were better off before. They pass, and small steady habits carry you through.

## Step 1 · Listen · Fog and low spark (4 min)

### Script (Eleven v4)

Voice direction: steady and matter-of-fact, kind without being soft; plain delivery on the safety line.

## Chapter 1 · Day twelve (0:00)

[warmly] Hi. Day twelve.

On day four we talked about a flat feeling, and you chose some small lifts. I'm coming back to it today, because for many people it shows up again around now, and it can look a little different. Less like sadness. More like fog. Slow thinking. Forgetting what you came into the room for. Things you usually enjoy feeling a bit grey.

If that's where you are, there's nothing wrong with you. Many people notice this in the first weeks.

## Chapter 2 · The thought that comes with it (0:36)

A foggy afternoon is when a familiar passenger leans forward. It says, "One would fix this."

And in a narrow sense, it's telling the truth. One would lift you, for a few minutes. Then the gap would be open again, and you'd be back at the start of the stretch you're nearly through. You've played that tape. You know where it goes.

The fog isn't your life without nicotine. It's the in-between. Many people notice their head clearing bit by bit over the weeks that follow. Riding out the flat days is how they end.

## Chapter 3 · One thing (1:19)

[calmly] On a foggy day, the kindest thing is to make the day smaller. Let's practice that.

Take a breath, in a way that feels comfortable for you. [long pause]

Notice where the heaviness sits. Behind your eyes, maybe. In your shoulders. A slowness all over. [long pause] Let it be there. You're not going to argue with it. [long pause]

Now think of the very next thing in your day. Not the whole list. One thing, small enough to finish. [long pause]

Picture yourself doing just that. Nothing after it yet. [long pause]

## Chapter 4 · Small and steady (2:28)

The lifts you chose on day four still work. So do the plain things. Daylight early in the day. A short walk. Proper meals and water. A regular bedtime.

In a moment, you'll pick two habits for this week. Choose the easy ones.

And as always, this matters. Feeling low for a while after stopping is common. But if low mood lasts more than about two weeks, or gets in the way of daily life, please talk to your doctor. And if you ever feel you can't cope or keep yourself safe, get help straight away. There are numbers for where you live in the app.

[warmly] The fog lifts. Until it does, one thing at a time. I'll see you in a couple of days.

# W3S2 · Out of nowhere

**Label:** Day 19 · **Theme:** Echoes · **Big idea:** Weeks in, an old cue can set off a strong craving. It's an echo, not the gap coming back. Same skill, and the link weakens each time.

## Step 1 · Listen · An old cue (4 min)

**Commit button:** "Old cue, same skill"

### Script (Eleven v4)

Voice direction: calm and reassuring, slightly matter-of-fact, as if describing weather; steady through the practice.

## Chapter 1 · Day nineteen (0:00)

[warmly] Hello again. Day nineteen.

By now you may go hours without a thought of it. Whole afternoons. And then, out of nowhere, one hits you. Strong, sudden, as if no time had passed. {s: Someone lights up ahead of you on the street, and the smell reaches you. | v: A sweet cloud drifts past you outside a bar. | p: A tin sits open on a friend's table.}

And a worried thought follows it. "I thought I was past this."

## Chapter 2 · What it is (0:26)

You are past the part you're thinking of. This is something else.

Certain places, smells and moments can still set off a craving, even weeks after you stop. Each time you get through one, that link tends to get weaker. The first time in each new situation is often the hardest.

Think back to the autopilot. For years, certain cues were paired with nicotine, over and over. You've already worn down the everyday ones. Your morning. Your coffee. Your drive. But some cues only come round now and then. A particular friend. A certain bar. The first warm evening of the year. Those haven't had their turn yet.

So when one fires, it isn't the gap coming back. It's an echo. An old cue, meeting you for the first time since you stopped.

## Chapter 3 · Meet one (1:25)

[calmly] Let's rehearse meeting one.

Take a breath, in a way that feels comfortable for you. [long pause]

Picture a place or a moment you haven't been in since you stopped. Somewhere you always used. [long pause]

Imagine the craving arriving. Fast, and stronger than you expected. [long pause]

Name it. "Old cue." [long pause]

Find it in your body, the way you know how. And watch it like a wave. It rises. [long pause] It peaks. [long pause] And it falls. [long pause]

A craving rises, peaks and passes, usually within minutes. This one is no different. It only feels bigger because it surprised you.

## Chapter 4 · Same skill (2:33)

You don't need a new tool for this. You need the one you've used every day for nearly three weeks.

In a moment, you'll pick the cues that could catch you off guard, so they're less of a surprise when they come.

[warmly] Old cue. Same skill. And smaller next time. I'll see you in a couple of days.
