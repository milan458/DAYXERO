## Chapter 1 · Day two (0:00)

[warmly] Welcome back. Day two.

If yesterday was about your body, today is often about your head. Around now, the thoughts tend to get busy. And they're clever. They don't say "use nicotine." They say things that sound like you. [pause] "You've proved your point." "Today's been hard enough." "Just one, and then back to it."

Two nights ago, on the evening of Day Xero, I said that if a thought like that showed up, you'd know what it was. A thought. Not an order. Today we make that a skill.

## Chapter 2 · The passengers (0:40)

Here's a way to picture it. [pause]

Imagine you're driving a bus. You know where you're going. You chose the route yourself, back when you wrote down your reasons.

And the bus has passengers. Some sit quietly. Some are loud. A few of them lean forward and tell you, very confidently, that you're going the wrong way, and that there's a much better turn just here.

You could stop the bus and argue with them. People try. The trouble is, while you're arguing, you're not driving, and they have endless energy for it. Push a thought away and it tends to come straight back, louder.

Or you could let them talk. [pause] They're allowed to be on the bus. They're allowed to be noisy. They just don't get the wheel.

That's all today is. They can shout. They can't steer.

## Chapter 3 · Body first (1:43)

[calmly] Let's practice. And we'll start with the body, because that's where a craving lives before it turns into words.

Take a breath, in a way that feels comfortable for you. [long pause]

Bring to mind the last time you wanted one. This morning, maybe. Or right now. [long pause]

Find where it sits. A tightness in your chest. A pull in your throat. Restless hands. [long pause]

See if you can let that feeling be there, exactly as it is, without doing anything about it. You're not trying to make it go away. You're making room for it. [long pause]

Notice that you can feel it, and still be sitting here. Still breathing. [long pause]

Now listen for the passenger. What's the thought that comes with the feeling? [pause] Whatever it says, try putting a few words in front of it. "I'm having the thought that... one wouldn't hurt." [long pause]

You don't have to argue. You don't have to believe it or disbelieve it. You heard it. And your hands are still on the wheel. [long pause]

## Chapter 4 · Keep driving (3:25)

A craving rises, peaks and passes, usually within minutes. The thought that rides along with it passes too. If some of the urge is still here, that's okay. It will keep fading.

In a moment, you'll write down what your loudest passengers say, and one calm line to say back. Not a clever line. Something like, "I hear you. Still not stopping."

And today, when one of them pipes up, just name it. "There's that passenger again." Then carry on with whatever you were doing.

[warmly] You're two days in, and you're the one driving. I'll see you tomorrow.

## Step 3 · Myth or fact · The voice (3 min)

### Statement 1
"If I'm still thinking about it this much, I'm doing it wrong." · Answer: **Myth** · Verdict: Thoughts aren't the test. · Explanation: Thinking about nicotine a lot in the first days is expected. It was part of every hour of your day. What counts isn't whether the thought shows up. It's that you let it talk and kept going. · Source: Hughes, Nicotine Tob Res 2007

### Statement 2
"The best way to beat a craving thought is to argue it down." · Answer: **Myth** · Verdict: Let it talk instead. · Explanation: Arguing keeps your attention on the thought and gives it something to push against. Noticing it, naming it and carrying on tends to work better. In a large trial, an app built on this approach helped more people quit than a standard one. · Source: Bricker, JAMA Intern Med 2020

### Statement 3
"A craving rises, peaks and passes." · Answer: **Fact** · Verdict: Usually within minutes. · Explanation: It feels like it will keep climbing until you do something. It doesn't. It comes up, tops out and falls away, whether or not you act on it. Each one you ride is practice for the next. · Source: smokefree.gov
