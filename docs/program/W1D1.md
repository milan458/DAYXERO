## Chapter 1 · Day one (0:00)

[warmly] Good morning. [pause] Day one.

Yesterday you were getting through Day Xero an hour at a time. This morning you woke up with a whole day behind you. Nobody can take that day back.

However the night went, it's okay. Maybe you slept well. Maybe you were awake at three, staring at the ceiling. Maybe the first thing you thought of when you opened your eyes was {s: a cigarette | v: your vape | p: a pouch}. [pause] All of that is normal on a first morning, and none of it means anything has gone wrong.

## Chapter 2 · Why it's loud (0:40)

Let me tell you what's happening, because it helps to know.

All through the prep week, you watched the gap. That restless, slightly empty feeling that showed up between one and the next. Each time, you closed it, and it opened again.

Since yesterday, nothing has closed it. So these first days, it's loud. [pause] That isn't the quit going badly. That's what it sounds like when the gap is on its way out.

The first few days are usually the hardest. For most people, withdrawal eases over the next few weeks. Feeling restless, irritable, low, foggy or hungrier than usual is common right now. It's temporary. It's a sign your body is adjusting.

So today, I'm not going to ask much of you. Today is about the basics.

## Chapter 3 · Is it a craving? (1:40)

[calmly] Here's something worth knowing this week. Being hungry, thirsty or tired can feel a lot like a craving. The same restlessness. The same sense that something's missing.

So before you decide a feeling is a craving, check the basics first. Let's try it now.

Take one breath, in a way that feels comfortable for you. [long pause]

Now notice your body. On your first prep day, you asked where you felt the gap. Ask again. Your chest, your throat, your hands, your stomach. [long pause]

Whatever you find, you don't have to fix it. Just let it be there for a moment. [long pause]

Now three quick questions. When did I last eat? [pause] When did I last drink some water? [pause] How did I sleep? [long pause]

If one of those answers is "a while ago," start there. Eat something. Drink a glass of water. And if what's left afterwards is a craving, you know what to do with a craving. You notice it, you name it, you breathe, and you let it pass.

## Chapter 4 · Today (3:11)

So, today. Three proper meals. Water close by, because a glass of water gives your hands and your mouth something to do while a wave passes. And something easy to reach for between meals.

It's common to feel hungrier, and some people put on a little weight after stopping. That's normal. Regular meals, water and some movement can help. This isn't the moment for a strict diet.

[warmly] You've done a whole day. Today you do the basics, and let the day carry you. I'll see you tomorrow.

## Step 3 · Myth or fact · Day one (3 min)

### Statement 1
"If I'm restless and on edge today, it must be a craving." · Answer: **Myth** · Verdict: Check the basics first. · Explanation: Hunger, thirst and tiredness bring the same restless, something's-missing feeling. Eat, drink some water and see what's left. If it's still there, it's a craving, and you know how to ride one. · Source: smokefree.gov

### Statement 2
"I should start a diet today so I don't gain weight." · Answer: **Myth** · Verdict: Not this week. · Explanation: Feeling hungrier is common after stopping, and some people gain a little. Regular meals, water and some movement help. A strict diet on top of withdrawal makes both harder. There's time for that later. · Source: Aubin, BMJ 2012

### Statement 3
"The first few days are usually the hardest part." · Answer: **Fact** · Verdict: And you're in them now. · Explanation: Withdrawal is usually strongest in the first days and eases over the next few weeks for most people. A rough morning today isn't a forecast. It's the steep part, and you're already on it. · Source: Hughes, Nicotine Tob Res 2007
