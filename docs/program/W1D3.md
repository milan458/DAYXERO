## Chapter 1 · Day three (0:00)

[gently] Hi. Day three.

I'm going to be straight with you, the way I have been since the first day. For a lot of people, this is the toughest stretch. If you woke up tired, short-tempered, a bit tearful, or wondering why you ever started this, you're in very good company.

And if today feels fine, that happens too. Take it. You haven't missed a step.

Either way, I'm glad you opened this. You opened it when it would have been easier not to.

## Chapter 2 · Lower the bar (0:36)

So here's today's whole idea. [pause] Lower the bar.

Today doesn't have to be a good day. You don't have to be productive, or patient, or cheerful. You don't have to feel grateful that you've stopped. Today, the only job is to get to tonight without nicotine. Everything else is extra.

If you get less done, that's fine. If you're poor company, the people who care about you can cope with that for a few days. If dinner is something simple, good.

Feeling restless, irritable, low, foggy or hungrier than usual is common right now. It's temporary. It's a sign your body is adjusting. The gap that used to open every hour or two is running out of places to come from.

## Chapter 3 · You've felt this before (1:31)

[calmly] I'd like to remind you of something you did in the prep week.

There was a day when you imagined throwing everything away, right then, before you were ready. You noticed what showed up in your body. Some of it was close to panic. You gave it a number. You breathed with the ring. And then you gave it a number again. [pause]

It came down. Not because you fought it. Because that's what these feelings do when you stay with them.

Today's feelings are bigger, and they're real. But they're the same kind of thing. Let's do a small version now.

Take a breath, in a way that feels comfortable for you. [long pause]

Notice the hardest thing in your body right now. The tiredness, the edge, the wanting. [long pause]

Give it a number, from zero to ten. You don't have to tell anyone. [long pause]

Now just stay with it for a few breaths. No fixing. [long pause] [long pause]

And check the number again. [long pause] Whatever it is now, you stayed. That's the skill.

## Chapter 4 · Decide now (3:10)

One practical thing for today. Decide now what you'll do if it gets hard, so you don't have to decide in the moment.

Planning ahead in a simple if-then way, if this happens, then I'll do that, makes people more likely to follow through.

And if you're stuck for a "then", try moving. Even a few minutes of movement, like a brisk walk or some stairs, can help a craving pass.

In a moment, your reasons will be on your screen. The ones you wrote before any of this started. Read them slowly, especially the one you chose as your main reason. That person knew today was coming, and wanted this anyway.

[warmly] Lower the bar, and step over it. I'll see you tomorrow.

## Step 3 · Myth or fact · The toughest stretch (3 min)

### Statement 1
"Day three should feel easier than day one." · Answer: **Myth** · Verdict: Often it's the hardest. · Explanation: Withdrawal is usually strongest in the first few days, and many people find the third day the low point. A harder day today doesn't mean you're going backwards. For most people it eases over the next few weeks. · Source: Hughes, Nicotine Tob Res 2007

### Statement 2
"A few minutes of movement can help a craving pass." · Answer: **Fact** · Verdict: Five minutes counts. · Explanation: A brisk walk, some stairs or a stretch gives the restlessness somewhere to go. It doesn't have to be a workout, and you don't have to feel like it first. Studies of short bouts of activity found cravings dropped afterwards. · Source: Haasova, Addiction 2013

### Statement 3
"If I'm this irritable, quitting must not suit me." · Answer: **Myth** · Verdict: It's withdrawal, and it's temporary. · Explanation: Irritability is one of the most common withdrawal symptoms, and one of the first to ease. It's your body adjusting, not your personality without nicotine. Go easy on yourself, and tell the people around you it's a rough few days. · Source: Hughes, Nicotine Tob Res 2007
